## Summary

The approximate degree $\widetilde{\deg}(f)$ of a Boolean function is the least degree of a real polynomial that approximates $f$ to error $1/3$ in $\ell_\infty$. Aaronson and Shi (J. ACM 2004) proved the tight bounds $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{Col}) = \tilde\Omega(N^{1/3})$ and $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{ED}) = \tilde\Omega(N^{2/3})$ for the Collision and Element Distinctness functions, but their proof was non-constructive (symmetrization plus Markov-Bernstein inequalities). This paper reproves both bounds constructively by exhibiting explicit *dual polynomials*: explicit feasible solutions to the dual of the linear program whose optimum is the approximate degree. These are the concrete primal-dual certificates the project wants for the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge. The main deliverables are a closed-form dual witness $\psi$ for $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ proving $\widetilde{\deg} = \Omega(N^{1/3})$ (Theorem 13) and a dual-side reduction turning any Collision dual witness into an Element Distinctness one (Theorem 16, Corollary 17).

## Key definitions and results

### The approximate-degree LP and its dual (Section 2.2, page 3)

For a partial Boolean function $f : D \to \{-1,1\}$ with $D \subseteq \{-1,1\}^n$, a degree-$d$ multilinear polynomial $p(x) = \sum_{|S|\le d} c_S \chi_S(x)$ (where $\chi_S(x) = \prod_{i\in S} x_i$ is the parity on $S$) that minimizes the error $\varepsilon$ is the optimum of an LP. The dual LP is the load-bearing object:

$$\max \sum_{x\in D}\phi(x)f(x) - \sum_{x\notin D}|\phi(x)| \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_x |\phi(x)| = 1, \quad \sum_x \phi(x)\chi_S(x) = 0 \text{ for all } |S|\le d.$$

**Theorem 1 (dual characterization, page 3).** $\widetilde{\deg}_\varepsilon(f) > d$ if and only if there exists $\phi : \{-1,1\}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ with (1) $\sum_{x\in D} f(x)\phi(x) - \sum_{x\notin D}|\phi(x)| > \varepsilon\cdot\sum_x |\phi(x)|$ (high correlation with $f$), and (2) $\sum_x \phi(x)\chi_S(x) = 0$ for all $|S|\le d$ (this property is called *pure high degree* $d$: $\phi$ has no Fourier mass below level $d$). Such a $\phi$ is an $(\varepsilon,d)$-dual polynomial. Strong LP duality makes the method *lossless*: a tight witness always exists, unlike symmetrization which throws away information (page 1, "The Method of Dual Polynomials"). This is the exact primal-dual pairing the dossier wants made explicit.

### Collision and Element Distinctness as Boolean functions (Section 2.3, page 4)

Input $x \in \{-1,1\}^n$ is parsed as the truth table of a function $g_x : [N] \to [R]$ ($n = N\log_2 R$, $R \ge N$, $R$ a power of 2). $g_x$ is $k$-to-1 if every range element has exactly $k$ preimages; $T_k$ is the set of $k$-to-1 inputs. **Definition 2**: $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ is the *partial* function on $T_1 \cup T_2$ that is $1$ exactly on the 1-to-1 inputs $T_1$ (promise: input is 1-to-1 or 2-to-1). **Definition 3**: $\mathrm{ED}_{N,R}$ is the *total* function that is $1$ iff $g_x$ is 1-to-1. The dual-witness conditions are restated for these promise sets at the bottom of page 4.

### Valid triples and the symmetrization lemma (Sections 2.4, 2.7)

A triple $(m,a,b)$ is *valid* if $a \mid m$ and $b \mid (N-m)$; $R_{m,a,b}$ is the orbit (under $S_N \times S_R$) of the canonical input $g_{m,a,b}$ that is $a$-to-1 on $m$ inputs and $b$-to-1 on the rest (page 7). **Lemma 5 (page 8, a refinement of Kutin's Lemma 7):** for any degree-$d$ polynomial $p$, there is a *trivariate* degree-$d$ polynomial $P$ with $P(m,a,b) = \mathbb{E}_{x\in R_{m,a,b}}[p(x)]$ for every valid triple. This is the "$k$-to-1 inputs matter" miracle (Section 2.6, page 7): an $n$-variate degree-$d$ polynomial restricted to $k$-to-1 inputs collapses to a univariate degree-$d$ polynomial in $k$. It is the primal-side fact the dual construction exploits to verify pure high degree.

### Warm-up bound (Section 3, Theorem 10, page 10)

A clean explicit dual witness gives a sub-optimal bound first. **Lemma 8 (page 8):** there is an explicit $\omega : \{1,\dots,L\} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\omega(1) \ge \frac{1-\delta}{2}$, $-\omega(2) \ge \frac{1-\delta}{2}$, $\sum_k |\omega(k)| = 1$, and $\sum_k p(k)\omega(k) = 0$ for every polynomial of degree $\le \zeta\sqrt{\delta L}$. The construction (page 9) places $\hat\omega$ at the node set $T = \{1\} \cup \{ci^2 : 0\le i\le m\}$ (squares), uses a finite-difference identity (Lemma 9: $\sum_{k=0}^L (-1)^k \binom{L}{k} q(k) = 0$ for $\deg q < L$, from O'Donnell-Servedio Appendix A), and verifies the $\ell_1$ mass decays quadratically away from the spike. **Theorem 10:** taking $N = L!$ so that every $k \le L$ divides $N$, and setting $\phi(x) = \omega(k)/|T_k|$ on $T_k$, yields an explicit $(1-2\delta, d)$-dual polynomial for $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ with $d = \Omega(\sqrt{\delta L}) = \Omega(\sqrt{\delta \log N / \log\log N})$. The divisibility requirement ($k \mid N$ for $k$ in the support) is what costs the $\log N/\log\log N$ here.

### The optimal $\Omega(N^{1/3})$ bound (Section 4, Theorem 13, page 13)

This is the headline result. **Theorem 13 (page 13):** for $N$ a multiple of 4 and $R \ge N$, there is an *explicit* $(1-6\delta, d)$-dual polynomial $\psi$ for $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ with $d = \Omega(\delta^{1/3} N^{1/3})$. Equivalently $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}) = \Omega(N^{1/3})$.

The witness is built from three explicit ingredients:
- **$\omega$** (Lemma 8), the OR-style dual witness on the squares, repurposed.
- **$\eta_k$** (Lemma 11, page 11, a refinement of the authors' earlier MAJ dual witness): an explicit $\eta_k : [[N]] \to \mathbb{R}$ supported on $\{2k, 4k, \dots\} \cup \{N/2\}$ with $\eta_k(N/2) > \frac{1-\delta}{2}$, $\sum_r |\eta_k(r)| = 1$, and $\sum_r p(r)\eta_k(r) = 0$ for every $p$ of degree $\le \rho\sqrt{\delta} N/k$. This is the Majority dual witness; it is the piece tied to MAJORITY and hence to the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge.
- A combined object $\Psi(m,k) = \omega(k)\cdot\mathbb{1}_{m=N/2} - \frac{\omega(k)}{\eta_k(N/2)}\mathbb{1}_{k\ge 3}\cdot\eta_k(m)$ (page 14) whose $\eta_k$ terms are engineered to cancel the weight $\omega$ places on $k$ that do *not* divide $N$.

**Lemma 12 (page 13):** $\psi$ is assembled as $\psi = a\psi_1 + b\psi_2$ where $\psi_1, \psi_2$ are two explicit dual polynomials, each with pure high degree $\ge d = \Omega(\delta^{1/3}N^{1/3})$, each placing a constant fraction of $\ell_1$ mass on $T_1$ resp. $T_2$, and each constant on every valid $R_{m,a,b}$. Crucially neither $\psi_1$ nor $\psi_2$ is individually well-correlated with $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$, but their masses on the invalid set $R_{N/2,2,1}$ *exactly cancel* (Properties 6 and 7 of Lemma 12; the cancellation is verified algebraically on page 14, Eq. (5)). **Lemma 15 (page 14):** pure high degree is additive, so $\psi_1+\psi_2$ inherits it. The correlation bound $\ge \frac12 - 2\delta \ge (1-6\delta)\|\psi\|_1$ closes Theorem 13 (page 14).

The construction of $\psi_1$ (page 15): $\hat\psi_1(x) = \Psi(m,k)/|R_{m,k,1}|$ on $R_{m,k,1}\setminus T_1$ and $\Psi(N/2,1)/|T_1|$ on $T_1$, then normalized. The pure-high-degree verification (Eqs. (6), (7), page 16) is where Lemma 5 enters: $\sum_x \hat\psi_1(x)\chi_S(x)$ rewrites via the trivariate $Q(m,k)$ as $\sum_k \omega(k)Q(N/2,k) - \sum_k \frac{\omega(k)}{\eta_k(N/2)}(\eta_k(N/2)Q(N/2,k) + \sum_i \eta_k(2ik)Q(2ik,k))$; the first sum vanishes by Lemma 8 (Part 4), the second by Lemma 11 (Part 4). $\psi_2$ is the symmetric construction on $T_2$ / $R_{m,k,2}$.

**Discussion (Section 2.6, page 6-7):** the authors stress the structural mimicry of Kutin's symmetrization proof: $\psi = \psi_1 + \psi_2$ mirrors Kutin's first case-split on whether $P(N/2,2,1)$ is large; the $\psi'$ piece is built from their OR dual witness (Nisan-Szegedy line) and the $\psi''_{N/2,a,1}$ pieces from their MAJ dual witness (Paturi line, Bernstein inequality). So OR-vs-Markov and MAJ-vs-Bernstein from the primal proof reappear as two explicit dual gadgets.

### Element Distinctness via a dual-side reduction (Section 5)

**Theorem 16 (page 17):** any $(1-\delta, d)$-dual polynomial $\psi$ for $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ yields an explicit $(1-2\delta, d)$-dual polynomial $\varphi$ for $\mathrm{ED}_{M,R}$ whenever $M \ge 2\sqrt{N\log(2/\delta)}$ (so $M^2 \approx N$). The dual reduction is the transpose of Aaronson-Shi's primal reduction: where the primal averages a polynomial $p$ over size-$M$ subsets of blocks, the dual *averages $\psi(y)$ over a set of extensions* from each short input $x$ to a longer input $y$. Explicitly $\varphi(x) = \frac{1}{\binom{N}{M}}\sum_{y\in \mathrm{ext}(x)}\psi(y)$ (page 17), where $\mathrm{ext}(x)$ is the multiset of extensions whose restriction to some $M$-block subset equals $x$. The correlation and pure-high-degree checks (page 18) use the birthday-bound observation that a random size-$M$ restriction of a 2-to-1 input is collision-free with probability $\le \exp(-M^2/4N)$.

**Corollary 17 (page 17):** for any $\delta > 0$ there is an explicit $(1-\delta, d)$-dual polynomial for $\mathrm{ED}_{M,R}$ with $d = \Omega((\delta/\log(1/\delta))^{1/3} M^{2/3})$, i.e. $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{ED}) = \tilde\Omega(M^{2/3})$. The paper notes (page 1) this remains the best-known approximate-degree lower bound for any function in $\mathsf{AC}^0$.

### Tightness (Section 6 and Appendix A)

Section 6 (complementary slackness, pages 19-21) reverse-engineers *why* the dual is supported on near-$k$-to-1 inputs: it builds an explicit asymptotically optimal *primal* approximation $p$ for $\mathrm{Col}_{N,R}$ from the Brassard-Hoyer-Tapp collision-finding algorithm, using a Chebyshev polynomial $A_d$ on the cross-collision count $\mathrm{cross}_S$, and argues the $k$-to-1 inputs are (with high probability over the random subset $S$) the maximum-error points, so complementary slackness forces the dual to live there. Appendix A (Propositions 19, 20, pages 24-25) shows Theorem 13 and Corollary 17 are tight up to a $\log R$ factor in their dependence on both $\delta$ and $N$ for vanishing $\delta$.

## Techniques and proof ideas

A BUILDER's reusable toolkit from this paper:

1. **The dual LP as the certificate format.** Theorem 1 is the template: to prove $\widetilde{\deg}(f) > d$, exhibit $\phi$ with high correlation and pure high degree $d$ (zero Fourier mass below level $d$). This is exactly the primal-dual object the dossier names as missing in explicit form for the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step.
2. **Spike-on-the-squares dual witnesses.** $\omega$ and $\eta_k$ both place a $\pm$ spike and let the mass decay quadratically over a node set built from perfect squares ($ci^2$), then enforce pure high degree via the finite-difference identity (Lemma 9). This is the canonical OR / Nisan-Szegedy and MAJ / Paturi dual-witness pattern made fully explicit.
3. **Symmetrization as a verification device, not a construction device.** Lemma 5 (degree-$d$ polynomial collapses to univariate degree $d$ on $k$-to-1 inputs) is used to *check* pure high degree of a hand-built dual, not to build a primal lower bound. This inverts the usual use of symmetrization.
4. **Additivity of pure high degree (Lemma 15) plus exact cancellation on the bad set.** Build two dual gadgets each individually mis-correlated, arrange their masses on the obstruction set $R_{N/2,2,1}$ to cancel exactly, and sum. This "two-stage / cancellation" pattern is the central trick for handling functions that are neither symmetric nor block-composed.
5. **Dual-side reductions by averaging over extensions.** Theorem 16 shows how to transport a dual witness across a reduction by transposing the primal averaging: average $\psi$ over extensions instead of averaging $p$ over restrictions. Reusable wherever a primal reduction is known.
6. **Complementary slackness to locate dual support.** Section 6 turns an explicit primal approximation (from an algorithm) into a prediction of where the optimal dual must concentrate. This is a methodology for guessing dual witnesses, not just verifying them.

## Relevance to this project

This source sits squarely on the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge of the dossier's leading path. The dossier identifies the hinge as the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step where the polynomial method dies because MAJORITY has approximate degree $\Theta(n)$ (Paturi 1992). This paper supplies the explicit *dual* side of approximate-degree lower bounds: it is the concrete primal-dual object the strand asks for. Three specific connections:

- **The MAJ dual witness $\eta_k$ (Lemma 11) is the MAJORITY dual obstruction in dual form.** The dossier's claim that the polynomial method dies at $\mathsf{TC}^0$ because $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{MAJ}) = \Theta(n)$ (Paturi 1992) is here instantiated as an explicit certificate. The construction (squares node set, quadratic decay, spike at $N/2$) is the dual realization of why MAJORITY resists low-degree approximation. A builder wanting to understand the hinge concretely can read off the obstruction from $\eta_k$.
- **It demonstrates the lossless dual method.** Unlike symmetrization, the dual LP always has a tight witness (page 1). For the project this matters because the dossier wants the lower-bound certificate to be a tangible object that can be inspected for barrier behavior, not an existence claim.
- **Barrier discipline note (SURVEYOR caveat, not a resolution).** Approximate-degree / polynomial-method lower bounds are precisely the techniques the algebrization barrier (Aaronson-Wigderson 2008) is built to capture: they are arithmetization-flavored, working with low-degree real polynomials. The dossier's missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant, and its diagnosis is that "every rational trace/rank/volume invariant algebrizes." The dual polynomials here are rational-coefficient real objects of exactly that flavor. This is consistent with LEARNINGS finding (1) "barriers compose" and the hinge diagnosis: the explicit dual witness is a sharp picture of the technique that the barrier predicts will not, by itself, cross into $\mathsf{TC}^0$ super-polynomial territory. ## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions and LEARNINGS findings:

- **Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds toward $\mathsf{P/poly}$):** Element Distinctness's $\tilde\Omega(N^{2/3})$ is the best approximate-degree bound known for any $\mathsf{AC}^0$ function (page 1). Approximate degree lower bounds feed circuit lower bounds (sign-rank, threshold degree, $\mathsf{AC}^0$ vs depth-2 MAJ); this paper is a clean, explicit entry into that machinery. The block-composed / hardness-amplification line referenced here ([17], [18], [32], [43]) is the path from these single-function bounds toward harder classes.
- **Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion / MCSP):** indirect. The constructivity of the dual witness (it is hand-built and explicit) is the *good* kind of constructivity for a certificate, distinct from the largeness+constructivity that triggers Razborov-Rudich. No direct MCSP connection in this paper.
- **The $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge (the dossier's stated crux):** this is the central payoff. The paper gives the explicit MAJORITY-side dual obstruction ($\eta_k$) and the explicit Collision dual ($\psi$). It is the concrete object to inspect when asking what a fast-computable invariant for the hinge would have to look like and why the standard ones algebrize.
- **LEARNINGS finding (5) average-case is not worst-case** and **finding (9) the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant:** the complementary-slackness analysis (Section 6) builds the optimal primal approximation from a *quantum algorithm* (Brassard-Hoyer-Tapp). This is a direct, small-scale instance of the algorithm-to-lower-bound spine the dossier rides, restricted to one function. It shows the spine working in miniature, and it shows the invariant involved ($\mathrm{cross}_S$, a cross-collision count, computed exactly by a low-degree polynomial) is exactly the algebrizing kind.

Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes:

1. **Does the explicit dual witness $\psi$ (Theorem 13) or $\eta_k$ (Lemma 11) algebrize?** The dossier predicts yes (it is the prototypical arithmetization object). Confirming this through the project checker turns a prediction into a logged barrier profile. This is the most direct follow-up.
2. **Is there a dual witness whose support or coefficient structure carries mod-2 torsion (the dossier's "Bockstein bridge" candidate)?** Every witness here is a real/rational object. The question of what a *non-algebrizing* dual certificate would even look like is sharpened by having the algebrizing one fully explicit.
3. **Beyond symmetric and block-composed:** the authors note (page 6) that dual polynomials had only been built for symmetric functions and highly structured block-composed functions; Collision/ED fall into neither, and they introduce the two-stage cancellation technique to handle them. Which broader function classes (toward $\mathsf{TC}^0$-hard targets) admit explicit dual witnesses by extending this technique remains open and is the live frontier the paper points at.
4. **The $\log N / \log\log N$ gap in the warm-up (Theorem 10):** it stems from the divisibility requirement $k \mid N$. Whether a cleaner construction avoids the divisibility constraint without the full $\psi_1 + \psi_2$ machinery is a small open technical question.
