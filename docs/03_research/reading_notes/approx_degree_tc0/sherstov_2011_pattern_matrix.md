# Reading notes: The Pattern Matrix Method (Alexander A. Sherstov, 2011)

- **Source:** `references/approx_degree_tc0/sherstov_2011_pattern_matrix.pdf`
- **Type / venue:** Journal article, SIAM J. Comput. 2009 (this is the arXiv v1, 0906.4291). Earlier conference version: STOC 2008, "The Pattern Matrix Method for Lower Bounds on Quantum Communication."
- **Dossier strand:** $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge / approximate degree. This is the canonical "lifting" paper: it turns a degree obstruction on a base function $f$ into a communication and approximate-rank lower bound on a structured matrix built from $f$.
- **Read:** Read in full, all 30 pages. Sections 1 (introduction and results), 2 (preliminaries: matrix analysis, approximation and sign-representation, quantum model, generalized discrepancy method), 3 (LP duality for approximate degree and threshold weight), 4 (pattern matrices and their exact singular values), 5 (main theorem from approximate degree), 6 (threshold-weight version for small bias), 7 (discrepancy of pattern matrices), 8 (approximate rank and trace norm), 9 (new proof of Razborov's quantum bounds), 10 (discrepancy of $\mathsf{AC}^0$), 11 (log-rank conjecture for pattern matrices), 12 (related work: Shi-Zhu), and the reference list.

## Summary

The paper introduces the **pattern matrix method**: a general technique that converts an analytic lower bound on the approximate degree (or threshold weight) of a Boolean function $f$ into a lower bound on the bounded-error communication complexity, the approximate rank, the approximate trace norm, and the discrepancy of a structured matrix built from $f$, called the pattern matrix. The central machinery has two ingredients that are then plugged into the generalized (Klauck-Razborov) discrepancy method: (1) a closed-form computation of the exact singular values of a pattern matrix (Theorem 4.3), and (2) a linear-programming dual characterization of approximate degree and threshold weight (Theorems 3.2, 3.3, 3.4) that produces the "witness" function $\psi$. The main bound (Theorem 1.1) is $Q_{1/3}^*(F) > \tfrac{1}{4}\deg_{1/3}(f)\log(n/t) - 3$, valid for both classical and quantum protocols, even with unlimited prior entanglement. The method yields a new and simple proof of Razborov's optimal quantum lower bounds for every symmetric predicate (Theorem 1.3 / Section 9), exponential lower bounds on approximate rank for any function of high approximate degree (Theorem 1.4 / Section 8), and exponentially small discrepancy for explicit $\mathsf{AC}^0$ functions (Theorems 1.5, 1.6 / Section 10).

## Key definitions and results

### Core objects (Sections 2, 4)

- **$\epsilon$-approximate degree** $\deg_\epsilon(f)$ (Section 2.2, p. 9): the least degree of a real polynomial $p$ with $\|f - p\|_\infty \le \epsilon$. The canonical setting is $\epsilon = 1/3$. The relevant fact behind the whole $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge is **Paturi's estimate** (Theorem 2.6, p. 10): for a symmetric $f(x) \equiv D(\sum x_i)$, $\deg_{1/3}(f) = \Theta(\sqrt{n \ell_0(f)} + \sqrt{n \ell_1(f)})$, where $\ell_0, \ell_1$ measure how close to the endpoints the predicate $D$ changes value. For MAJORITY this gives $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$.
- **Degree-$d$ threshold weight** $W(f,d)$ (Sections 1, 2.2, pp. 2, 9): the minimum $\sum_{|S|\le d}|\lambda_S|$ over integers $\lambda_S$ with $f \equiv \mathrm{sgn}(\sum_{|S|\le d}\lambda_S \chi_S)$. Threshold degree $\deg_\pm(f)$ is the least $d$ with $W(f,d) < \infty$. Roughly, threshold weight corresponds to the $\epsilon$-approximate degree for $\epsilon = 1 - o(1)$ (small bias).
- **Pattern matrix** (Definition 4.1, p. 16): for $\phi:\{0,1\}^t \to \mathbb{R}$ and integers $t \mid n$, $t < n$, partition $[n]$ into $t$ contiguous blocks of size $n/t$. Let $\mathcal{V}(n,t)$ be the sets $V$ picking exactly one index from each block ($|\mathcal{V}(n,t)| = (n/t)^t$). The $(n,t,\phi)$-pattern matrix is $A = [\phi(x|_V \oplus w)]_{x \in \{0,1\}^n,\, (V,w) \in \mathcal{V}(n,t)\times\{0,1\}^t}$, of size $2^n$ by $(n/t)^t 2^t$. Communication interpretation: Alice holds $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ (she knows all $n$ values but not which are relevant), Bob holds the relevant index set $V$ (he knows which are relevant but not the values), and they compute $f(x|_V)$.

### The exact spectrum (Theorem 4.3, p. 17)

For the $(n,t,\phi)$-pattern matrix $A$, the nonzero singular values, counting multiplicities, are exactly
$$\bigcup_{S:\hat{\phi}(S)\neq 0}\Big\{\sqrt{2^{n+t}(n/t)^t}\cdot|\hat{\phi}(S)|(t/n)^{|S|/2}, \text{ repeated } (n/t)^{|S|}\text{ times}\Big\}.$$
In particular $\|A\| = \sqrt{2^{n+t}(n/t)^t}\max_S\{|\hat{\phi}(S)|(t/n)^{|S|/2}\}$. The spectral norm is dominated by the lowest-order Fourier coefficient present in $\phi$ (small $|S|$ makes $(t/n)^{|S|/2}$ large). This is the lever the dual witness pulls: if $\phi$ has no low-order Fourier mass, $\|A\|$ is small.

### Main communication lower bound (Theorem 1.1 / 5.1, pp. 3, 18)

Let $F$ be the $(n,t,f)$-pattern matrix. For every $\epsilon \in [0,1)$ and $\delta < \epsilon/2$,
$$Q_\delta^*(F) \ge \tfrac{1}{4}\deg_\epsilon(f)\log_2(n/t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2\!\big(3/(\epsilon - 2\delta)\big).$$
In particular $Q_{1/7}^*(F) > \tfrac{1}{4}\deg_{1/3}(f)\log_2(n/t) - 3$ (eq. 1.1). The bound holds for quantum protocols **regardless of prior entanglement**. By de Wolf's remark (Proposition 5.1, p. 19), $F$ also has a classical deterministic protocol of cost $O(\deg_{1/3}(f)^6 \log(n/t))$ via Beals et al., so the bound is tight up to a polynomial factor, exhibiting a large class of problems with polynomially related quantum and classical complexity even with entanglement.

### Threshold-weight (small-bias) version (Theorem 6.1, p. 20)

For the $(n,t,f)$-pattern matrix and every integer $d \ge 1$, $\gamma \in (0,1)$,
$$Q_{1/2 - \gamma/2}^*(F) \ge \tfrac{1}{4}\min\{d\log(n/t),\ \log(W(f,d-1)/(2t))\} - \tfrac{1}{2}\log(3/\gamma),$$
and in particular $Q_{1/2-\gamma/2}^*(F) \ge \tfrac{1}{4}\deg_\pm(f)\log(n/t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log(3/\gamma)$. This is shown nearly optimal by a matching protocol (Theorem 6.2, p. 21) of cost $d\log(n/t) + 3$.

### Approximate rank and trace norm (Theorem 1.4 / 8.1 / 8.2, pp. 4, 24)

For the $(n,t,f)$-pattern matrix with $s = 2^{n+t}(n/t)^t$ entries: $\mathrm{rk}_\delta F \ge \big(\tfrac{\epsilon - \delta}{1+\delta}\big)^2 (n/t)^{\deg_\epsilon(f)}$ and the small-bias version $\mathrm{rk}_{1-\gamma} F \ge \big(\tfrac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}\big)^2 \min\{(n/t)^d,\ W(f,d-1)/(2t)\}$, with the $\deg_\pm$ specialization $\mathrm{rk}_{1-\gamma} F \ge \big(\tfrac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}\big)^2 (n/t)^{\deg_\pm(f)}$. So any base function of high approximate degree yields a matrix whose $\epsilon$-approximate rank is exponential, generalizing Klivans-Sherstov's bounds for disjunctions, majorities, and decision lists to arbitrary high-degree functions. The $\epsilon$-approximate rank is $\mathrm{rk}_\epsilon F = \min\{\mathrm{rk}\,A : \|F - A\|_\infty \le \epsilon\}$ (Section 2.1, p. 8). The proof routes through the $\epsilon$-approximate trace norm $\|F\|_{\Sigma,\epsilon} = \min\{\|A\|_\Sigma : \|F-A\|_\infty \le \epsilon\}$ via Proposition 2.3 (p. 8): $\mathrm{rk}_\epsilon F \ge (\|F\|_{\Sigma,\epsilon})^2 / \sum_{i,j}(|F_{ij}|+\epsilon)^2$.

### Discrepancy (Theorems 1.5 / 7.3, pp. 4, 22)

$\mathrm{disc}(F) \ge \tfrac{1}{8 W(f,d)}(t/n)^d$ and $\mathrm{disc}(F)^2 \le \max\{2t/W(f,d-1), (t/n)^d\}$, with the clean form $\mathrm{disc}(F) \le (t/n)^{\deg_\pm(f)/2}$ (eq. 7.3). This is the **Degree/Discrepancy Theorem** (Theorem 7.4, p. 23, attributed to Sherstov's earlier work), here sharpened. Application (Theorems 1.6 / 10, pp. 5, 26): for the Minsky-Papert function $\mathrm{MP}_m(x) = \bigvee_{i=1}^m \bigwedge_{j=1}^{4m^2} x_{ij}$, $f(x,y) = \mathrm{MP}_m(x \vee y)$ has $\mathrm{disc}(f) = \exp\{-\Omega(m)\}$ (since $\deg_\pm(\mathrm{MP}_d) \ge d$). A new short proof is also given for the Buhrman-Vereshchagin-de Wolf $\mathsf{AC}^0$ function $\mathrm{OMB}_n$ (ODD-MAX-BIT, eq. 10.1), $\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{OMB}_n(x\wedge y)) = \exp\{-\Omega(n^{1/3})\}$. These give $\exp\{\Omega(m)\}$ and $\exp\{\Omega(n^{1/3})\}$ size lower bounds for depth-2 majority-of-threshold circuits computing these functions (Theorem 10.3, p. 27).

### Log-rank for pattern matrices (Theorem 11.1, p. 28)

$\mathrm{rk}\,F \ge (n/t)^d \ge \exp\{\Omega(D(F)^{1/4})\}$, so pattern matrices satisfy the log-rank conjecture (the second inequality uses Nisan-Smolensky, $\mathrm{dt}(f) \le 2\deg(f)^4$).

## Techniques and proof ideas

The reusable engine is the **generalized discrepancy method** (Sections 2.4, 5). To lower-bound the cost of computing $F$ you produce a matrix $\Psi$ with $\|\Psi\|_1 = 1$ such that $\langle F,\Psi\rangle$ is large (correlation) while $\|\Psi\|$ (spectral norm) is small (hardness). Theorem 2.8 (p. 12) makes this quantitative: $4^{Q_\epsilon^*(F)} \ge \langle\Psi,F\rangle - 2\epsilon$ over $3\|\Psi\|\sqrt{|X||Y|}$. The proof of Theorem 2.8 passes from a low-cost quantum protocol to a low-rank-ish matrix factorization (Theorem 2.7, p. 12: the acceptance-probability matrix factors as $AB$ with $\|A\|_F, \|B\|_F \le 2^C\sqrt{|X|}, 2^C\sqrt{|Y|}$) and bounds $\langle\Psi, J - 2\Pi\rangle$ two ways using $\langle A,B\rangle \le \|A\|\,\|B\|_\Sigma$ (eq. 2.2) and $\|AB\|_\Sigma \le \|A\|_F\|B\|_F$ (Prop 2.4, p. 9).

The two pieces a builder would reuse:

1. **Construct $\Psi$ from a dual witness via LP duality** (Section 3). The Ioffe-Tikhomirov norm-duality theorem (Theorem 3.1, p. 14) gives, as a corollary, the dual of approximate degree (Theorem 3.2, p. 15): if $\deg_\epsilon(f) = d$ there is $\psi:\{0,1\}^t \to \mathbb{R}$ with (i) $\hat\psi(S) = 0$ for all $|S| < d$ (orthogonality to low degree), (ii) $\sum |\psi(z)| = 1$ ($L_1$ normalization), and (iii) $\sum \psi(z)f(z) > \epsilon$ (correlation with $f$). The analogous duals for threshold degree (Theorem 3.3, Gordan's transposition) and threshold weight (Theorem 3.4, eqs. 3.2-3.3, due to Hajnal et al. and Freund) give the small-bias witnesses. The author notes that although approximate degree and its dual are classical, this use of the duality to prove communication lower bounds was new.

2. **Lift the witness to a pattern matrix and read off its norm.** Set $\Psi$ to be the $(n,t, 2^{-n}(n/t)^{-t}\psi)$-pattern matrix. Then $\|\Psi\|_1 = 1$ and $\langle F,\Psi\rangle > \epsilon$ follow from normalization and correlation (eq. 5.6). The orthogonality condition $\hat\psi(S) = 0$ for $|S| < d$, combined with the exact spectrum (Theorem 4.3), forces every surviving singular value of $\Psi$ to carry a factor $(t/n)^{|S|/2}$ with $|S| \ge d$, so $\|\Psi\| \le (t/n)^{d/2}(2^{n+t}(n/t)^t)^{-1/2}$ (eq. 5.8). Plugging into Theorem 2.8 gives the $\tfrac{1}{4}\deg(f)\log(n/t)$ bound. The whole method is: **low-degree-orthogonality of the dual witness kills the large (low-$|S|$) singular values of the lifted matrix; what survives is small; the discrepancy method does the rest.**

The spectral computation itself (Theorem 4.3) is the elegant core. Decompose $A = \sum_S \hat\phi(S) A_S$ over the parity pattern matrices $A_S$ (eq. 4.3), show $A_S A_T^\mathsf{T} = 0$ and $A_S^\mathsf{T} A_T = 0$ for $S \neq T$ via the orthogonality of characters (eqs. 4.4-4.5), then apply the singular-values-of-a-sum lemma (Lemma 4.2, p. 16: if $AB^\mathsf{T} = 0$ and $A^\mathsf{T}B = 0$ then the nonzero singular values of $A+B$ are the union of those of $A$ and $B$). Each $A_S^\mathsf{T}A_S$ is a Kronecker product of a rank-1 sign matrix with a block-diagonal all-ones matrix, giving the closed form (p. 18). The pattern structure (one variable per block) is exactly what makes the cross terms vanish.

The two-ingredient design is robust: swapping the approximate-degree dual for the threshold-weight dual gives the small-bias / discrepancy results (Sections 6, 7); composing with Proposition 2.3 gives approximate rank and trace norm (Section 8); applied to symmetric $f$ with Paturi's estimate it re-proves Razborov's quantum bounds for all symmetric predicates (Section 9, Theorems 9.1, 9.2, 1.3). The extension to the multiparty model (Section 12, p. 29) works because the rows of a pattern matrix are applications of the *same* function to different subsets of variables; this self-contained re-proof of Razborov's bounds (no Hahn matrices, no $\rho(g)$ blow-up) is the methodological selling point over the contemporaneous Shi-Zhu approach.

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the explicit machine behind the dossier's $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge. The leading-path narrative says the polynomial method dies at the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step because MAJORITY has approximate degree $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$, which is too small to push the degree-to-circuit lifting through to threshold circuits. Sherstov's pattern matrix method is exactly the lifting in question, stated in its cleanest form: **approximate degree $d$ of base $f$ becomes a communication / approximate-rank lower bound that scales as $(n/t)^{\Theta(d)}$.** Reading it pins down precisely what "the degree is the bottleneck" means quantitatively. For a function whose approximate degree is only $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ (MAJORITY, and more generally any symmetric predicate via Paturi, Theorem 2.6), the lifted lower bound is at most $\sqrt{n}\cdot\log(n/t)$ in communication and $(n/t)^{\Theta(\sqrt{n})}$ in approximate rank. That is strong against $\mathsf{AC}^0$-style and depth-2 majority circuits (Section 10) but is structurally incapable of separating $\mathsf{TC}^0$ from above, because MAJORITY itself is the cheap object and its degree is the ceiling.

On barrier discipline: the method is built entirely from real-polynomial approximation and singular values. It is the prototype of an **analytic, polynomial-method argument**, the family the project flags as algebrizing. Every quantity it produces (spectral norm, trace norm, approximate rank, discrepancy) is a rational linear-algebraic invariant of the matrix. This matches LEARNINGS finding (9): the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant, and finding (4)/(2) that natural, low-degree, "well-behaved" analytic arguments are common and barrier-bound. The pattern matrix method is a high-water mark of the algebrizing toolkit applied to communication and constant-depth circuits. It does not claim to attack $\mathsf{P/poly}$ or $\mathsf{TC}^0$ and it should not, by the project's reading, be expected to.

The connection to the dual-polynomial strand is direct: the witness $\psi$ here is precisely the **dual polynomial** object that Bun-Thaler later develop into a method (the sibling references `bun_thaler_2016_dual_polynomials.pdf`, `bun_thaler_2017_ac0_approx_degree.pdf`). Theorem 3.2 is the LP-duality statement those notes build on. Sherstov's Section 3 is the cleanest derivation of "a low approximate degree is certified by a low-degree-orthogonal, correlated, $L_1$-normalized dual" in the literature.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the four research directions:

- **01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** This is the analytic counterpart to the Williams spine, not part of it. The pattern matrix method gives circuit consequences only at the depth-2 majority-of-thresholds level (Theorem 10.3) and only for functions of high $\deg_\pm$ (Minsky-Papert MP$_m$, ODD-MAX-BIT). It does not reach unbounded-depth $\mathsf{TC}^0$, and the reason is structural: the bound is governed by the approximate degree of the base function. A builder should treat Theorems 1.4 and 7.3 as the best available "degree-to-lower-bound" exchange rate and remember the ceiling.
- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, MCSP crux):** The method is an analytic property and, applied to a generic base function, is large and constructive in the Razborov-Rudich sense. It does not evade the natural-proofs barrier and is not designed to. It belongs to the catalog of barrier-bound techniques, which is itself useful: it sharpens what "non-natural" must mean.
- **03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP):** Tangential. The discrepancy and small-bias results (Sections 6, 7, 10) connect to the $\mathsf{PP}^{cc}$ / margin-complexity world but not directly to propositional proof systems.
- **04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions):** Tangential, but there is a thematic rhyme worth flagging for SYNTHESIZER: both this method and GCT extract an invariant (here, singular-value multiplicities; there, representation-theoretic multiplicities) and both run into a "no-go" when the invariant turns out to be too coarse (here, bounded by approximate degree; there, the Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova occurrence obstruction). LEARNINGS finding (7).

Cruxes and open questions this source exposes:

1. **The degree ceiling is the hinge made precise.** Any pattern-matrix-style separation of $\mathsf{TC}^0$ would require a base function whose approximate degree the polynomial method can lower-bound past $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ for a function that is itself in or near $\mathsf{TC}^0$. Paturi (Theorem 2.6) caps symmetric functions at $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$. The open question is whether a non-symmetric base or a different lifting can break the $\sqrt{n}$ barrier in a way that still maps to circuit depth. (This is exactly the territory of the sibling Bun-Thaler and Kane-Williams notes.)
2. **Algebrization audit.** Every invariant in the paper is rational and linear-algebraic. Confirming whether any of them (or a torsion / mod-2 refinement, the dossier's "Bockstein bridge" candidate) could be made non-algebrizing is an ADVERSARY task. The honest reading is that nothing here escapes the algebrization barrier; the paper is evidence for finding (9), not a counterexample.
3. **Tightness limits.** Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 6.2 show the communication bounds are tight up to polynomial factors, so the method extracts essentially all the communication hardness that approximate degree alone certifies. There is no slack to exploit at the communication level; gains must come from a stronger degree lower bound on a better base function, not from a cleverer lift.
