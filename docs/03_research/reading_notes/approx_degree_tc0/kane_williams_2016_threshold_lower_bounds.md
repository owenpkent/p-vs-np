## Summary
Kane and Williams prove the first super-linear gate lower bounds and the first super-quadratic wire lower bounds for depth-2 linear-threshold ($\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$) circuits and for depth-3 majority ($\mathsf{TC}^0_3 = \mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ}$, and the slightly larger $\mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$) circuits, against explicit functions computable in $\mathsf{P}$ (in fact in uniform $\mathsf{TC}^0$). For Andreev's function $A_n$ they get an average-case $\Omega(n^{3/2}/\log^3 n)$ gate bound and $\Omega(n^{5/2}/\log^{7/2} n)$ wire bound at depth 2 (Theorem 1.1), and for a code-modified Andreev function $B_n$ they get the same orders at depth 3 (Theorem 1.3). The central new tool is a random restriction lemma for LTFs driven by the Littlewood-Offord lemma from additive combinatorics, plus a counting/correlation argument for hardness of random low-depth threshold functions.

## Key definitions and results

**Models (Section 1, Section 2).** An LTF is a pair $(L,t)$ with real weights, $L(x)=\sum_i a_i x_i$, output $\llbracket L(\sigma) \geq t \rrbracket$ (page 4). $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ is depth-2 with arbitrary real weights; $\mathsf{TC}^0_3 = \mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ}$ restricts weights to $\mathbb{Z} \cap [-\mathrm{poly}(n), \mathrm{poly}(n)]$ (page 1). These correspond to neural nets with one or two hidden layers.

**The hard functions.**
- *Andreev's function* $A_n$ (page 1, [And87]): for $n = 2 \cdot 2^k$, write $A_n(x, a_{1,1}\dots a_{k,2^k/k}) = M_{2^k}(x, \bigoplus_j a_{1,j} \bmod 2, \dots, \bigoplus_j a_{k,j} \bmod 2)$, where $M_{2^k}$ is the multiplexer (address-and-data lookup) on the $2^k$-bit table $x$. So $A_n$ takes parities of $k$ disjoint blocks and uses the resulting $k$-bit string to index into the truth table $x$. It is a long-standing target for formula-size lower bounds and is in linear-size $\mathsf{TC}^0_3$.
- *Code-modified function* $B_n$ (Definition 6.1, page 15): $B_{n,k}(x,a) := F(z,x)$ where $z_i = \bigoplus_{j} a_j \bmod 2$ over $n/k$-bit blocks ($k = 16\log_2 n$), and $F(z,x) = M(A_{m,n} x, z)$ feeds $x$ through the generator matrix $A_{m,n}$ of an $1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$-balanced (small-biased) error-correcting code before applying the multiplexer. This is similar to the Komargodski-Raz [KR13] modification of Andreev's function. The point of the code is to manufacture many $\log n$-bit subfunctions that are pairwise nearly uncorrelated, so a top majority gate cannot succeed.

**Theorem 1.1 (depth-2 Andreev, page 2).** Any $f$ that agrees with $A_n$ on a $(1/2 + \epsilon)$-fraction of inputs, for $\epsilon \gg \sqrt{\log(n)/n}$, cannot be computed by $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ circuits with fewer than $\Omega(\epsilon^3 n^{3/2}/\log^3 n)$ gates nor fewer than $\Omega(\epsilon^3 n^{5/2}/\log^{7/2} n)$ wires. This is an average-case (correlation) lower bound.

**Theorem 1.2 (matching upper bounds, page 2).** $A_n$ has uniform depth-3 $\mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ}$ ($\mathsf{TC}^0_3$) circuits of $O(n)$ gates, uniform $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ circuits of $O(n^3/\log n)$ gates, parity decision trees of depth $\leq \log_2 n$, and uniform $\mathsf{MOD3} \circ \mathsf{MOD2}$ circuits of $O(n^2)$ gates. Hence Theorem 1.1 establishes a genuine average-case size hierarchy: $A_n$ is in $O(n)$-gate $\mathsf{TC}^0_3$ but needs $\Omega(n^{3/2 - o(1)})$ gates as a depth-2 LTF circuit even on a $1/2 + \epsilon$ fraction.

**Corollary 1.1 (approximate-majority refinement, page 2).** Every $\epsilon$-Approximate Majority of $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ for $A_n$ needs $\Omega(\epsilon^3 n^{3/2}/\log^3 n)$ gates and $\Omega(\epsilon^3 n^{5/2}/\log^{7/2} n)$ wires. Consequence (page 12): $A_n$ has no $\mathsf{TC}^0_3$ circuit of $O(n^{1.1})$ gates where the output gate has fan-in $o(n^{2/15})$.

**Theorem 1.3 (depth-3, page 2 and page 15).** There is no $\mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ circuit of $o(n^{3/2}/\log^3 n)$ gates or $o(n^{5/2}/\log^{7/2} n)$ wires computing $B_n$.

**Theorem 1.4 (tight PARITY in depth-2, page 2 and Section 4).** The gate complexity of $\mathsf{MAJ} \circ \mathsf{MAJ}$ circuits agreeing with PARITY on 99% of $n$-bit inputs is $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$; the wire complexity is $\Theta(n^{3/2})$. The lower bounds hold even for $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$. This shows the classical Impagliazzo-Paturi-Saks [IPS93] $\Omega(n^{3/2})$ wire / $\Omega(n^{1/2})$ gate PARITY bounds are tight in the average case, and (crucially) explains why those PARITY bounds cannot be pushed: PARITY has $O(\sqrt n)$-gate, $O(n^{3/2})$-wire $\mathsf{MAJ}\circ\mathsf{MAJ}$ circuits agreeing on 99% of inputs.

**Context on the Allender-Koucky connection (page 1).** By [AK10] (self-reducibility / hardness amplification), to separate $\mathsf{NC}^1$ from $\mathsf{TC}^0$ it suffices to exhibit an $\mathsf{NC}^1$ function with no $n^{1.1}$-gate depth-$d$ threshold circuit for every $d \geq 2$. So even proving super-linear ($n^{1.1}$) gate bounds for all constant depths would suffice. This paper makes the first super-linear progress at depths 2 and 3.

### The analytic engine

**Lemma 1.1 / Lemma 3.1 (Random Restrictions on LTFs, pages 3, 7 to 9).** Let $f$ be an LTF on $[n]$, let $\mathcal{P}$ be a partition of $[n]$ into equal-size parts, and let $\mathcal{R}_\mathcal{P}$ be the distribution that leaves one random element of each part free ($\star$) and fixes everything else to uniform random bits. Then
$$\Pr_{\rho \sim \mathcal{R}_\mathcal{P}}[f \text{ is not forced to a constant by } \rho] = O(|\mathcal{P}|/\sqrt{n}).$$
"Forced to a constant" means the restricted linear form either always exceeds or never reaches the threshold (page 7, Proposition 1 lets a forced gate be deleted). The refinement (Lemma 3.1, page 9) is for thresholds depending on few inputs: if $f$ depends on only $w$ inputs, then $\Pr[f \text{ not forced to a function of a single input}] = O(w |\mathcal{P}|^{3/2}/n^{3/2})$. A gate forced to a single-input function can be replaced by one wire, which is what powers the wire bounds.

**Lemma 2.1 (scaled Littlewood-Offord, page 4, [LO43], [Erd45]).** For $L(x) = \sum a_i x_i$ and any finite interval $I \subset \mathbb{R}$, if at least $k$ of the $|a_i| \geq |I|$, then $\Pr_{x \in_u \{0,1\}^n}[L(x) \in I] \leq O(1)/\sqrt{k}$. This is the additive-combinatorics core: the anti-concentration of a random sign sum is what forces an LTF to be "decided" by a random restriction.

**Counting / hardness of random LTF circuits (Section 2, pages 5 to 6).** Winder's theorem (Theorem 2.1, [Win60]): there are $2^{O(n^2)}$ LTFs on $n$ variables. Chow's theorem (Theorem 2.2, [Cho61]): an LTF is determined by its $n+1$ degree-$\leq 1$ Fourier coefficients. Roychowdhury-Siu-Orlitsky generalization (Theorem 2.3, [ROS94b], reproved self-contained here): there are $2^{O(n^2 s)}$ distinct $\mathsf{LTF} \circ \mathsf{LTF}$ circuits with $s$ bottom gates. Corollary 2.1: a random function needs $\Omega(2^n/n^2)$ gates in depth-2. Corollary 2.2 (inapproximability, page 6): for $\epsilon \gg \sqrt{n/2^n}$, all but an $\eta$-fraction of functions have no size-$o(\eta^2 2^n / n^2)$ depth-2 LTF circuit agreeing on more than $1/2 + \epsilon$ of inputs.

### Additive-combinatorics ingredients for depth 3 (Section 6)

**Proposition 2 (small-biased generator matrix, page 13).** Using Alon-Goldreich-Hastad-Peralta [AGHP92], there is a poly-time computable $A_{m,t} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m \times t}$ so that for $x \neq y$, $Ax$ and $Ay$ agree on $m(1/2 + O(\delta))$ bits.

**Lemma 6.1 (Gram-matrix / spectral correlation bound, page 14).** If $\mathcal{S}$ is a set of $m$-bit strings pairwise at relative Hamming distance $1/2 + \Omega(\gamma)$, then any other string $T$ is well-correlated ($|rh(T,S) - 1/2| > \Omega(\gamma^{1/2})$) with at most $O(\gamma^{-1})$ of them. Proof builds a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix $G$ and derives a contradiction from $vGv^T < 0$ for a designed vector $v$.

**Corollary 6.2 (existence of a globally bad table, page 15).** There is an $x$ so that for all $\log_2 m$-bit functions $C$ implementable by $o(n/\log^2 n)$-gate $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuits, $C(z)$ agrees with $F(z,x)$ on at most a $(1/2 + \delta)$ fraction of $z$, with $\delta = O(n^{1/2})/m^{1/4}$.

## Techniques and proof ideas

1. **Random restriction tuned for LTFs.** Unlike $\mathsf{AC}^0$ where AND/OR gates are "forced" by a single $0$/$1$ input, an LTF is forced only when its restricted linear part lands outside an interval. Kane and Williams reduce "not forced" to "linear form lands in interval $I$" and bound the interval-hitting probability by Littlewood-Offord (Lemma 2.1). The restriction is adversarial-structured: $\mathcal{P}$ is an arbitrary partition (so the analysis tolerates a circuit designer choosing the bottom-gate fan-ins), leaving one free element per block. This is the key generalization over [IPS93], whose lemmas (a) need $\geq n^{3/2}$ wires to be nontrivial, (b) fix a particular random partition rather than an adversarial one, and (c) only reduce bottom gates rather than using anti-concentration to compute the forcing probability (page 7, "Comparison with IPS").

2. **Multiplexer-plant-a-hard-function (Theorem 1.1, Section 5).** Plug the truth table of a random $k$-bit function into the $x$-input of $A_n$'s multiplexer (a trick already in [And87]). Restrict so the $2^k$ table bits are uniform random and one $a_{i,j}$ per block survives. If the candidate $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuit $C$ were small, the random restriction lemma forces almost all bottom gates to constants or single inputs, collapsing $C$ to a single $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ on $k = O(\log n)$ bits, which by the random-function inapproximability Corollary 2.2 cannot agree well with a random $k$-bit function. Setting parameters yields the contradiction.

3. **Approximate-majority lift (Corollary 1.1, page 12).** Since the bound is average-case, a $1/2+\epsilon$ majority vote of small $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuits is captured by a Boolean matrix argument: each circuit row has $< 1/2+\epsilon$ ones, yet correctness forces $\geq 1/2+\epsilon$ ones per column, a contradiction. This is the partial bridge into $\mathsf{TC}^0_3$ (a majority of depth-2 subcircuits).

4. **Code modification for full depth 3 (Theorem 1.3, Section 6).** Andreev's function fails the depth-3 argument because its $\log n$-bit subfunctions correlate at $\sim 1/\sqrt n$ with majority (page 13). Replacing the multiplexer table by a small-biased code $A_{m,n}$ manufactures $> \log_2 n$-bit functions $F(\cdot, x)$ that are pairwise near-orthogonal (Proposition 2), so no single $\log n$-bit string (hence no small bottom subcircuit) correlates with many of them (Lemma 6.1, Corollary 6.2). The top $\mathsf{MAJ}$ then has $o(1/s)$ correlation with $B_n$, contradicting the standard "$s$-size $\mathsf{MAJ}\circ g$ has $\Omega(1/s)$ correlation with some subcircuit $g$" fact (page 13).

5. **Tight PARITY (Theorem 1.4, Section 4).** Upper bound: build the top gate as a sum of exact-threshold indicators $L_k - L_{k+1}$ over $k$ within $\pm c\sqrt n$ of $n/2$, agreeing with parity on 99% of inputs with $O(\sqrt n)$ gates / $O(n^{3/2})$ wires. Lower bound: a single LTF on two inputs cannot approximate PARITY beyond 75% (Minsky-Papert [MP69]); restricting to two free inputs and applying both random-restriction lemmas shows the expected number of nontrivial bottom gates is $O(\min(s/\sqrt n, w/n^{3/2}))$, so if $s \ll \sqrt n$ or $w \ll n^{3/2}$ the circuit collapses to a single LTF and fails.

**Honest limit the authors flag (page 16).** They explicitly cannot prove that $B_n$ disagrees with every small $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuit on a $1/2 + \epsilon$ fraction for $\epsilon = n^{-\Omega(1)}$. Their method only shows that *after an appropriate restriction* the remaining subcircuits have low correlation with the remaining subfunction. There is always a $\Omega(1/\sqrt n)$ probability that no bottom gate simplifies and the circuit agrees on all consistent inputs, so the approach is inherently insufficient for correlation bounds with $\epsilon < 1/\sqrt n$. This is the $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ wall showing up directly.

## Relevance to this project

This paper sits exactly on the dossier's identified **$\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge**: the step where the polynomial method dies because MAJORITY has approximate degree $\Theta(\sqrt n)$. The $\sqrt n$ scaling here is not incidental, it is the Littlewood-Offord anti-concentration exponent $1/\sqrt k$ (Lemma 2.1) surfacing as the $\Omega(n^{3/2}) = \Omega(n \cdot \sqrt n)$ gate-bound order and as the explicit $\epsilon < 1/\sqrt n$ wall the authors hit (page 16). It is a clean, concrete instance of the dossier claim that the threshold barrier is governed by the $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ approximate degree of MAJORITY: the very same square-root both *enables* the lower bound (random restrictions force most LTFs) and *caps* it (a $\Omega(1/\sqrt n)$ survival probability blocks any $\epsilon < 1/\sqrt n$ correlation bound).

On the **leading path** (the Williams algorithm-to-lower-bound spine, here in its lower-bound-technique guise rather than the SAT-algorithm guise): this is Williams himself, working the depth-2/3 threshold frontier with a non-algebraic, restriction-based method. The conclusion (page 16, item 3 and item 4) explicitly connects the random restriction lemma to faster $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ SAT algorithms ([IPS13], [Wil14]) and asks whether a "concentration" version (number of surviving gates tightly concentrated around expectation) or the [Wil14] fast evaluation algorithm could yield exponential gate bounds. That is the algorithm-to-lower-bound bridge in miniature.

On the **barrier discipline**: this technique is non-natural in flavor (it targets a specific structured Andreev/code function, not a large random-function property) and non-algebrizing in flavor (the engine is Littlewood-Offord anti-concentration plus small-biased codes, not arithmetization or low-degree extension). It is consistent with LEARNINGS finding (6), Williams as the existence proof that barriers are passable, and with finding (9), the missing object being a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant: the restriction lemma is precisely a combinatorial, non-algebraic property. A formal pass through `experiments/_shared/barriers.py` was not run here (SURVEYOR reports; the ADVERSARY should score it), but the technique's profile (combinatorial restriction + anti-concentration + coding) does not obviously relativize or algebrize, and is not a natural property because it is not large.

## What this enables / what remains open

**Maps to research direction 01 (circuit lower bounds toward $\mathsf{P/poly}$).** This is direct progress at the lowest threshold depths: the first super-linear gate and super-quadratic wire bounds at depth 2 and 3. Via [AK10] (page 1), super-linear gate bounds at *all* constant depths would separate $\mathsf{NC}^1$ from $\mathsf{TC}^0$; this paper clears depths 2 and 3 for explicit (in fact $\mathsf{TC}^0$) functions. A BUILDER should treat the random restriction lemma (Lemma 1.1 / 3.1) as a reusable primitive for any threshold-depth construction, and treat the $A_n$/$B_n$ multiplexer-plus-code template as the canonical hard-function scaffold.

**Maps to LEARNINGS findings.**
- Finding (1) barriers compose and finding (6) Williams shows barriers are passable: this is a passable, non-natural, non-algebrizing-flavored technique that nonetheless stalls at $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, illustrating that evading the named barriers does not by itself defeat the threshold approximate-degree wall.
- Finding (5) average-case is not worst-case: the headline bounds are explicitly correlation (average-case) bounds (Theorem 1.1), and Theorem 1.4 shows the average-case PARITY answer matches the worst-case [IPS93] answer, a concrete data point on the average/worst boundary.
- The dossier's $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ approximate-degree hinge is realized literally: the authors prove they cannot pass $\epsilon < 1/\sqrt n$ (page 16).

**Concrete open questions this source exposes (Section 7, page 16).**
1. Can one get asymptotically tight $A_n$/$B_n$ bounds with $\epsilon \leq 1/n^{\Omega(1)}$, or average-case lower bounds for functions in $\mathrm{TIME}[2^{O(n)}]$? The authors cannot cross the $1/\sqrt n$ correlation wall; crossing it is the live $\mathsf{TC}^0$-hinge crux.
2. Are there poly-size $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuits for IP2? By [AM05] either answer separates threshold circuit classes.
3. Is there a faster $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ SAT algorithm (currently nontrivial only for slightly super-linear wires [IPS13])? A "concentration" version of the random restriction lemma (gates surviving tightly concentrated around the expectation) is the named sub-goal, and is the natural algorithm-to-lower-bound lever for the leading path.
4. Can the [Wil14] $2^{n} \cdot \mathrm{poly}(n)$-time evaluation of $\mathsf{LTF}\circ\mathsf{LTF}$ circuits be turned into an exponential gate lower bound? This is the direct Williams-spine question for this strand.
