## Summary
A short authoritative survey of the geometric complexity theory (GCT) program for the permanent-versus-determinant problem, written by one of the central figures and updated with a "note added in proof" reporting the 2016 no-go results. It sketches the known upper bound $\mathrm{dc}(m) \le 2^m - 1$ (Grenet) and lower bound $m^2/2 \le \mathrm{dc}(m)$ (Mignon-Ressayre), then develops the representation-theoretic obstruction idea: a lower bound $\mathrm{dc}(m) > n$ would follow from an "occurrence obstruction," a partition $\lambda$ whose multiplicity in the coordinate ring of the determinant orbit closure vanishes while its plethysm multiplicity does not. The survey then ties the feasibility of finding such obstructions to the complexity of deciding positivity of Kronecker coefficients (shown NP-hard by Ikenmeyer-Mulmuley-Walter), and the note added in proof reports that occurrence obstructions provably cannot prove $\mathsf{VP} \ne \mathsf{VNP}$ (Ikenmeyer-Panova; Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova).

## Key definitions and results

### Motivation and the target (Section 1)
- $\det_n := \sum_{\pi \in S_n} \mathrm{sgn}(\pi) \prod_i x_{i\pi(i)}$ is computable in $O(n^3)$ arithmetic operations (Gaussian elimination); $\mathrm{per}_n := \sum_{\pi \in S_n} \prod_i x_{i\pi(i)}$ drops the sign and is VNP-complete (Valiant 1979, ref [59]). Proving $\mathrm{per}_n$ requires superpolynomially many arithmetic operations amounts to $\mathsf{VP} \ne \mathsf{VNP}$, the algebraic analog and "easier variant" of $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$ (Section 1, page 2).

### Determinantal complexity (Section 2)
- **Definition 2.1** (page 2): $\mathrm{dc}(f)$ of a polynomial $f$ is the smallest $s$ such that there exists a size-$s$ square matrix $A$ with affine-linear entries and $f = \det(A)$. Write $\mathrm{dc}(m) := \mathrm{dc}(\mathrm{per}_m)$. Known small values: $\mathrm{dc}(2) = 2$, and $\mathrm{dc}(3) = 7$ (optimality of Grenet's size-7 representation of $\mathrm{per}_3$, refs [30],[2]). Historical no-go: Polya 1913 (ref [52]) asked whether $\mathrm{per}_n = \det[\epsilon_{ij} x_{ij}]$ for a sign matrix; disproved by Szego the same year ([58]); Marcus-Minc ([44]) showed no matrix of linear forms gives $\mathrm{per}_n = \det[f_{pq}]$ for $n \ge 3$.
- **Theorem 2.2 (Grenet, ref [27]):** $\mathrm{dc}(m) \le 2^m - 1$. Proof (page 2-3): interpret $\det(A) = \sum_c \mathrm{sgn}(c)\,\mathrm{weight}(c)$ over cycle covers of a weighted digraph; build a digraph $P_m$ on the power set $2^{[m]}$ with edges $S \to S \cup \{j\}$ of weight $x_{ij}$, so $\mathrm{per}_m(X) = \sum_\pi \mathrm{weight}(\pi)$ over directed paths $\emptyset \to [m]$. Adding unit-weight loops and identifying $\emptyset$ with $[m]$ yields a $(2^m - 1)$-size adjacency matrix whose determinant is $\pm \mathrm{per}_m$. Landsberg-Ressayre (ref [37]) proved this representation optimal among those respecting "half of the symmetries" of $\mathrm{per}_m$.
- **Theorem 2.3 (Mignon-Ressayre, ref [45]):** $m^2/2 \le \mathrm{dc}(m)$ if $\mathrm{char}\,K = 0$. This is the best known lower bound (a slight improvement over $\mathbb{R}$ is $(m-1)^2 + 1 \le \mathrm{dc}(m)$, Yabe ref [63]; an extension to positive characteristic is ref [16], Cai-Chen-Li). Proof (pages 3-4) via the Hessian $H_f := [\partial^2 f / \partial x_\alpha \partial x_\beta]$. Lemma 2.4: under an affine substitution $F = f(Lx + b)$ the Hessian transforms as $H_F(x) = L^T H_f(Lx+b) L$. Lemma 2.5: $\mathrm{rank}\,H_{\det}(N)$ at a rank-deficient $N$ (rank $s < n$) is $\le 2n$, because $\det$ is an $\mathrm{SL}_n \times \mathrm{SL}_n$ invariant (computation in normal form). Lemma 2.6: there exists $M$ with $\mathrm{per}(M) = 0$ but $H_{\mathrm{per}}(M)$ of full rank $m^2$ (explicit $M$ with $1-m$ on a corner and ones elsewhere). Combining: if $\mathrm{dc}(m) \le n$ then $m^2 = \mathrm{rank}\,H_{\mathrm{per}}(M) \le \mathrm{rank}\,H_{\det}(N) \le 2n$.

### The GCT attempt (Section 3)
- **Definition 3.1 / 3.4:** Over $K = \mathbb{C}$, the determinant variety is the orbit closure $\Omega_n := \overline{\mathrm{GL}_{n^2} \cdot \det_n} \subseteq \mathrm{Sym}^n \mathbb{C}^{n^2}$. Theorem 3.4: $\Omega_n$ is Zariski-closed (Euclidean and Zariski closures coincide for constructible sets, Mumford [50]).
- **Padded permanent (eq 3.1, page 5):** homogenizing $\mathrm{per}_m$ with an extra variable $t$ gives the padded permanent $t^{n-m} \mathrm{per}_m(X)$, which lies in $\Omega_n$ whenever $\mathrm{dc}(m) \le n$. The Mulmuley-Sohoni proposal (ref [48]): prove $t^{n-m}\mathrm{per}_m(X) \notin \Omega_n$, which is stronger than $\mathrm{dc}(m) > n$ but approachable by algebraic geometry / geometric invariant theory.
- **Strategy:** exhibit a polynomial function $R: \mathrm{Sym}^n\mathbb{C}^{n^2} \to \mathbb{C}$ vanishing on $\Omega_n$ but not on the padded permanent. To find $R$, exploit the $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}$-symmetry of the vanishing ideal $I(\Omega_n)$ and decompose into irreducibles.
- **Representation theory primer (3.2):** irreducible $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}$-modules $V_\lambda$ are labelled by highest weights / partitions $\lambda$. Plethysm coefficients $\mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda)$ appear in $\mathrm{Sym}^d \mathrm{Sym}^n \mathbb{C}^{n^2} = \bigoplus_{\lambda \vdash dn} \mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda) V_\lambda$ (eq 3.2). The degree-$d$ vanishing ideal decomposes as $I(\Omega_n)_d = \bigoplus \mathrm{multdet}_n(\lambda) V_\lambda$ (eq 3.3).
- **Definition 3.7 (Kronecker coefficient):** $k(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3) := \mathrm{mult}(V_{\lambda_1} \otimes V_{\lambda_2} \otimes V_{\lambda_3}, \mathrm{Sym}^N(\mathbb{C}^{m_1} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{m_2} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{m_3}))$; equivalently (Schur-Weyl, page 7) $k(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3) = \dim([\lambda]\otimes[\mu]\otimes[\nu])^{S_N}$ via Specht modules. Rectangular special case: $k_n(\lambda) := k(\lambda, n\times d, n\times d)$ for $\lambda \vdash dn$ (eq 3.4).
- **GCT-coefficients (eq 3.5):** $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda) := \mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda) - \mathrm{multdet}_n(\lambda)$, the multiplicity of $V_\lambda$ in the coordinate ring $\mathbb{C}[\Omega_n]$.
- **Theorem 3.9 (Mulmuley-Sohoni, ref [49]):** $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda) \le k_n(\lambda)$ for $\lambda \vdash_{n^2} dn$ (refinement in ref [15]). This is the key inequality tying GCT-coefficients to rectangular Kronecker coefficients.
- **Occurrence obstruction (Definition 3.11, Proposition 3.12):** an obstruction is a $\lambda \vdash_{n^2} dn$ with $k_n(\lambda) = 0$ and $\mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda) > 0$. Then $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda) = 0$ but the plethysm multiplicity is positive, forcing a type-$\lambda$ polynomial vanishing on $\Omega_n$; a generic $f$ in few variables then satisfies $\mathrm{dc}(f) > n$. Example 3.13 (Ikenmeyer, ref [29]): $\lambda = (13,13,2,2,2,2,2)$ is an obstruction for degree-3 forms in 7 variables, found by computer (plethysm = 1, $k_3(\lambda) = 0$, search space $\dim \mathrm{Sym}^{12}\mathbb{C}^{165} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{19}$).
- **Theorem 3.10 (Landsberg-Kadish, refs [15],[33]):** a nonvanishing obstruction $\lambda \vdash_{n^2} dn$ must satisfy $\ell(\lambda) \le \ell + 1$ and $\lambda_1 \ge |\lambda|(1 - m/n)$. So obstructions have few rows and almost all boxes in the first row.
- **Proof sketch of Theorem 3.9 (3.4):** Theorem 3.15 (Frobenius, ref [24], rediscovered by Dieudonne [21]): the stabilizer $\mathrm{stab}_n$ of $\det_n$ is $(\mathrm{SL}_n \times \mathrm{SL}_n)/\mu_n \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$. Via the algebraic Peter-Weyl theorem, the multiplicity of $V_\lambda$ in $\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}\cdot\det_n]$ equals $\dim(V_\lambda)^{\mathrm{stab}_n}$; Schur-Weyl duality restricts $V_\lambda$ to $\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_n$ with Kronecker multiplicities, and the $\mathrm{SL}_n \times \mathrm{SL}_n$-invariants give exactly $k_n(\lambda)$ (rectangular case $\mu = \nu = n \times d$).
- **Obstructions must be gaps (3.5):** the highest-weight set $K_n := \{\lambda \vdash_{n^2} dn : k_n(\lambda) > 0\}$ is a finitely generated monoid. Theorem 3.19 (Burgisser-Christandl-Ikenmeyer, ref [8]): the saturation of $K_n$ equals all partitions with $\ell(\lambda) \le n^2$ and $|\lambda|$ a multiple of $n$. This excludes asymptotic techniques for finding obstructions (obstructions must be true monoid gaps, not just saturation gaps). Theorem 3.20 (Kumar, ref [36], conditional on Alon-Tarsi for $n$) and Theorem 3.21 (Burgisser-Huttenhain-Ikenmeyer, ref [10], unconditional): for $\lambda$ with $\ell(\lambda) \le n$ and $|\lambda|$ a multiple of $n$, $\tilde{k}_n(s\lambda) > 0$ for some stretching factor $s$.

### Positivity of Kronecker coefficients (Section 4)
- **Theorem 4.1 (Ikenmeyer-Mulmuley-Walter, ref [31]):** testing positivity of Kronecker coefficients is NP-hard (even with partitions encoded in unary, which makes the problem easier). This refutes the natural hope, in Mulmuley's conjecture (ref [46]), that positivity could be decided in polynomial time in general (it can for Littlewood-Richardson coefficients, refs [47],[40],[12], and for fixed $m$ Kronecker coefficients, ref [18]).
- **Proof outline (pages 11-13):** via 3D-relations (finite subsets $R \subseteq \mathbb{N}^3$) and their $x,y,z$-marginals. Lemma 4.3 (Manivel ref [43], rediscovered in [13],[31]): $p(\lambda,\mu,\nu) \le k(\lambda,\mu,\nu) \le t(\lambda,\mu,\nu)$, sandwiching the Kronecker coefficient between counts of pyramids (plane partitions) and 3D-relations with prescribed marginals. Lemma 4.4: simplex-like triples force a 3D-relation to be a pyramid, giving $k = t$. Theorem 4.5 (Brunetti-Del Lungo-Gerard, ref [5]): deciding $t(\lambda,\mu,\nu) > 0$ is NP-hard; the reduction from 3D-matching lands on simplex-like triples, yielding the NP-hardness of Kronecker positivity and an explicit way to produce many monoid gaps.
- **The catch (page 13):** the reduction breaks down precisely for the "most wanted" partition triples $(\lambda, \mu, \mu)$ with $\mu$ rectangular, because $t(\lambda, n\times d, n\times d) > 0$ whenever $\ell(\lambda) \le \min\{d^2, n^2\}$ (ref [31], Thm 6.9). So the NP-hardness does not by itself solve Problem 3.14 (finding obstruction families for few-row forms).
- **Theorem 4.7 (Burgisser-Christandl-Mulmuley-Walter, ref [9]):** the asymptotic positivity problem for Kronecker coefficients (deciding whether $k(s\lambda, s\mu, s\nu) > 0$ for some $s \ge 1$, binary encoding) is in $\mathsf{NP} \cap \mathsf{coNP}$. NP via the moment map / Kronecker polytope membership (Mumford [51]); coNP via Ressayre's facet description ([55]) made efficiently testable by Vergne-Walter ([61]). The authors conjecture this asymptotic version may be in $\mathsf{P}$.

### Note added in proof (Section 5): the no-go results
- **Theorem 5.1 (Ikenmeyer-Panova, ref [32]):** for $\lambda \vdash dn$ with $\ell(\lambda) \le m^2$, $\lambda_1 \ge |\lambda|(1-m/n)$, and $n > 3m^4$, $\mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda) > 0$ implies $k_n(\lambda) > 0$. Consequence: the vanishing of rectangular Kronecker coefficients cannot prove superpolynomial determinantal-complexity lower bounds, i.e. occurrence obstructions of this rectangular Kronecker type fail.
- **Theorem 5.2 (Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova, ref [14]):** for $\lambda \vdash dn$ with $\ell(\lambda) \le m^2$, $\lambda_1 \ge |\lambda|(1-m/n)$, and $n > m^{25}$, $\mathrm{pleth}_n(\lambda) > 0$ implies $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda) > 0$. This is the GCT-coefficient version: it rules out proving $\mathsf{VP} \ne \mathsf{VNP}$ via occurrence obstructions (page 14). Main ingredients: a splitting technique, combinatorial encoding of highest-weight vectors in plethysms via tableaux with contents $d \times n$, and a "lifting" of highest weight vectors (Kadish-Landsberg ref [33]) using stability of plethysm coefficients; only information needed about $\Omega_n$ is that it contains certain padded power sums.
- **Final remarks (5.1):** the multiplicity (rather than occurrence) version of the GCT obstruction approach remains open: if the orbit closure $Z_{n,m}$ of the padded permanent is contained in $\Omega_n$ there is a surjective $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}$-equivariant map $\mathbb{C}[\Omega_n] \to \mathbb{C}[Z_{n,m}]$, so $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda)$ strictly smaller than the $Z_{n,m}$-multiplicity would prove non-containment. But ref [18] shows comparing multiplicities by asymptotic methods alone cannot suffice for separation. Larsen-Pink (ref [39]) is flagged by Mulmuley as potentially relevant.

## Techniques and proof ideas
A builder working the GCT strand can reuse the following machinery from this survey:

1. **Hessian rank as a separating invariant (Theorem 2.3, Lemmas 2.4-2.6).** The determinant's Hessian has bounded rank ($\le 2n$) at rank-deficient points because $\det$ is $\mathrm{SL}_n \times \mathrm{SL}_n$-invariant; the permanent's Hessian can be full rank at a zero. This is the cleanest, most elementary lower-bound technique in the survey, fully self-contained, and gives the quadratic bound. It is a "differential" invariant, not a representation-theoretic obstruction.
2. **Orbit-closure / GIT framing.** Reduce a complexity lower bound to a non-containment of orbit closures ($t^{n-m}\mathrm{per}_m \notin \Omega_n$), then study the vanishing ideal $I(\Omega_n)$ as a $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}$-representation. The separating function $R$ must come in modules of type $\lambda$.
3. **Symmetry-to-multiplicity reduction (Theorem 3.9 via Frobenius Theorem 3.15 and algebraic Peter-Weyl).** Multiplicities in the coordinate ring of the orbit equal dimensions of stabilizer-invariants; Schur-Weyl converts these into Kronecker coefficients of rectangular shape. This is the engine that turns "find an obstruction polynomial" into "find a vanishing Kronecker coefficient with positive plethysm."
4. **Monoid / saturation viewpoint (Theorem 3.19).** Obstructions must be genuine gaps of the finitely generated monoid $K_n$; saturation fills all admissible $\lambda$, so any obstruction-finding method must be sensitive to non-asymptotic structure. This is exactly the structural reason asymptotic methods cannot find occurrence obstructions, and it foreshadows the no-go.
5. **Combinatorial sandwich for Kronecker positivity (Lemmas 4.3-4.4, pyramids/3D-relations).** $p \le k \le t$ via discrete tomography; simplex-like marginals collapse the sandwich. This is the tool both for the NP-hardness reduction and for understanding why rectangular triples evade it.

## Relevance to this project
This survey is the authoritative primary source for the project's GCT strand (research direction 04) and directly substantiates LEARNINGS finding (7): "GCT hit its occurrence-obstruction no-go." The two no-go theorems in Section 5 (Ikenmeyer-Panova 2016, ref [32]; Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova 2016, ref [14]) are precisely the Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova result the project tracks as the 2016 obstruction no-go. The survey, written by a co-author of the no-go, gives the full chain: how the occurrence-obstruction program was set up (Mulmuley-Sohoni Theorem 3.9 reducing GCT-coefficients to rectangular Kronecker coefficients), why it looked promising (Example 3.13, an explicit obstruction found by computer), and exactly which step collapsed (plethysm positivity forcing Kronecker/GCT-coefficient positivity in the rectangular regime, Theorems 5.1 and 5.2).

On the barrier discipline: GCT was originally conceived as an explicit route to evade the natural-proofs barrier, since exhibiting a single vanishing-multiplicity obstruction is a one-shot, non-generic certificate rather than a large+constructive property. This survey documents that the occurrence-obstruction form of that route is now closed, but it carefully leaves open (Section 5.1) the multiplicity-comparison form, which is the surviving piece of the strand. Note that these are unconditional algebraic-geometry results, not barrier-theorem applications; the relativization / natural-proofs / algebrization checker does not directly score GCT obstruction-finding, since the no-go is a representation-theoretic theorem about plethysm versus Kronecker coefficients, not an oracle or pseudorandomness argument.

The tie to meta-complexity (the project's leading path) is indirect but real: Theorem 4.1 (Kronecker positivity is NP-hard) and Theorem 4.7 (asymptotic positivity in $\mathsf{NP} \cap \mathsf{coNP}$) are themselves complexity-of-complexity-invariant statements, the same flavor as the MCSP/meta-complexity questions in directions 01-02.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions and LEARNINGS findings:

- **Direction 04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions):** This source confirms the occurrence-obstruction sub-approach is closed (Theorems 5.1, 5.2) and pinpoints the surviving target: multiplicity comparison, $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda) <$ multiplicity of $\lambda$ in $\mathbb{C}[Z_{n,m}]$, would prove $Z_{n,m} \not\subseteq \Omega_n$ (Section 5.1). Concrete crux: understand the coordinate ring $\mathbb{C}[Z_{n,m}]$ of the padded-permanent orbit closure well enough to compute or lower-bound its multiplicities, possibly via Larsen-Pink (ref [39]).
- **LEARNINGS finding (7), GCT no-go:** fully sourced here, with the precise hypotheses ($\ell(\lambda) \le m^2$, $\lambda_1 \ge |\lambda|(1-m/n)$, $n > 3m^4$ resp. $n > m^{25}$). The no-go is asymptotic in $n$; it does not literally cover all $\lambda$, but it covers the entire shape regime forced by the Landsberg-Kadish constraints (Theorem 3.10), which is why it is decisive.
- **LEARNINGS finding (5), average-case is not worst-case, and finding (2), natural proofs are common:** ref [18] (Christandl-Doran-Walter, cited in Section 5.1) is flagged as showing comparing multiplicities by asymptotic methods cannot suffice for separation. This is a GCT-internal echo of the natural-proofs lesson: generic/asymptotic certificates are too common to separate.
- **Connection to meta-complexity (leading path) and directions 01-02:** Theorems 4.1 and 4.7 place Kronecker-positivity testing in the meta-complexity landscape ($\mathsf{NP}$-hard exact, $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ asymptotic). Open question worth flagging to SYNTHESIZER: is there a structural analogy between the "asymptotic positivity is easy, exact is hard" phenomenon for Kronecker coefficients and the MCSP gap-versus-exact phenomenon in meta-complexity?

Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes:
1. **Problem 3.14 (page 8, "major open problem"):** find families of obstructions for forms with few rows. The survey shows occurrence obstructions of rectangular-Kronecker type cannot supply this; it remains open whether any other obstruction family exists.
2. **Multiplicity-obstruction feasibility (Section 5.1):** can $\tilde{k}_n(\lambda)$ be shown strictly smaller than the padded-permanent orbit-closure multiplicity for some $\lambda$? This is the surviving GCT path and is unaddressed by any current result.
3. **Determinantal-complexity gap:** the bounds remain $m^2/2 \le \mathrm{dc}(m) \le 2^m - 1$ (Theorems 2.3, 2.2). No representation-theoretic lower bound on $\mathrm{dc}(m)$ has ever been obtained ("no lower bound on $\mathrm{dc}(m)$ has been obtained along these lines," page 9), despite obstructions existing for border rank of tensors (ref [13]).
4. **Asymptotic Kronecker positivity in $\mathsf{P}$?** Theorem 4.7 puts it in $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$; the authors conjecture polynomial time.
