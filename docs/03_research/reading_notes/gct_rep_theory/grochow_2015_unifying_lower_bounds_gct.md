## Summary
The paper makes a single unifying observation and develops its consequences: most known arithmetic circuit lower bounds, and most implications between lower bounds, already prove a representation-theoretic obstruction in the sense of GCT, namely a "separating module." A separating module is a $\mathrm{GL}_n$- (or $S_n$-, or $\mathrm{AGL}_n$-) invariant space of test polynomials that vanishes on every function in the easy class but not on some hard function (Definition 2.10). Grochow shows that the partial-derivatives method (Nisan-Wigderson), Razborov-Smolensky over $\mathbb{F}_p$, multilinear formula lower bounds (Raz), the degree bound (Strassen, Baur-Strassen), the connected-components / Betti-number technique (Ben-Or, Yao), depth-3 bounds over finite fields (Grigoriev-Karpinski), permanent versus determinant (Mignon-Ressayre, Landsberg-Manivel-Ressayre), matrix multiplication border rank (Burgisser-Ikenmeyer), the chasms at depths 3 and 4, matrix rigidity (Valiant), and tensor-rank-to-formula-size (Raz) all factor through separating modules with little extra work. The message is deliberately positive: where Razborov-Rudich, Razborov's bounded-arithmetic papers, and natural proofs give barriers, this paper gives a route, arguing (Section 3, Appendix B) that invariant properties are essentially necessary and that separating modules are by far the easiest type of property to construct.

## Key definitions and results

**Input polynomials versus test polynomials (Section 2.1).** The central bookkeeping distinction. An *input polynomial* is a polynomial in the variables $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ whose coefficients $a_e$ are constants; these are the objects we care about (determinant, permanent, etc.), and they are points in $\mathrm{Poly}^d(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$. A *test polynomial* is a polynomial whose variables are the coefficients $a_e$ of input polynomials and which defines a test for some property. The running example is $\mathrm{Poly}^2(x, y) = \{ax^2 + bxy + cy^2\}$ with test polynomial $b^2 - 4ac$ (the perfect-square test). For a test polynomial $p$ and input polynomial $f = \sum_e \alpha_e \mathbf{x}^e$, $p(f)$ means evaluating $p$ with each test variable $a_e$ set to $\alpha_e$.

**Property defined by polynomials (Definition 2.1).** A property $\Pi$ of input polynomials is *defined by test polynomials* if there is a set $p_1, \ldots, p_k$ such that $f$ has $\Pi$ iff $p_1(f) = \cdots = p_k(f) = 0$. Remark 2.2: this is exactly the notion of an algebraic (Zariski-closed) subset of the vector space $\mathrm{Poly}^d$, the algebro-geometric viewpoint on complexity (border rank and "infinitesimal approximation" in GCT are both algebro-geometric in this sense). Remark 2.3: by Hilbert's Basis Theorem, finitely many test polynomials always suffice.

**Linear-invariant property (Definition 2.4).** $\Pi$ is *linear-invariant* if for every $f$ and every $A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$, $f(\mathbf{x})$ has $\Pi$ iff $f(A\mathbf{x})$ has $\Pi$. Example 2.5: being a perfect square is linear-invariant. Example 2.6: the dimension of the space of all partial derivatives $\partial(f)$ of a homogeneous $f$ is a linear-invariant property, and it is defined by polynomials. This is the seed observation, attributed in spirit to Kayal [Kay11, Sec. 5.2], that several lower bounds use linear-invariant properties at their core; Grochow extends it to "defined by polynomials" and to most arithmetic circuit lower bounds.

**Test $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$-module (Definition 2.7) and the correspondence (Fact 2.8, proved in Appendix A as Fact A.1).** Each $B \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$ induces a linear map $\mathrm{Coeff}_B$ on coefficient vectors, hence on test polynomials. A *test $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$-module* is a finite-dimensional vector space $T$ of test polynomials closed under this action. Fact 2.8: there is a *many-to-one* correspondence between test $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$-modules and linear-invariant properties defined by polynomials. Over an algebraically closed field two test modules define the same property iff their generated ideals have the same radical (footnote 6, Nullstellensatz). The proof (Appendix A) shows $\Pi_{GT}$ is the unique maximum $G$-invariant subset of $\Pi$, so a $G$-invariant property equals the property of its $G$-orbit-closed defining test module.

**Separating module (Definition 2.10), the core object.** A *separating module* for $\mathcal{C}_{hard} \not\subseteq \mathcal{C}_{easy}$ is a test module $T$ that vanishes on every function in $\mathcal{C}_{easy}$ but does not vanish at some $f_{hard} \in \mathcal{C}_{hard}$. Footnote 7: separating modules are nearly equivalent to the "HWV (highest-weight-vector) obstructions" of Burgisser-Ikenmeyer [BI12]; Appendix C states the smallest $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$-module containing an HWV obstruction is a separating module, and every separating module contains an HWV obstruction.

**First worked example (Theorem 2.11, Proposition 2.12).** Theorem 2.11 = Nisan-Wigderson [NW97] Thm 0: over characteristic zero, any homogeneous depth-3 arithmetic circuit computing $e_{2d,n}$ (the elementary symmetric polynomial) has size $\Omega((n/4d)^d)$, exponential when $d = cn$, $0 < c \le 1/4$. The proof bounds $\dim \partial(C) \le s 2^d$ for size-$s$ homogeneous depth-3 circuits and $\dim \partial(e_{2d,n}) \ge \binom{n}{d}$. Proposition 2.12 turns this into a separating module: the property "$\dim \partial(f) \le r$" is the vanishing of the $(r+1) \times (r+1)$ minors of the partial-derivative matrix $M_f$ (rows indexed by derivative operators, columns by monomials; entries are linear in the coefficients $a_e$), so each minor is a degree-$(r+1)$ test polynomial. Their span $T(r)$ is a test $\mathrm{GL}_n$-module (because $M_{f(A\mathbf{x})}$ is related to $M_{f(\mathbf{x})}$ by left and right multiplication by matrices built from $A$, and minors of $BM_fC$ are linear combinations of minors of $M_f$), hence a separating module.

**Generalizations (Section 2.4).** Input objects may be matrices (matrix rigidity), semi-algebraic sets (degree bound, sorting), or circuits. Invariance may be permutation ($S_n$, the weakest natural requirement; multilinear formulas and rigidity are $S_n$-invariant but not $\mathrm{GL}_n$-invariant) or affine ($\mathrm{AGL}_n$). The analog of Fact 2.8 holds for all of these (Fact A.1).

**Necessity of invariant properties (Section 3.1).** If $\mathcal{C}_n$ is $G$-invariant, any property $\Pi$ proving a lower bound can be replaced by $\Pi^G$, the unique maximum $G$-invariant subset of $\Pi$, which proves the same lower bound. All complexity measures Grochow is aware of are permutation-invariant; many algebraic ones are linear- or affine-invariant. Notably $\mathsf{AC}^0[2]$ is $\mathrm{AGL}_n(\mathbb{F}_2)$-invariant but $\mathsf{AC}^0$ is *not* $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_2)$-invariant (parity is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear).

**Border complexity and constructibility (Section 3.2, 3.3).** Test polynomials prove bounds against the Zariski-closure $\overline{\mathcal{C}_n}$ ("border-$\mathcal{C}_n$"), which consists of all limits of functions in $\mathcal{C}_n$ (e.g. border rank for matrix multiplication, border determinantal complexity for permanent vs determinant). Open Question 3.3: does (quasi-)polynomial border determinantal complexity imply (quasi-)polynomial determinantal complexity, i.e. is $\overline{\mathsf{VP}_{ws}} \subseteq \mathsf{VQP}$ or $\overline{\mathsf{VQP}} = \mathsf{VQP}$? Theorem 3.5 (Chevalley's constructibility theorem) plus Corollary 3.6: every naturally occurring nonuniform complexity class $\mathcal{C}_n$ is Zariski-constructible (finite, or closed under projections with a complete problem, or defined by finitely many circuit shapes, or first-order definable over a Noetherian ring). Remark 3.7: orbit closures for $\mathrm{GL}_n$/$\mathrm{AGL}_n$ are the special constructible classes that current GCT studies; extending to all $G$-invariant classes is the generalization this paper offers.

**Relation to GCT and multiplicity obstructions (Section 4.1).** Two representations are *equivalent* (Definition 4.2) if related by a $\mathrm{GL}_n$-equivariant linear bijection that forgets the test-polynomial structure. Over $\mathbb{C}$, complete reducibility writes the space of all test polynomials as $\bigoplus_\lambda \bigoplus_{i=1}^{m_\lambda} T_{\lambda,i}$, with $\lambda$ ranging over irreducible equivalence classes (partitions) and $m_\lambda$ the *multiplicity*. **Multiplicity obstruction (Definition 4.3, Mulmuley-Sohoni [MS08]):** an irreducible class $\lambda$ with $m_\lambda(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{easy}}) > m_\lambda(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{hard}})$. An *occurrence* (geometric) obstruction further has $m_\lambda(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{easy}}) = m_\lambda$, i.e. every module equivalent to $\lambda$ vanishes on $\mathcal{C}_{easy}$. The existence of a multiplicity obstruction implies the existence of a separating module. The payoff: multiplicities can be studied by pure representation theory (irreducibles of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ and $S_n$ are classified by partitions) without reference to actual test polynomials.

**The upgrade ladder (Open Questions 4.4 to 4.7).** Q4.4: upgrade the proofs in this paper from separating modules to multiplicity (or stronger, occurrence) obstructions. Q4.5: determine the partition labels $\lambda$ of the separating modules in these lower bounds. Q4.6: upgrade to multiplicity obstructions whose label $\lambda_n$ at input length $n$ is computable in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time (Mulmuley's [Mul10] conjecture that this verification is in $\mathsf{P}$, and his claim that a poly-time verifier is a crucial first step toward proving obstructions exist unconditionally). Q4.7: the complexity of verifying that a given $\lambda_n$ is a multiplicity or occurrence obstruction.

**Explicitness and constructivity (Section 4.3).** Mulmuley [Mul10] and Williams [Wil13] argue constructive methods are necessary. The representation-theoretic viewpoint quantifies constructivity three ways: (1) degree and dimension of the test polynomials used (as in Razborov-Rudich and Williams, measured against truth-table size; for degree-$O(n)$ polynomials in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ variables the number of monomials is $2^{O(n \log n)}$, comparable to truth-table size); (2) the complexity of *verifying* a given test module is separating; (3) the bit-complexity of constructing from $0^n$ the partition $\lambda_n$ labeling an obstruction.

**Boolean lower bounds (Section 4.4, Observation 4.8).** For any Boolean circuit lower bound against a permutation-invariant complexity class there exists a separating $S_n$-module (Proof: the space of Boolean functions on $n$ variables is finite, so every property is defined by test polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_2$, then apply Fact A.1 for $S_n$). Williams's $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{ACC}^0$ [Wil11] is highlighted (page 16) as "an interesting crucible": it is a nonuniform lower bound against a permutation-invariant Boolean class, so a separating $S_n$-module exists, but the proof uses the nondeterministic time hierarchy in a seemingly crucial way. Extracting a *natural* separating module from Williams's proof is flagged as a first step toward extending the framework to uniform lower bounds.

**Lower bounds that do not (yet) fit (Section 4.5, 4.6).** Those that "most use heavily machine-based diagonalization": uniform permanent bounds [Allender, Allender-Gore, Koiran-Perifel], time-space tradeoffs for SAT [Fortnow, Williams, Buhrman-Fortnow-Thierauf], $\Sigma_2 \mathsf{P} \cap \Pi_2 \mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathsf{SIZE}(n^k)$ [Kannan], $\mathsf{MA}_{\mathsf{EXP}} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$, and the Jansen-Santhanam [JS12, JS13] $\mathbb{Z}$ hitting-set technique (which mixes algebraic and Boolean frameworks). Shannon's counting argument also resists a natural separating module. Section 4.6 records a mismatch: all current finite-field lower bounds depend on field *size*, not just characteristic, so none extends to $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$; this motivates Open Question 4.11 (is there a chasm at depth 3 over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$?).

**Section 5, the catalog of separating modules.** Stated in a compact "hard function / complexity class / lower bound / invariance / separating module" format. The technically central entries:
- *Nisan-Wigderson partial derivatives:* hard function $e_{n/4,n}$, homogeneous depth-3 char 0, bound $2^{\Omega(n)}$, $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$-invariant; separating module = the $(r+1)\times(r+1)$ minors of $M_f$ (Prop 2.12).
- *Permanent vs depth 4 (Gupta-Kamath-Kayal-Saptharishi [GKKS12]):* hard function $\mathrm{perm}_n$, $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ depth-4 with bottom fan-in $O(\sqrt n)$, bound $2^{\Omega(\sqrt n)}$, $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$; uses *shifted* partial derivatives $\partial^{=k}(f)_{\le \ell}$ (products of degree-$\le\ell$ polynomials with $k$-th derivatives), separating module = minors of the shifted-derivative matrix $\widetilde{M}_f$.
- *Multilinear formulas (Raz [Raz09]):* hard function $\det_n$ or $\mathrm{perm}_n$, syntactic multilinear formulas char 0, bound $\Omega(n^{\log n})$, *permutation*-invariant ($S_n$), not linear-invariant (Remark 5.2: multilinearity is not $\mathrm{GL}_n$-invariant). Raz uses $\dim \partial(f)$ under random restrictions $\rho$; for each restriction the $(r+1)$-minors of $M_{f(\rho(X))}$ give a test $S_n$-module $V_\rho$, and the separating module is the product $\prod_\rho V_\rho$ over the finitely many relevant restrictions (Fact 5.1: the product of test modules vanishes exactly on the union of their vanishing sets, so disjunctions of polynomial-defined invariant properties stay polynomial-defined).
- *Razborov-Smolensky:* hard function $\mathrm{MOD}_3$, $\mathsf{AC}^0[2]$, exponential, $\mathrm{AGL}_n(\mathbb{F}_2)$-invariant. Work in $\Omega_{\mathbb{F}_2}^{d,n} = \mathrm{Poly}^d_{\mathbb{F}_2}/\langle x_i^2 = x_i\rangle$ (functions, not formal polynomials). Key property (Smolensky [Smo87] Lem 2): there is a "good" $\Gamma \subseteq \mathbb{F}_2^n$ of size $\ge 2^n - 2^{n-r}$ on which $f$ agrees with a degree-$\le (2\lambda r)^k$ polynomial, true for any depth-$k$ parity-gate circuit with $r = o(n^{1/2k})$ but false for $\mathrm{MOD}_3$. For each $\Gamma$ the coefficients of monomials of degree $> d$ (mod the vanishing ideal $I_\Gamma$) give a test module $T_\Gamma$; the separating module is $\prod_\Gamma T_\Gamma$ over all large $\Gamma$.
- *Depth-3 over $\mathbb{F}_q$ (Grigoriev-Karpinski [GK98]):* a more complicated existential-quantifier property over subsets $S \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$, turned into a big product of test modules (the $n\times n$ minors of a linear system defining $I \cap \Lambda \neq 0$).
- *Permanent vs determinant (Mignon-Ressayre [MR04], Landsberg-Manivel-Ressayre [LMR10]):* bound $m \ge n^2/2$, border determinantal complexity $n^2/2$, $\mathrm{GL}_{m^2}(\mathbb{C})$. Key property = rank of the Hessian matrix $\mathrm{Hess}(f)$. The subtlety: the upper bound on $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{Hess}(\det)$ holds only where $\det(X) = 0$, not at all matrices; LMR10 find the polynomial equations vanishing exactly on pairs $(f,g)$ with $f \mid g$ (a classical question in algebraic geometry) and use them with $f = \det$, $g = $ minors of $\mathrm{Hess}(\det)$ to extract a genuine separating module.
- *Matrix multiplication (Burgisser-Ikenmeyer [BI12]):* $n\times n$ matrix mult, bilinear circuits char 0, border rank $\ge \frac32 n^2 - o(n^2)$, $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}^{\times 3}$-invariant; BI12 explicitly construct the separating modules (in fact occurrence obstructions). Plus the degree bound (Strassen), sorting / element distinctness via connected components (Ben-Or, with Hardt's Triviality Theorem giving affine-invariance), and $k$-equals via higher Betti numbers (Yao).

**Section 6, relations between lower bounds give relations between separating modules.**
- *Baur-Strassen [BS83]:* if computing $(\partial f/\partial x_1, \ldots, \partial f/\partial x_n)$ needs size $s$, computing $f$ needs size $s/3$. The map $\varphi: f \mapsto \langle \partial f/\partial x_1, \ldots\rangle$ to the Chow variety is $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})$-equivariant, so pulling back a test module $T$ gives the test module $\varphi_*(T) = \{t \circ \varphi : t \in T\}$.
- *Tensor rank to formula size (Raz [Raz10]):* if $t_n \in (\mathbb{F}^n)^{\otimes r(n)}$ has tensor rank $\ge n^{r(n)(1-o(1))}$ for $\omega(1) \le r(n) \le O(\log n / \log\log n)$, then the symmetrization $f_n$ needs super-polynomial formulas. The symmetrization embedding $\varphi: \mathrm{Poly}^r(\mathbf{x}) \hookrightarrow (\mathbb{F}^n)^{\otimes r}$ is $\mathrm{GL}_n$-equivariant, so a tensor-rank (border-rank) separating module pulls back to a formula-size one.
- *Chasm at depth 4 [AV08, Koi12]* and *depth 3 [GKKS13]:* polynomial-size circuits are contained in depth-4 size $2^{O(\sqrt n \log^2 n)}$ (resp. depth-3 size $2^{O(\sqrt n \log^{3/2} n)}$, only char 0 or large characteristic, by Fischer's trick dividing by $2^{d-1} d!$), so a separating module for the shallow class is one for the deep class.
- *Matrix rigidity to linear circuits (Valiant [Val77]):* if $A_n$ has rigidity $R_{A_n}(n/2) \ge \Omega(n^{1+\varepsilon})$, then $x \mapsto A_n x$ has no simultaneously size-$O(n)$ depth-$O(\log n)$ linear circuit. Rigidity is only *permutation*-invariant ($S_n \times S_n$); non-rigid matrices do not form an algebraic set, but the best rigidity results [KLPS09] use the algebro-geometric (test-polynomial) approach.

**Appendix B, why separating modules are the easiest property.** Heuristic complexity-of-verification argument. To prove $f_{hard,n} \notin \mathcal{C}_n$ with $\overline{\mathcal{C}_n} = \mathcal{A}_n$ and boundary $\mathcal{B}_n$, three approaches: (1) find a single test polynomial $t$ vanishing on $\overline{\mathcal{C}_n}$ but not at $f_{hard}$; (2) show $f_{hard} \in \mathcal{B}_n$ (needs the defining set of $\mathcal{B}_n$, exponentially many test polynomials in the examples except matrix mult / rigidity); (3) find a closed $\mathcal{D}_n \ni f_{hard}$ disjoint from $\mathcal{C}_n$. Verification complexities: (1) is essentially $\mathsf{coRP}$ (generate random points of $\mathcal{C}_n$ via the parametrizing map, evaluate $t$); (2) is at least exponential time; (3) reduces to deciding whether a variety is empty, the Hilbert Nullstellensatz problem $\mathsf{HN}$, which is $\mathsf{NP}$-hard in general. Hence approach (1), the separating-module / single-test-polynomial route, is the only one that looks feasible by brute-force search.

## Techniques and proof ideas
The reusable engine is a three-step recipe a BUILDER can apply to any candidate lower bound:
1. **Identify the underlying linear-invariant property.** Express the lower-bound argument as "every easy function has measure $\mu(f) \le r$, some hard function has $\mu(f) > r$," where $\mu$ is a dimension, rank, degree, Betti number, or connected-component count.
2. **Realize the property as the vanishing of minors / coefficients.** Bound-on-rank becomes vanishing of $(r+1) \times (r+1)$ minors of a matrix whose entries are linear in the coefficients $a_e$ of the input polynomial (the partial-derivative matrix $M_f$, the shifted-derivative matrix $\widetilde{M}_f$, the Hessian $\mathrm{Hess}(f)$, etc.). Bound-on-degree becomes vanishing of high-degree coefficients modulo a vanishing ideal. Each such minor or coefficient is a *test polynomial*.
3. **Close under the symmetry group and take products for disjunctions.** Verify the span is a test $\mathrm{GL}_n$- (or $S_n$-, $\mathrm{AGL}_n$-) module using that $M_{f(A\mathbf{x})} = B M_f C$ and minors of $BM_fC$ are linear combinations of minors of $M_f$ (Prop 2.12 / Example 2.6). For properties that are disjunctions over restrictions or subsets (Raz multilinear, Razborov-Smolensky, Grigoriev-Karpinski), use Fact 5.1: the product $V \cdot W$ (linear span of pairwise products) of two test modules is a test module whose vanishing set is the union of the two.

Two pull-back lemmas handle implications between lower bounds: an equivariant map $\varphi$ between input spaces (differentiation, symmetrization, padding to depth) pulls a separating module $T$ on the target back to $\varphi_*(T) = \{t \circ \varphi : t \in T\}$ on the source (Section 6, Baur-Strassen and tensor-rank derivations).

The deeper representation-theoretic technique (Section 4.1) replaces explicit test polynomials with partitions: decompose the test-polynomial space into isotypic components $\bigoplus_\lambda \bigoplus_i T_{\lambda,i}$, compare multiplicities $m_\lambda(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{easy}})$ versus $m_\lambda(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{hard}})$, and search for an occurrence or multiplicity obstruction by representation-theoretic computation (Kronecker / plethysm coefficients) rather than direct minor computation.

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the conceptual backbone of the GCT strand (direction 04) and the single most useful map of how the *classical* lower-bound measures sit inside the representation-theoretic frame. For the dossier's algebrization analysis it is load-bearing in a specific way.

**Which measures are trace/rank functionals (hence algebrize).** The paper makes precise, measure by measure, that every separating module in the catalog is the *vanishing locus of minors* of some matrix linear in the input coefficients, or of high-degree polynomial coefficients. That is exactly the family the dossier's missing-object analysis flags as algebrizing: dimension-of-partial-derivatives (NW), shifted-derivative rank (GKKS depth-4), Hessian rank (Mignon-Ressayre, LMR10), tensor/border rank (matrix mult), Smolensky low-degree approximation, projective degree / Hilbert polynomial (Strassen), and Betti numbers (Yao). All of these are *rank or rank-derived* invariants of explicit matrices, and rank-type and trace-type functionals are precisely the quantities the dossier identified as algebrizing (every rational trace / rank / volume invariant algebrizes). The matrix-rigidity entry (Section 6) is the sharpest illustration: rigidity is the minimum number of entries to change to drop the rank, manifestly a rank functional, manifestly algebrizing.

**Where a non-algebrizing GCT invariant would have to differ.** The multiplicity obstruction (Definition 4.3) is the one quantity in this paper that is *not* a rank of an explicit linear-in-coefficients matrix. A multiplicity $m_\lambda$ is the number of copies of an irreducible $\mathrm{GL}_n$-representation $\lambda$ inside a coordinate ring, computed by plethysm / Kronecker arithmetic over the rationals or integers. This is the natural place to look for the dossier's "missing object" (a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant). Two caveats the paper itself supplies sharpen the search:
- Footnote 12 records Eric Allender's observation that all the lower bounds here use Razborov-Rudich-natural properties, and Grochow's belief (citing his thesis [Gro12, Sec. 3.4.3]) that GCT over $\mathbb{C}$ "philosophically avoids" the natural-proofs barrier, while conceding there is "no known algebraic analog of the Razborov-Rudich barrier" and that the algebraic properties here "seem like they ought to fulfill the requirements of such an analog, were it to exist." This is a direct, honest flag from the source that the separating modules in the catalog are natural-proofs-flavored and that the escape, if any, is not yet a theorem.
- The Burgisser-Ikenmeyer-Panova 2016 no-go (the dossier's GCT occurrence-obstruction obstacle, LEARNINGS finding 7) lands precisely on the *occurrence* obstructions that this paper recommends pursuing in Open Questions 4.4 to 4.6 (BI12's matrix-multiplication occurrence obstructions are cited approvingly here as the model to extend). So this 2015 paper sits chronologically and logically *upstream* of that no-go: it is the optimistic program statement that the 2016 result later constrained for permanent vs determinant.

**Barrier-discipline reading.** Run through the three-barrier checker informally, the separating-module *technique class* this paper describes (rank/minor/degree test polynomials over a fixed field, closed under the symmetry group) reads as: relativization barrier likely evaded (these are algebraic, not oracle-relative, arguments, and Williams [Wil11] is cited as the existence proof that such Boolean bounds need not relativize); natural-proofs barrier *not* clearly evaded (footnote 12's own admission; the properties are large by Shannon-type counting and constructive as minor evaluations); algebrization barrier *not* evaded for the rank/trace measures (they are exactly the algebrizing functionals). The candidate that *could* evade all three is the multiplicity obstruction, but only if its label $\lambda_n$ is poly-time constructible (Open Question 4.6) and only if a multiplicity gap survives the BIP no-go, neither of which this paper establishes. This is consistent with LEARNINGS finding 9 (the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant) and with the dossier's hinge: rank-based polynomial-method invariants die where MAJORITY's approximate degree is $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, and the GCT analog of "go beyond rank" is "go beyond occurrence to multiplicity, and find an integer/torsion-valued invariant rather than a rational rank."

## What this enables / what remains open

**Maps to research directions.**
- *Direction 04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions).* This is the primary deposit. It gives the precise taxonomy (separating module $\subseteq$ multiplicity obstruction $\subseteq$ occurrence obstruction, Definition 4.3) and the upgrade ladder (Open Questions 4.4 to 4.7) that direction 04 should track. It also supplies the catalog of which classical bounds already give separating modules, so direction 04 can ask "which partition $\lambda$ labels the NW / Hessian / Smolensky separating module?" (Open Question 4.5) as a concrete, possibly-feasible task.
- *Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly).* The Williams crucible (page 16): a separating $S_n$-module for $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{ACC}^0$ exists by Observation 4.8, but extracting a *natural* (in the plain-English, not Razborov-Rudich, sense) one is open. This is a direct bridge between the GCT strand and the Williams-spine leading path (LEARNINGS finding 8).
- *Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion, the MCSP crux).* Footnote 12 is the explicit hook: GCT's claim to evade natural proofs is, by the author's own statement, philosophical and lacks an algebraic analog of the barrier theorem. Direction 02 should treat "is there an algebraic Razborov-Rudich barrier, and do separating modules / multiplicity obstructions violate its largeness or constructivity clause?" as a named open problem.
- *Direction 03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP).* Less direct, but Appendix B's reduction of the "find a single separating test polynomial" verification problem to $\mathsf{coRP}$, and the $\mathcal{D}_n$ approach to $\mathsf{HN}$ ($\mathsf{NP}$-hard), connects the constructivity question to proof-system / Nullstellensatz-certificate language.

**Maps to LEARNINGS findings.**
- *Finding 7 (GCT hit its occurrence-obstruction no-go):* this paper is the pre-no-go program statement; reading it clarifies that the no-go targeted exactly the occurrence obstructions recommended in Open Questions 4.4 to 4.6, and that the *multiplicity* (non-occurrence) obstructions are the surviving hope.
- *Finding 9 (the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant):* the paper's catalog is the negative half of the evidence, it shows the existing measures are all rank/degree (algebrizing) functionals, so the missing object must be of a different type. Multiplicity $m_\lambda$ is the paper's own candidate for a different type.
- *Finding 6 (Williams is the existence proof barriers are passable):* the Williams crucible discussion (page 16) ties the GCT frame to that existence proof.

**Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes.**
1. Open Question 4.5 (determine the partition $\lambda$ of each catalog separating module) is the most actionable; resolving it for the Nisan-Wigderson and Mignon-Ressayre modules would test whether their labels are even candidate obstruction labels.
2. Open Question 4.6 / Mulmuley's poly-time-verifiability conjecture: is the label $\lambda_n$ of an obstruction at input length $n$ constructible in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time? This is the constructivity crux for the GCT strand and the analog of the MCSP constructivity crux (finding 3) in the algebraic world.
