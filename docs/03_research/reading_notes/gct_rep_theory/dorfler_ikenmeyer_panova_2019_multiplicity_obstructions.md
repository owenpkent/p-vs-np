## Summary
The paper exhibits, for the first time, a pair of group varieties where representation-theoretic *multiplicities* give a separation that representation-theoretic *occurrences* provably cannot. The setting is deliberately simple and natural: the Chow variety $\mathsf{Ch}^n_m$ (products of $n$ homogeneous linear forms in $m$ variables) versus the higher secant variety $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}$ of the Veronese (polynomials of border Waring rank at most $k$). They prove that the partition $\lambda = (n^2 - 2, n, 2)$ is a multiplicity obstruction showing $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k} \not\subseteq \mathsf{Ch}^n_m$ for $m \ge 3$, $n \ge 2$, $k = d = n+1$, and in two finite settings they show a strictly stronger separation that no occurrence obstruction can witness for any $k$. As a side result they prove a generalization of Hermite reciprocity that confirms Foulkes' conjecture for a new infinite family of 3-partition cases (Theorem 3.5 / Corollary 4.8). The separation achieved is described by the authors as "extremely modest," which they read as evidence that the multiplicity-over-occurrence advantage is generic rather than exceptional.

## Key definitions and results

### The two varieties being compared (Section 2, page 2)
Work in $\mathbb{A}^n_m := \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_m]_n$, the space of homogeneous degree-$n$ polynomials in $m$ variables, with $V := \mathbb{A}^1_m$ the degree-1 forms. The two subvarieties:
- **Chow variety:** $\mathsf{Ch}^n_m := \{\ell_1 \cdots \ell_n \mid \ell_i \in V\} \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n_m$, the products of $n$ linear forms (already Zariski closed; see Landsberg [Lan11], section 8.6).
- **Higher secant of the Veronese (bounded border Waring rank):** $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k} := \overline{\{\ell_1^n + \cdots + \ell_k^n \mid \ell_i \in V\}}$, the closure of sums of $k$ $n$-th powers. These are exactly the polynomials of border Waring rank at most $k$ (Landsberg [Lan11], section 5.4).

Both are closed under the natural $\mathsf{GL}_m$ action $g(v^n) := (gv)^n$ extended linearly, so both are $\mathsf{GL}_m$-varieties. The coordinate ring $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{A}^n_m]$ carries the dual ("pullback") action $(gf)(h) := f(g^{-1}h)$, is graded, and each graded piece $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{A}^n_m]_d$ is a finite-dimensional polynomial $\mathsf{GL}_m$-representation (Section 2, page 2).

### Multiplicities, the plethysm coefficient, and the two obstruction notions (Section 2, page 3)
$\mathsf{GL}_m$ is linearly reductive, so every representation $W$ decomposes into irreducibles $\{\lambda\}_m$ indexed by $m$-partitions $\lambda$, with well-defined multiplicities $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(W)$. The central quantity is the **plethysm coefficient**
$$a_\lambda(d[n]) := \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{A}^n_m]_d),$$
the object of Foulkes' conjecture and Problem 9 of Stanley's list [Sta00]. Foulkes' conjecture (equation 2.1) states $a_\lambda(n[d]) \le a_\lambda(d[n])$ for all $d \ge n$; it is known for 2-partitions with equality (Hermite reciprocity [Her54]), and this paper proves new 3-partition cases.

When $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k} \subseteq \mathsf{Ch}^n_m$, restriction of functions gives a $\mathsf{GL}_m$-equivariant surjection $\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d$, and Schur's lemma forces the key inequality (equation 2.2):
$$\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) \ge \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d).$$
The two obstruction notions follow:
- **Multiplicity obstruction:** a $\lambda$ that *violates* equation 2.2, i.e. $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) < \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d)$. This proves non-containment.
- **Occurrence obstruction:** the special case where additionally $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) = 0$ (the irreducible occurs in the second ring but not the first). This is the original, structurally weaker, Mulmuley-Sohoni plan.

The motivation for the paper is stated explicitly (Section 1, page 1, and page 2): Scott Aaronson raised whether a setting exists where multiplicity obstructions are provably more powerful than occurrence obstructions, and this paper gives the first example. The authors note that the relevant multiplicities are generally #P-hard to compute (citing [Nar06, Bl08, BOR09]), so the existence of a polynomial-time algorithm to compute them would imply $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{NP}$.

### Main theorem (Theorem 2.3, page 4)
1. For $m \ge 3$, $n \ge 2$, $k = d = n+1$, and $\lambda = (n^2 - 2, n, 2)$: one has $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) < \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d)$, so $\lambda$ is a multiplicity obstruction proving $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k} \not\subseteq \mathsf{Ch}^n_m$.
2. In two finite settings a strictly stronger separation holds:
   - (a) $k = 4$, $n = 6$, $m = 3$, $d = 7$, $\lambda = (n^2 - 2, n, 2) = (34, 6, 2)$: $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^6_3]_7) = 7 < 8 = \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^6_{3,4}]_7)$.
   - (b) $k = 4$, $n = 7$, $m = 4$, $d = 8$, $\lambda = (47, 7, 2)$: $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^7_4]_8) < 11 = \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^7_{4,4}]_8)$.
   - Crucially, separations (a) and (b) **cannot be achieved by any occurrence obstruction, for any $k$**: in these settings, every $m$-partition $\mu$ with $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0$ also has $\mathrm{mult}_\mu(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_{d'}) > 0$. So in (a) and (b) the multiplicity obstruction is provably strictly stronger.

Remark 2.4 (page 4) notes that $(n^2 - 2, n, 2)$ is the type of one of Brill's classical set-theoretic equations for $\mathsf{Ch}^n_m$ (citing [Gua18]).

### The two ingredients of the main-theorem proof (Section 3)
**Equality with the plethysm coefficient on the power side (equation 3.1, page 4):** $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d) = a_\lambda(d[n])$ for the relevant parameters. Finite cases verified with LiE (Proposition 3.2): $\mathrm{mult}_{(34,6,2)}(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^6_{3,4}]_7) = 8 = a_{(34,6,2)}(7[6])$ and $\mathrm{mult}_{(47,7,2)}(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^7_{3,4}]_8) = 11 = a_{(47,7,2)}(8[7])$. General case: Proposition 3.3 (from BIP19 [BIP19], Prop. 3.2): if $\lambda$ is an $m$-partition of $dn$ and $k \ge d$, then $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,k}]_d) = a_\lambda(d[n])$.

**Upper bound on the Chow side (Lemma 3.4, page 5):** for any $m$-partition $\lambda$ and $n \ge m$, $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) \le a_\lambda(n[d])$. The proof identifies $\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]$ with the coordinate ring of the closure of a $\mathsf{GL}_n$ orbit of $x_1 \cdots x_n$, then uses geometric invariant theory: $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{GL}_n(x_1 \cdots x_n)]_d) = \mathrm{mult}_{\lambda^*}(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{GL}_n]^H_d)$ where $H$ is the stabilizer (a torus extended by $\mathfrak{S}_n$), and the algebraic Peter-Weyl theorem gives $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{GL}_n]^H_d) = \dim\{\lambda\}^H = a_\lambda(n[d])$.

**The sharp gap (Theorem 3.5, page 5):** for $\lambda = (n^2 - 2, n, 2)$,
$$a_{(n^2-2,n,2)}(n+1[n]) = 1 + a_{(n^2-2,n,2)}(n[n+1]).$$
Combining: $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Pow}]) = a_\lambda(d[n]) = a_\lambda((n+1)[n])$ exceeds the Chow upper bound $a_\lambda(n[d]) = a_\lambda(n[n+1]) \ge \mathrm{mult}_\lambda(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}])$ by exactly $1$. This is the entire separation: a gap of $1$ in multiplicity, which is why the authors call it "extremely modest." Theorem 3.5 is itself a corollary of the more general plethysm computation in Corollary 4.8.

### The "no occurrence obstructions" half (Section 3, pages 5 to 7)
To prove part (2) of Theorem 2.3, they show that in the finite settings every $\mu$ with $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0$ already occurs in the Chow ring (so no $\mu$ can be an occurrence obstruction). For $n=6, m=3$ the precise claim (equation 3.6, page 5) is $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0 \Rightarrow \mathrm{mult}_\mu(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) > 0$, independent of $k$. The argument:
- Classify which $\mu$ have $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0$. Lemma 3.7 (page 5): if $\bar\lambda := (\lambda_2, \lambda_3, \dots)$ (the partition without its first row) is in $\{(3,3),(3,1),(2,1),(1,1),(1)\}$ then $a_\lambda(d[n]) = 0$ (proved by finite calculation, extending IP17 [IP17] Thm 1.10(a)).
- The set of positive $\mu$ forms a finitely generated semigroup (equation 3.8, from BII18 [BI18] Prop. 21.2.6): $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0$ and $a_\nu(d'[n]) > 0$ imply $a_{\mu+\nu}((d+d')[n]) > 0$. Proposition 3.9 (page 6) gives the explicit generating set $X$ of about 110 listed 3-partitions for $a_\mu(d[6]) > 0$, certified by LiE computation (the case $d = 18$ for $\mu = (45,45)$ used Stanley's formula [Sta08, Cor. 4.2.8] because LiE ran out of memory).
- Proposition 3.10 (page 6): every 3-partition $\lambda$ of $6d$ with $d \ge 15$ and $\bar\lambda$ outside the bad set is a sum of generators from $X$ (proved by direct computation for $15 \le d \le 17$ and induction above, using a pigeonhole on column counts $c_1, c_2, c_3$).
- The multiplicity side semigroup property (equation 3.11) is analogous. Proposition 3.12 (page 7): a length-at-most-2 partition $\mu$ with $a_\mu(d[n]) > 0$ has $\mathrm{mult}_\mu(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_2]_d) > 0$, by an inheritance result plus the fundamental theorem of algebra (this is essentially how Hermite reciprocity is reproven).
- For $n=7, m=4$ (Lemma 3.13, Proposition 3.14, page 7) the analogous semigroup of positive 4-partitions has **948 generators** (listed in Proposition 7.1). They verified $\mathrm{mult}_\mu > 0$ for the generators by LiE; for $(49,49)$ and $(24,24,23,23)$ separately. For the harder finite case $n=7, k=d=8, m=5$ they hit the computational wall: 5016 generating partitions, verified for 5000 of them, but the remaining ones exhausted 200 GB of RAM without resolving (page 7). So part (2)(b) is the limit of what their implementation could certify.

### Some occurrence obstructions do appear when parameters degenerate (Proposition 3.15, page 7)
As $n$ approaches $m$, multiplicity obstructions can degenerate into occurrence obstructions. Table on page 7 lists explicit occurrence obstructions $\lambda$ proving $\mathsf{Pow}^n_{m,d} \not\subseteq \mathsf{Ch}^n_m$ with $a_\lambda(d[n]) > 0$ but $a_\lambda(n[d]) = 0$: e.g. $(m,n) = (3,2), \lambda = (2,2,2)$; $(3,3), (7,3,2)$; $(3,4), (11,9,8)$; $(3,5),(12,9,9)$; $(4,6),(14,14,13,13)$ with $a_\lambda(d[n]) = 11$. This delimits exactly when the occurrence/multiplicity distinction collapses.

### Plethysm inequalities and the Foulkes/Hermite side result (Section 4, pages 8 to 12)
Working in the ring of symmetric functions $\Lambda$, they compute the plethysm coefficient as an inner product $a_\lambda(d[n]) = \langle s_\lambda, h_d[h_n]\rangle$ (equation 4.1), expand $s_\lambda$ via Jacobi-Trudi (equation 4.2), and reduce to monomial-expansion counts $c_\nu(d,n) = \#\{(b) : |b| = d, \sum_i b_i \alpha^i = \nu\}$ (equation 4.3, 4.4). Proposition 4.5 (page 9) gives generating-function identities for $c_{(L,k,j)}$, $j \le 2$, in terms of $q$-binomial coefficients (the generating function $p_r(a,b)$ counting partitions in an $a \times b$ box). Proposition 4.6 (page 11) gives a closed $q$-form for $a_{(L,r,2)}(d[n])$. Proposition 4.7 (page 11) gives the difference $a_{(L,r,2)}(d[n]) - a_{(L,r,2)}(n[d])$ as a $q$-binomial expression. Corollary 4.8 (page 12), which is "Theorem 3.5" restated, computes for $d = n+1, \lambda = (n^2 + n - 2 - r, r, 2)$:
$$a_\lambda((n+1)[n]) - a_\lambda(n[n+1]) = \begin{cases} 0 & r < n \\ 1 & r = n \\ > 0 & r > n, n \ge 7 \end{cases}$$
with a single exceptional zero at $n = 8, r = 35$ where $a_{(35,35,2)}(9[8]) = a_{(35,35,2)}(8[9])$ (corresponding to $p_{26}(9,6) = 227 = p_{27}(9,6)$, a known coincidence of $q$-binomial coefficients). The strict positivity for $r > n$ uses Kronecker-coefficient positivity from Pak-Panova [PP13]. This is the new infinite family of Foulkes-conjecture confirmations (a slight generalization of Hermite reciprocity).

### The computational engine (Section 5, pages 12 to 15)
To certify $\mathrm{mult}_\mu(\mathbb{C}[\mathsf{Ch}^n_m]_d) > 0$ (Proposition 5.1), they use highest weight vectors (HWV). Via Schur-Weyl duality (equation 5.2) $\mathrm{mult}_\lambda(W) = \dim \mathsf{HWV}_\lambda(W)$ (equation 5.3), and a chain of $\mathsf{GL}_m$-equivariant surjections (equation 5.4) reduces positivity to finding a $\pi \in \mathfrak{S}_{dn}$ and a point $p \in \mathsf{Ch}^n_m$ with a tensor contraction $(p^{\otimes d})\pi v_\lambda$ nonzero (equations 5.5 to 5.7). The contraction equals a product of determinants of matrices of size $\le m$ (efficiently computable), summed over proper placements (equation 5.8). The algorithmic contribution is a dynamic-programming / breadth-first construction of placements column by column (equations 5.9, 5.10) with an equivalence-class memoization that drastically cuts time at the cost of large memory, and randomization over semistandard tableaux when full enumeration is infeasible. This refines and speeds up the method of Cheung-Ikenmeyer-Mkrtchyan [CIM16] (the "symmetrizing tableaux / 5th case of Foulkes" paper). Computations done over a finite field to avoid numerical issues. Sections 6, 7, 8 are the certifying tableau data.

## Techniques and proof ideas
A builder working the multiplicity-obstruction coordinate would reuse the following kit:
1. **The containment-to-multiplicity-inequality reduction (equation 2.2):** any orbit-closure containment of $\mathsf{GL}$-varieties forces a multiplicity inequality direction via the equivariant restriction surjection and Schur's lemma. Violating it certifies non-containment. This is the GCT separation template, here instantiated for a tractable pair (Chow vs power sums) rather than permanent vs determinant.
2. **Pin one side to a known coefficient and bound the other (Prop 3.3 vs Lemma 3.4):** the power side equals the plethysm coefficient $a_\lambda(d[n])$ exactly; the Chow side is upper bounded by $a_\lambda(n[d])$ via GIT and the algebraic Peter-Weyl theorem. The separation reduces to a Foulkes-type inequality $a_\lambda(d[n]) > a_\lambda(n[d])$, which makes the whole problem about plethysm asymmetry.
3. **Symmetric-function / $q$-binomial computation of plethysm differences (Section 4):** Jacobi-Trudi plus monomial-expansion counting plus $q$-binomial generating functions yields a closed form for $a_{(L,r,2)}(d[n]) - a_{(L,r,2)}(n[d])$. This is the reusable analytic engine for 3-row partitions and is what powers the infinite family (Theorem 2.3 part 1).
4. **Semigroup generators + inductive coverage to rule out occurrence obstructions (Prop 3.9, 3.10):** classify all positive $\mu$ by a finite generating set of the positivity semigroup, then show positivity propagates to the Chow ring on generators, then cover all $\mu$ by sums of generators. This is the mechanism for proving the *negative* statement "no occurrence obstruction exists here."
5. **HWV positivity via tensor contraction = product of small determinants (Section 5):** the practical way to certify $\mathrm{mult}_\mu > 0$ without full character computation, with a dynamic-programming placement enumeration and equivalence memoization.

## Relevance to this project
This is the keystone paper for the GCT strand (Architecture 4 in the atlas) after the BIP 2016 no-go. The atlas (docs/research_atlas/README.md, Architecture 4) records the exact situation this paper addresses: occurrence obstructions are dead for permanent-vs-determinant (BIP 2016), and "multiplicity obstructions remain open but require strictly harder representation-theoretic computations." Dorfler-Ikenmeyer-Panova give the first proof that the harder computation is worth it: there is a concrete, natural geometric setting (Chow variety vs higher secant of the Veronese) where multiplicities separate and occurrences provably cannot, for any $k$ (Theorem 2.3 part 2). This is precisely the "multiplicity-obstruction sign coordinate" the dossier flags as the live successor: a positive existence result that the multiplicity refinement has strictly more separating power than the occurrence refinement that BIP killed.

Three caveats that keep this honest, all stated by the authors:
- The setting is **not** permanent vs determinant. It is a deliberately simplified pair chosen so the multiplicities are actually computable. The relevance is methodological: it shows the multiplicity-over-occurrence gap is a real phenomenon, not that GCT now separates $\mathsf{VP}$ from $\mathsf{VNP}$.
- The separation is a multiplicity gap of exactly $1$ (Theorem 3.5). The authors call it "extremely modest" and read the modesty as evidence the behavior is generic ("expected in many more cases"), not as a weakness of the method.
- BIP19 and IP17 rule out occurrence obstructions for the padded-polynomial families relevant to circuit lower bounds only in ranges where they would give very strong new lower bounds, so the authors expect multiplicity obstructions to be hard to find there too (page 4). The good news here is confined to the easy unpadded Chow/power setting.

On the barrier discipline: GCT is the one architecture in the program whose primary obstruction is not one of the three classical barriers but an architecture-specific no-go (the BIP occurrence no-go). This paper does not engage relativization, natural proofs, or algebrization directly; its obstruction is the internal GCT question of whether the multiplicity refinement has separating power. So for the barrier scorecard the relevant entry is the GCT-internal no-go, and this paper is a positive result against that no-go (in a toy setting). It does not bear on whether a GCT multiplicity argument would be natural or would algebrize; those remain open and downstream.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions:
- **04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions):** This is the central source. It converts "multiplicity obstructions might be stronger than occurrence obstructions" from a hope into a theorem in a concrete setting. A builder pursuing the GCT strand should treat the Chow-vs-power-sum pair as the canonical worked example and the $a_\lambda(d[n]) > a_\lambda(n[d])$ Foulkes asymmetry as the engine of separation. The $q$-binomial plethysm machinery of Section 4 is directly reusable for any 3-row partition computation.
- **01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** indirect. GCT targets the algebraic analog $\mathsf{VP}$ vs $\mathsf{VNP}$. This paper does not advance the Boolean circuit lower bounds, but it keeps the algebraic-geometric branch alive as a parallel attack.
- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, MCSP crux):** not directly addressed. GCT obstructions are not obviously natural properties; whether a successful multiplicity obstruction would be checkable/large in the Razborov-Rudich sense is open and not touched here.
- **03 (proof complexity):** not relevant.

Mapping to LEARNINGS findings:
- Directly sharpens **finding (7) (GCT hit its occurrence-obstruction no-go):** this paper is the first concrete demonstration that the no-go does not extend to multiplicity obstructions. Finding (7) should be annotated: the no-go is specific to occurrences; the multiplicity refinement provably escapes it in at least one natural setting. The dead branch (occurrences) is confirmed, and the live branch (multiplicities) now has a proof-of-concept rather than only a hope.
- Consistent with the project stance that negative results are coordinates: BIP closed occurrences, and this paper shows the closure does not propagate to the finer invariant, which is exactly the "narrow the search" reading.

Concrete open questions and cruxes this source exposes:
1. **Scale-up to padded polynomials.** The authors expect multiplicity obstructions to be hard to find for the padded-polynomial families relevant to permanent-vs-determinant (page 4), because IP17/BIP19 rule out occurrence obstructions there only in ranges that would imply very strong lower bounds. Crux: is there any setting closer to permanent-vs-determinant where the multiplicity gap is computable and positive? This paper does not reach it.
2. **The computational wall.** The $n=7, k=d=8, m=5$ case could not be certified (200 GB RAM insufficient, 5000 of 5016 generators verified, page 7). The HWV / determinant-product method of Section 5 is the current state of the art; a genuinely better algorithm for certifying $\mathrm{mult}_\mu > 0$ (or computing plethysm/Kronecker coefficients) is the practical bottleneck. Recall the authors' own note that polynomial-time computation of these multiplicities would imply $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{NP}$ (page 1), so the bottleneck is fundamental, not merely engineering.
3. **Is the gap always $1$, or can it grow?** Theorem 3.5 gives a gap of exactly $1$ for $r = n$ and $> 0$ (unquantified) for $r > n$. Whether larger, more robust multiplicity gaps exist in natural settings (which would be more convincing evidence the method scales) is open.
4. **Foulkes conjecture for 3-partitions in general.** Corollary 4.8 proves a new infinite family but only for partitions of the shape $(L, r, 2)$ at $d = n+1$. The general 3-partition Foulkes case remains open, and the $q$-binomial method here is a partial tool toward it.
5. **Barrier status of a hypothetical scaled-up multiplicity obstruction.** Not addressed by this paper and worth flagging to ADVERSARY: even if multiplicity obstructions eventually separate permanent from determinant, whether such an argument would be natural (Razborov-Rudich) or would algebrize is undetermined. The toy result here does not certify barrier-evasion; it only defeats the GCT-internal occurrence no-go.
