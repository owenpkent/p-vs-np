# Reading notes: Threshold Values of Random K-SAT from the Cavity Method (Mertens, Mezard, Zecchina, 2006)

- **Source:** `references/stat_physics/mertens_mezard_zecchina_2006_ksat_thresholds.pdf`
- **Type / venue:** Journal article, Random Structures and Algorithms (the PDF is the accepted preprint, "To appear in Random Structures and Algorithms," dated 24 February 2005; published 2006).
- **Dossier strand:** Statistical physics of computation. This is the primary quantitative source for the random K-SAT satisfiability threshold $\alpha_c(K)$ predicted by the one-step replica-symmetry-breaking (1RSB) cavity method, including the headline value $\alpha_c(3) \simeq 4.267$ and the large-$K$ expansion in powers of $2^{-K}$.

## Summary
The paper reformulates the 1RSB cavity computation of random K-SAT thresholds (previously done for $K=3$ in references [23,24]) as a pure statistical analysis of survey-propagation (SP) message-passing equations on the factor graph, assuming no physics background. It derives the satisfiability threshold $\alpha_c(K)$ for general $K \ge 3$, gives a population-dynamics numerical solution producing $\alpha_c(3) \simeq 4.267$ (refined to $4.26675 \pm 0.00015$) and a table of values up to $K=10$, and develops an analytic large-$K$ expansion of $\alpha_c(K)$ in the small parameter $\varepsilon = 2^{-K}$ whose leading term reproduces the best known rigorous upper bound. It also computes the stability of the 1RSB solution and finds the SAT/UNSAT threshold always lies in the stable region, supporting (but not proving) the conjecture that the cavity values are exact.

## Key definitions and results

**Random K-SAT ensemble (Section 1, pages 1 to 2).** $N$ Boolean variables, $M$ clauses, each clause an OR of $K$ literals; the $K$ variables in each clause are chosen uniformly at random and each appears negated with probability $1/2$. The control parameter is the clause-to-variable ratio $\alpha = M/N$, held fixed in the limit $N \to \infty$. Numerical simulations (reference [16]) indicate a sharp threshold $\alpha_c(K)$: for $\alpha < \alpha_c(K)$ a random instance is SAT with probability tending to 1, for $\alpha > \alpha_c(K)$ it is UNSAT with probability tending to 1. Hard instances cluster near $\alpha_c$, making this a phase transition relevant to typical-case complexity.

**Status of rigorous knowledge cited (Section 1, page 2).** Existence of a threshold phenomenon at large $N$ is proved (Friedgut, reference [11]) but the existence of a *limit* $\alpha_c$ as $N \to \infty$ is not established rigorously. Upper bounds via first-moment and variational-interpolation methods (references [8,17,14,10]); lower bounds via algorithm analysis (reference [1]) or second-moment methods (reference [2]). For even $K$, Guerra-style interpolation (reference [10]) proves the cavity $\alpha_c(K)$ is a rigorous upper bound.

**Factor graph and survey-propagation equations (Section 2.1, pages 4 to 5).** An instance is a bipartite factor graph: clauses are function nodes (squares), variables are variable nodes (circles), an edge joins a clause to each variable it contains, dashed if the variable appears negated and full otherwise. On each edge $a \to f$ sits a real number $\eta_{a \to f} \in [0,1]$, the cavity-bias survey, the message from clause $a$ to variable $f$. With $V_+^{(i)}$ the function nodes where variable $i$ appears unnegated (excluding cavity $a$) and $V_-^{(i)}$ those where it appears negated, define (Eq. 1)
$$\pi_\pm^i = \prod_{b \in V_\pm^{(i)}} (1 - \eta_{b \to i}).$$
The SP update (Eq. 2) is
$$\eta_{a \to f} = \prod_{i=1}^{K-1} \frac{\pi_+^i (1 - \pi_-^i)}{\pi_+^i + \pi_-^i - \pi_+^i \pi_-^i}.$$
Interpretation (pages 4 to 5): $\eta_{a \to f}$ is the probability that node $a$ sends a "warning" to $f$ (telling $f$ to take the value satisfying $a$), assuming the $K-1$ incoming warnings on the other inputs are independent. The four-case analysis (no warning $\pi_+^i \pi_-^i$; impeding only; supporting only; both, which signals a contradiction) yields Eq. 2 after conditioning on satisfiability (no contradiction). SP equations are exact whenever incoming warnings are independent, in particular on a tree; the random K-SAT factor graph is locally tree-like (function-node degree $K$ fixed, variable-node degree Poisson with mean $K\alpha$), which motivates but does not prove SP relevance.

**Clusters and complexity (Sections 2.2 to 2.3, pages 6 to 8).** A $d$-cluster is a connected component of the graph on SAT assignments with edges between assignments at Hamming distance $\le d$; it is a *constrained* cluster if some variable takes the same value in all its assignments. The 1RSB hypothesis: in a window $\alpha_s < \alpha < \alpha_c$ there exist exponentially many well-separated constrained clusters. The number $\mathcal{N}_c$ of constrained clusters defines the **complexity** $\Sigma = \lim_{N \to \infty} (1/N)\,\mathbb{E}(\ln \mathcal{N}_c)$. The explicit formula (Eq. 3) is $\Sigma = (1/N)[\sum_a \Sigma_a^c - \sum_j (n_j - 1)\Sigma_j^v]$ with clause contribution (Eq. 4) and site contribution (Eq. 5) written in terms of the $\pi$ and $\eta$ variables. The central, totally unproven, conjecture (page 9): the satisfiability threshold $\alpha_c(K)$ is the value of $\alpha$ where the complexity $\Sigma$ vanishes.

**Statistical analysis and the coupled integral equations (Section 3, pages 8 to 11).** Treating the survey $\eta$ on a random edge as a random variable, change variables to $\phi = -\ln(1 - \eta)$ and $x = -\ln(\pi)$ (Eq. 6) with pdfs $S(\phi)$ and $B(x)$, both mixtures (atomic at 0). Using that variable degrees are Poisson with mean $K\alpha$ (and $k_\pm^i$ are iid Poisson with mean $K\alpha/2$), one gets the two coupled self-consistency equations (Eqs. 7 and 9):
$$B(x) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty f_{K\alpha/2}(k)\, S^{\otimes k}(x), \qquad f_{K\alpha/2}(k) = \frac{(K\alpha/2)^k}{k!} e^{-K\alpha/2},$$
$$S(\phi) = \int \prod_{i=1}^{K-1} [B(x_+^i)dx_+^i\, B(x_-^i)dx_-^i]\, \delta\!\left(\phi + \ln\Big[1 - \prod_{i=1}^{K-1} \frac{e^{x_-^i} - 1}{e^{x_+^i} + e^{x_-^i} - 1}\Big]\right).$$
The complexity is then assembled (Eqs. 10 to 18) into the compact form (Eq. 18)
$$\Sigma = \Sigma_0 + \alpha[K I_{K-1} - (K-1) I_K],$$
with $\Sigma_0 = \int dx\,dz\, B(x)B(z)\ln(e^x + e^z - 1)$ (Eq. 15) and $I_s$ the integral (Eq. 13). The threshold $\alpha_c$ is the root of $\Sigma(\alpha) = 0$.

**Stability analysis (Section 4, pages 11 to 18).** Two instabilities of the 1RSB solution toward two-step RSB are checked, following Montanari and Ricci-Tersenghi (reference [28]). Type I, *iteration stability*: the Jacobian $T = \partial p_\gamma^\ell / \partial p_\beta^1$ (Eq. 20) of the SP update is iterated $d$ times; the perturbation grows as $[K\alpha(K-1)]^d \mathrm{Tr}\langle [T^1 \cdots T^d]^2\rangle \equiv \lambda_d$ (Eq. 21), with stability when the limit (Eq. 31) is negative. Type II, *stability to bug proliferation*: a $2 \times 2$ matrix $V$ (Eqs. 23, 32 to 34) tracks how a finite "bug" (a flipped warning) propagates; products containing the off-diagonal matrix twice vanish, so only the diagonal value $\tilde v$ (Eq. 32) matters, giving (Eq. 35)
$$\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{1}{d}\ln \mu_d = \ln\!\Big(\frac{K\alpha(K-1)}{2}\Big) + \lim_{d \to \infty}\frac{1}{d}\ln\langle [\tilde v^1 \cdots \tilde v^d]\rangle.$$
The 1RSB stability threshold $\alpha_s(K)$ is where this crosses zero.

**Numerical solution and threshold values (Section 5, pages 18 to 22).** After regularizing the delta-peaks at 0 (Eqs. 38, 39, with $t = 1 - (1-\tau)^{K-1}$ and $\tau = \exp(-\tfrac{K\alpha}{2}(1-t))$), the coupled integral equations (Eqs. 40 to 42) are solved by **population dynamics**: a population of $N$ field variables $\phi$ is iterated until stationary, then $\Sigma$ is estimated by sampling. The dynamic threshold $\alpha_d$ is the first $\alpha$ with a nontrivial stable pdf; $\alpha_c$ is the root of $\Sigma(\alpha)$.

Table 1 (page 22) collects, for $K = 3$ to $10$, the dynamic threshold $\alpha_d$ (bold, population dynamics), the first-moment prediction $\alpha_d^{(0)}$, the stability threshold $\alpha_s$ (bold), the satisfiability threshold $\alpha_c$ (bold), and the $\varepsilon$-expansion estimates $\alpha_c^{(1)}, \alpha_c^{(2)}, \alpha_c^{(7)}$ at orders 1, 2, 7. For all $K$, the ordering $\alpha_d(K) < \alpha_s(K) < \alpha_c(K)$ holds, so the SAT/UNSAT threshold always sits in the stable region (Conclusions, page 31). The headline refined value (Eq. 43) is
$$\alpha_c(3) = 4.26675 \pm 0.00015,$$
error bars being $\pm 2\sigma$ over population-dynamics runs (Figs. 4, 5; a single $K=3$ run with $N = 10^6$, $T = 70000$ consumes more than $10^{14}$ random numbers). For $K = 4$, linear regression of $\Sigma(\alpha)$ near the root gives $\alpha_c = 9.9324$ (Fig. 4).

**Large-$K$ expansion (Section 6 and Appendix A, pages 23 to 35).** With the rescaled $\hat\alpha = 2^{-K}\alpha$ (Eq. 46) and $\varepsilon = 2^{-K}$, the rigorous bounds scale (Eqs. 44, 45) as $2^{-K}\alpha_{\mathrm{LB/UB}}(K) \simeq \ln 2 - 2^{-K}(\cdots) + O(2^{-2K})$. Expanding the cavity equations through a systematic moment expansion (Section 6.2, Appendix A), the satisfiability threshold has the asymptotic series (Eq. 64, leading orders)
$$\hat\alpha_c = \ln 2 - \frac{\ln 2 + 1}{2}\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon^2),$$
and this leading-plus-first-correction expression is **identical to the best known rigorous upper bound** of references [8,17] (page 26). The coefficients $\hat\alpha_i$ in $2^{-K}\alpha_c(K) = \ln 2 + \sum_i \hat\alpha_i \varepsilon^i$ are polynomials in $K$ of degree $2i - 2$ (Conjecture 2, Eq. 87); the first three are given explicitly (Eqs. A.21 to A.23):
$$\hat\alpha_1 = -\frac{1 + \ln 2}{2}, \quad \hat\alpha_2 = \frac{1}{8} - \frac{\ln 2}{12} + \frac{3\ln 2 - 2}{8}K - \frac{\ln 2 + 2\ln^2 2}{8}K^2,$$
with $\hat\alpha_3$ a degree-4 polynomial in $K$ (Eq. A.23); coefficients up to $\hat\alpha_7$ are stated to be available on request. Already at second order the cavity value is strictly below the best rigorous upper bound; the 7th-order expansion matches the population-dynamics numbers to under 1 percent for $K=3$ and better for $K \ge 4$ (Fig. 8, Table 1).

Large-$K$ asymptotics of the other thresholds: the dynamic threshold (Eq. 73) is $\alpha_d = \frac{2^K}{K}(\ln K + d^\star)e^{e^{-d^\star}/2}$ with $d^\star$ solving $\exp(d^\star) = \tfrac12(\ln K + d^\star)$ (Eqs. 74, 75); the stability threshold (Eq. 86) is $\alpha_s = \frac{2^K}{K}(\ln(2K) + d^\star(2K))e^{\frac12 e^{-d^\star(2K)}}$ with $d_s(K) = d^\star(2K) + \ln 2$ (Eq. 85). The unstable window shrinks: $\lim_{K \to \infty} \alpha_d(K)/\alpha_s(K) = 1$, and $(\alpha_s - \alpha_d)/\alpha_d$ is of order $1/\ln K$ (page 31).

**Self-consistency check (Section 2.3, pages 3, 22; Conclusions).** The paper does not prove cluster existence; it leaves clustering as a mathematical conjecture. The contribution beyond earlier work is the stability check: for $K = 3$ the 1RSB solution is self-consistent for $\alpha_s(3) \simeq 4.15 < \alpha \le \alpha_c(3)$, and for all studied $K$ the analysis is self-consistent at $\alpha_c(K)$, which the authors take as added credit for the conjecture that $\alpha_c(K)$ is exact.

## Techniques and proof ideas

The methods a builder (or an experiment author) would reuse:

1. **Factor-graph message passing (SP) as the primitive.** The SP equations (Eqs. 1, 2) are belief-propagation-style updates in an enlarged variable space $\{0, 1, *\}^N$ ($*$ = unconstrained). The derivation is a careful four-case warning calculus conditioned on no contradiction. This is the same object that the project's SAT-phase-transition experiment exercises.

2. **Cavity / locally-tree-like approximation.** Independence of incoming messages is exact on trees and asymptotically valid because the random factor graph is locally tree-like (Poisson variable degrees, mean $K\alpha$). The whole apparatus rests on this heuristic; it is honestly flagged as unproven (page 5).

3. **Density-functional reduction.** Instead of tracking individual messages, track the *probability distribution* $S(\phi)$, $B(x)$ of messages over a random edge, via the change of variables $\phi = -\ln(1-\eta)$, $x = -\ln\pi$ (Eq. 6). This turns message passing into a pair of coupled integral fixed-point equations (Eqs. 7, 9). The Poisson convolution structure (Eq. 7) is the key analytic simplification.

4. **Population dynamics (Section 5.2, page 19).** A Monte Carlo representation of the densities: hold a population of $N$ sampled fields, repeatedly draw $K-1$ of them, apply the SP update, and replace a random member. This is the workhorse numerical algorithm. The explicit six-step recipe (page 19) is directly implementable. Initialization at $\mathbb{E}(\Phi) = 2^{1-K}$ is needed at large $K$ to avoid overflow and the trivial all-zero fixed point.

5. **Complexity as the order parameter.** The number of clusters is exponential with rate $\Sigma$; $\alpha_c$ is where $\Sigma \to 0$ (no more clusters, so no SAT assignments). The closed integral formula (Eqs. 13 to 18) lets $\Sigma$ be sampled directly from the converged population.

6. **Two-step-RSB stability via Jacobian products (Section 4).** Iteration stability uses $\mathrm{Tr}$ of products of update Jacobians along a chain (Eqs. 21, 31); bug-proliferation stability uses products of $2 \times 2$ matrices that collapse to a scalar recursion in $\tilde v$ (Eqs. 32 to 35). Both are computed on the same population.

7. **Systematic moment / cumulant expansion in $\varepsilon = 2^{-K}$ (Appendix A).** The circular map $S_r \mapsto [A, B] \mapsto D \mapsto S_r$ (Eqs. 41, 42, 51, 52) is rewritten in terms of moments $M_n = \mathbb{E}(\phi^n)$, central moments $b_n$, and cumulants $\hat b_n$, with scalings $\hat b_n = O(\varepsilon^{n-1})$, $b_n = O(\varepsilon^{\lceil n/2\rceil})$ (Eq. 50). Taylor coefficients $C_{n,\ell}$ (from $\ln^n(1/(1-x))$, recursion Eq. A.2) and $D_{p,q}^{(\ell)}$ (Eq. 54) close the recursion (Eq. A.4). Self-consistency enters through needing $\hat\alpha$ to first order (Eq. A.9). The series was pushed to 7th order with Maple.

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the quantitative substrate for the **SAT-phase-transition experiment** (`experiments/sat_phase_transition/`) and is the canonical citation for the random 3-SAT threshold $\alpha_c \approx 4.267$ recorded in the project's known-landmarks list (CLAUDE.md cites "Mertens-Mezard-Zecchina 2006" for exactly this value). Its specific connections:

- **It supplies the number and its uncertainty.** The project landmark "$\alpha_c \approx 4.267$" is precisely this paper's $\alpha_c(3) = 4.26675 \pm 0.00015$ (Eq. 43). For any experiment that compares an empirical finite-$N$ crossing point against the predicted threshold, this is the reference value, and the paper is explicit that the prediction is a *conjecture* (the cavity values are not proved exact), with the supporting evidence being self-consistency and stability, not a proof.

- **It is average-case, not worst-case.** The threshold is about random instances at fixed $\alpha$; it speaks to typical-case complexity (where hard instances concentrate near $\alpha_c$), not to worst-case separations. This is exactly LEARNINGS finding (5): average-case is not worst-case. The phase transition explains *why random SAT is empirically hard near the threshold* but does not, by itself, bear on $\mathsf{P}$ vs $\mathsf{NP}$ as a worst-case question.

- **Barrier-discipline status: out of scope, and that is the honest reading.** This is not a circuit or formula lower-bound technique, so the three-barrier checker (`experiments/_shared/barriers.py`) does not directly apply: there is no candidate technique here claiming to separate complexity classes. The cavity method is an analytic/heuristic tool for computing a threshold, not a lower-bound method. A barrier scorecard would be a category error. What *is* worth recording for ADVERSARY: the entire edifice is non-rigorous (unproven cluster existence, page 6; unproven message independence, page 5; the $\Sigma = 0$ criterion is "totally unproven," page 9). It is a source of conjectured constants and structural pictures, not of theorems.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions (01 to 04):
- **01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** no direct contribution. The cavity method does not produce circuit lower bounds.
- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, MCSP crux):** indirect and speculative. The SP/cavity picture is a structural account of the solution-space geometry of random SAT (clustering, freezing). If a meta-complexity argument ever needs average-case hardness of a search problem near a threshold, this clustering picture is the relevant phenomenology. No concrete link is established by this paper.
- **03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP):** tangential. The hardness of certifying *unsatisfiability* just above $\alpha_c$ is a proof-complexity question (resolution/Sum-of-Squares lower bounds for random UNSAT formulas). This paper gives the location $\alpha_c$ but does not address proof-system lower bounds.
- **04 (GCT):** not relevant.

Mapping to LEARNINGS findings:
- Strongly supports **(5) average-case is not worst-case**: this is the cleanest worked example of a sharp average-case complexity phenomenon (a phase transition) that is orthogonal to worst-case separation.
- Neutral on **(1) barriers compose**, **(2) natural proofs are common**, **(3) constructivity crux is MCSP**, **(4) relativization is finite/mechanical**, **(6) Williams is the existence proof**: the cavity method lives in a different methodological universe (analytic statistical physics) and does not interact with the barrier machinery.
- Provides a *contrast case* for **(9) the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant**: the complexity $\Sigma$ is exactly the kind of analytic/rational order parameter (a log-count of clusters, computed from rational expressions in the messages) that *would* algebrize if it were a lower-bound certificate. It is a useful negative example of what the "missing object" is *not*.

Concrete open questions and cruxes this source exposes:
1. **Rigor gap on the threshold itself.** Existence of the limit $\alpha_c(3)$ as $N \to \infty$ was not rigorous when this paper was written; the cavity value is conjectural. For the project's purposes the experiment should treat $4.26675$ as a conjectured target, not a theorem. Follow-up: the Ding-Sly-Sun 2015 large-$k$ sharp-threshold result (cited in CLAUDE.md landmarks, not in this paper) rigorously confirms the cavity prediction for large $k$; chase how far rigor has reached for $k = 3$ specifically.
2. **The $\Sigma = 0$ criterion is unproven** (page 9). Whether the satisfiability threshold genuinely coincides with the complexity-vanishing point is the load-bearing conjecture. ADVERSARY should note this is assumed, not derived.
3. **Cluster existence is assumed** (page 6). The whole 1RSB picture (exponentially many constrained clusters in $\alpha_s < \alpha < \alpha_c$) is a mathematical conjecture in this paper.
4. **Two-step-RSB neighborhood of $\alpha_d$** (Conclusions, page 31): the authors flag that near $\alpha_d(K)$ higher-order RSB is needed; the 1RSB numbers there are not trusted.
5. **References to follow up** (cited by bracket number, full bibliography not in the PDF text layer): [23,24] (the original $K=3$ cavity computation and the survey-propagation algorithm, Mezard-Parisi-Zecchina and Mezard-Zecchina), [4] (the detailed SP derivation / BP-in-$\{0,1,*\}$ space), [28] (Montanari and Ricci-Tersenghi, the two-step-RSB stability method), [11] (Friedgut, threshold existence), [8,17] (first-moment / variational upper bounds that the large-$K$ leading term matches), [10] (Guerra-interpolation upper bound for even $K$), [2] (second-moment lower bound), [22] (rigorous confirmation of 1RSB for K-XORSAT), [30] (K-XORSAT phase transition).
