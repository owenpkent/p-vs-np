# Reading notes: Circuit Minimization Problem (Valentine Kabanets, Jin-Yi Cai, 2000)

- **Source:** `references/meta_complexity/kabanets_cai_2000_mcsp.pdf`
- **Type / venue:** Conference paper, STOC 2000 (the on-disk copy is the November 15, 1999 preprint version).
- **Dossier strand:** meta-complexity device (the MCSP crux; foundation for direction 02 natural-proofs evasion).
- **Read:** Read in full. All 12 pages: Abstract and Section 1 (Introduction), Section 2 (MCSP and P) with all six subsections 2.1 through 2.6, Section 3 (MCSP and NP-completeness) subsections 3.1 and 3.2, Section 4 (Concluding Remarks and Open Problems), and the full reference list (pages 10 to 12).

## Summary
This is the paper that gives the Minimum Circuit Size Problem (MCSP) its modern name and its first systematic complexity-theoretic study. It does two things. First (Section 2) it argues MCSP is unlikely to be in P (or even P/poly) by deriving a cascade of surprising consequences from the assumption "MCSP is easy": breaking strong pseudorandom generators (factoring Blum integers on average), hardness amplification, a family of maximum-circuit-complexity functions in $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$, $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{ZPP}$, and collapse of global and local derandomization conditions. Second (Section 3) it argues that proving MCSP NP-complete (under "natural" reductions) is itself beyond current technique, because such a proof would force explicit strongly exponential circuit lower bounds for $\mathsf{E}$. The paper is the historical prerequisite that connects MCSP directly to the Razborov-Rudich natural-proofs barrier (Theorem 2).

## Key definitions and results

**MCSP, the central object (Section 1).** Instance: a Boolean function $f_n : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ given by its truth table of length $2^n$, and a number $s_n \in \mathbb{N}$ in binary. Question: is $f_n$ computable by a Boolean circuit of size at most $s_n$? The authors note this was studied in the USSR in the 1950s (Yablonski [Yab59a, Yab59b]) under the heading of eliminating "perebor" (brute-force search), and that Yablonski's impossibility argument was restricted to a special class of algorithms and does not establish hardness. They observe MCSP is obviously in NP: the input size is $O(2^n)$, so there is enough time to verify a guessed circuit of appropriate size on all $2^n$ inputs (Section 1, second paragraph after the problem statement). The motivating tension (Section 1): hard Boolean functions abound (most $n$-variable functions need circuits of size $\ge 2^n/n$ by Shannon [Sha49]), yet no explicit hard function is known, so the question is whether we can at least recognize a hard function efficiently.

**Natural properties (Section 2.1), the Razborov-Rudich framing.** The hardness $H(G_k)$ of a pseudorandom generator $G_k : \{0,1\}^k \to \{0,1\}^{2k}$ is the minimal $s$ such that some size-$s$ circuit $C$ distinguishes $G_k$'s output from uniform with advantage $\ge 1/s$. $G_k$ is **strong** if $H(G_k) > 2^{k^{\Omega(1)}}$. A combinatorial property $\{C_n\}$ of $n$-variable functions is $\Gamma$-**natural with density** $\delta_n$ if each $C_n$ contains a subset $C_n^*$ such that (1) the predicate "$f_n \in C_n^*$?" is computable in $\Gamma$ (with $f_n$ given as a truth table), and (2) $C_n^*$ contains at least a $\delta_n$ fraction of all $n$-variable functions. Standard setting: $\Gamma = \mathsf{P}$, $\delta_n = 2^{-O(n)}$. A property is **useful against** $\Lambda$ if every family $\{f_n\}$ with $f_n \in C_n$ infinitely often lies outside $\Lambda$.

**Theorem 1 (Razborov-Rudich, [RR97], restated in Section 2.1).** If a P/poly-natural property useful against P/poly exists, then there is no strong pseudorandom generator in P/poly.

**Theorem 2 (Section 2.1), the central barrier link.** If MCSP is in P/poly, then there is no strong pseudorandom generator in P/poly. Proof: if MCSP is in P/poly then the property "$f_n$ has no small circuit" is itself a P/poly-natural property useful against P/poly (it is checkable via the MCSP oracle, it has high density by Shannon's counting bound, and a family meeting it has high circuit complexity), so Theorem 1 applies. This is the explicit statement that MCSP being easy collides with the natural-proofs world.

**Corollary 3 (Section 2.1).** If MCSP is in P, then for any $\epsilon > 0$ there is a $2^{n^\epsilon}$-time algorithm that factors Blum integers well on average. Mechanism: the factoring-based generator is believed strong; Theorem 2 turns an efficient MCSP algorithm into a distinguisher, hence an average-case factoring algorithm faster than the best known $2^{n^{1/4}}$ deterministic [Pol74, Str76] or $2^{\sqrt n}$ probabilistic [LP92] bounds.

**Theorem 4 (Section 2.2), hardness amplification.** Assume MCSP is in P. Then there is a polynomial-time algorithm that, given the truth table of an $n$-variable function of circuit complexity $\ge 2^{\epsilon n}$, outputs $2^{\Omega(n)}$ functions on $m \in \Omega(n)$ variables each, all of circuit complexity greater than $\frac{2^m}{m}(1 + (1-\gamma)\frac{\log m}{m})$ for any $\gamma > 0$ (near-maximal by Lupanov). Proof uses **Theorem 5 (Impagliazzo-Wigderson [IW97])**: a function of complexity $2^{\epsilon n}$ converts in time $2^{O(n)}$ into a PRG $G_{dn} : \{0,1\}^{dn} \to \{0,1\}^{2^n}$ of hardness $> 2^n$; and **Theorem 6 (Lupanov [Lup59, Lup63])**: almost all $n$-variable functions need size $> \frac{2^n}{n}(1 + (1-\epsilon)\frac{\log n}{n})$. The PRG's outputs are mostly truth tables of hard functions; the MCSP oracle filters out the easy ones.

**Corollary 7 (Section 2.2).** If MCSP is in P, then $\mathsf{E}$ contains a family of circuit complexity $\ge 2^{\epsilon n}$ iff $\mathsf{E}$ contains a family of complexity $> \frac{2^n}{n}(1+(1-\gamma)\frac{\log n}{n})$ for any $\gamma > 0$. (Relatively-hard implies very-hard inside $\mathsf{E}$.)

**Theorem 8 and Corollary 9 (Section 2.3), natural properties revisited.** A "natural property" here is taken to be P-natural with density $1/N$, $N = 2^n$. Theorem 8: for any fixed family of functions of complexity $\ge 2^{\epsilon n}$ and any natural property $C$, the class $\mathsf{E}^f$ (and, via $O(n)$-size queries, $\mathsf{E}$ itself, Corollary 9) contains a family satisfying $C$. Interpretation: a single hard family carries enough information to find witnesses for every natural property in time $2^{O(n)}$.

**Theorem 10 and Corollary 11 (Section 2.4), hard functions in uniform classes.** If MCSP is in P, then $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ contains a family of maximum circuit complexity (proof follows [MVW99, Lemma 2]: under the MCSP-in-P assumption, the relevant queries become NP-questions). Corollary 11: if MCSP is in P, then for every $k$ there is a language $L_k \in \mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ requiring circuit size $\ge n^k$. The authors note (Section 2.4, after Cor 11) the best unconditional result is $\mathsf{ZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ [KW98], and that this is about optimal for relativizing techniques because there are oracles relative to which all of $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ has linear-size circuits [Wil85]. They draw the conclusion: relative to that same oracle, MCSP is not in P. This is an explicit relativization observation about MCSP.

**Theorem 12, Corollary 13 (Section 2.5), two-sided vs zero error.** $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{ZPP}^{\mathsf{MCSP}}$ (the ZPP machine guesses a truth table on $O(\log n)$ variables, uses the MCSP oracle to keep only hard ones, then derandomizes by [IW97] Theorem 5). Corollary 13: if MCSP is in P, then $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{ZPP}$. The authors note this would follow trivially from $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{ZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ if MCSP were NP-hard, but they cannot show NP-hardness.

**Theorem 14 (Section 2.6), global equals local derandomization.** If MCSP is in P, the following are equivalent: (1) $\mathsf{E}$ contains a function of complexity $\ge 2^{\epsilon n}$; (2) an efficient discrepancy set generator exists; (3) an efficient hitting set generator exists; (4) Promise-SAT is in polynomial time; (5) a polynomial-time circuit approximator exists. Unconditionally one only knows (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2)$\Leftrightarrow$(3) [ACR98, IW97, ISW99] and (4)$\Leftrightarrow$(5) [ACRT97, BF99]; the MCSP assumption bridges the global/local gap via a deterministic witness-finding routine that fixes one bit at a time guided by the circuit approximator.

**Section 3, the NP-completeness obstacle.** Definition of a **natural reduction** (Section 3.1): a Karp reduction $R$ from $A$ to $B$ is natural if for any instance $I$ the size of $R(I)$ (and its numerical parameters) depends only on $|I|$, and the two sizes are polynomially related. The authors note all NP-completeness reductions they know of are natural, including the reduction from SAT to the Minimum Size DNF Problem [Mas79].

**Theorem 15 (Section 3.1).** If MCSP is NP-hard under a natural reduction from SAT, then (1) $\mathsf{E}$ contains a family not in P/poly infinitely often, and (2) $\mathsf{E}$ contains a family of circuit complexity $2^{\Omega(n)}$ i.o., unless $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{SUBEXP}$. Proof idea: a natural reduction maps size-$n$ formulas to truth tables on $k = \theta(\log n)$ variables with parameter $s_n$ depending on $n$ only; if $s_n$ were polynomially bounded, all such MCSP instances would be solvable in quasipolynomial time, putting SAT in QP, which (with a $\mathsf{QP}^{\Sigma_k^p}$ superpolynomial-complexity language and PH collapse) forces NP into QP and yields a P/poly-evading family in $\mathsf{E}$. Otherwise the trivial unsatisfiable formulas get mapped to genuinely hard truth tables computable in $\mathsf{E}$.

**Theorem 18 and Corollary 19 (Section 3.2).** Combining Theorem 15 with hardness-randomness tradeoffs (Theorem 16 = Impagliazzo-Wigderson [IW97], Theorem 17 = Babai-Fortnow-Nisan-Wigderson [BFNW93]): if MCSP is NP-hard under a natural reduction from SAT, then (1) $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{SUBEXP}$ i.o., and (2) $\mathsf{BPP} = \mathsf{P}$ i.o. unless $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{SUBEXP}$. Corollary 19: $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \mathsf{E}$ would follow (via diagonalization against SUBEXP). Net reading: NP-hardness of MCSP via natural reductions would crack open derandomization, which is why it is hard to prove.

**Open problems (Section 4).** Is the Minimum Depth-$d$ Circuit Size Problem NP-complete for every $d \ge 2$? Only $d = 2$ is known [Mas79]. The obstacle for $\mathsf{AC}^0$ and monotone versions is the lack of strongly exponential lower bounds (known bounds for parity / CLIQUE / BMS are only exponential in some root of $n$). Can Corollary 7 be sharpened to "maximum" complexity? Is MCSP self-reducible (find a minimum circuit in poly($2^n$) time given an MCSP oracle)?

## Techniques and proof ideas
The unifying engine is the **hardness-to-randomness conversion of Impagliazzo-Wigderson (Theorem 5 / [IW97])**: a single hard truth table becomes a pseudorandom generator whose output strings are overwhelmingly truth tables of hard functions. Every "MCSP is easy" consequence is built by running this generator and using the hypothetical MCSP-in-P algorithm as a deterministic sieve that retains only the hard outputs (Theorems 4, 8, 12, 14). The complementary device is the **natural-properties dictionary of Razborov-Rudich** (Theorem 1): the MCSP predicate literally is a large, P-checkable property, so an efficient MCSP algorithm instantiates the forbidden object of [RR97]. A builder reusing this paper would take away three reusable moves: (a) treat MCSP as the canonical large+constructive property and plug it into the Razborov-Rudich machine; (b) use [IW97] to convert any hard function into a PRG and filter outputs with an MCSP oracle; (c) for negative meta-results, use the "natural reduction preserves size as a function of input length only" rigidity to argue that an NP-completeness reduction would have to manufacture explicit hard truth tables. The relativization observation (Section 2.4, using [Wil85]) is also reusable: MCSP-in-P is false relative to the oracle that puts $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ in linear-size circuits.

## Relevance to this project
This is the historical prerequisite for the entire meta-complexity strand and for direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion). Theorem 2 is the exact hinge the dossier calls the "constructivity crux is MCSP" (LEARNINGS finding 3): the property witnessing a circuit lower bound is constructive precisely when MCSP is easy, so MCSP sits at the boundary between the natural-proofs barrier and a real lower bound. The paper predates and sets up the later Williams-spine and meta-complexity literature: it is the first to show that an algorithm for MCSP yields lower bounds and breaks cryptography, which is the algorithm-to-lower-bound logic the project's leading path rides (LEARNINGS finding 8, futures converge on the Williams spine; finding 6, Williams is the existence proof barriers are passable). The natural-reduction obstruction in Section 3 is a clean instance of the barrier discipline: proving MCSP NP-complete by the only reduction style we know would force strongly exponential lower bounds, the same wall the project flags for the $\mathsf{AC}^0$ and monotone strands. The Section 2.4 relativization remark (MCSP not in P relative to the [Wil85] oracle) anchors LEARNINGS finding 4 (relativization is finite and mechanical) for this object.

## What this enables / what remains open
- **Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion, the MCSP crux):** This paper is the canonical citation for "MCSP easy $\Rightarrow$ no strong PRG" (Theorem 2). It makes precise that any route to circuit lower bounds that goes through an efficient MCSP algorithm is, by construction, a natural proof. The open builder question it exposes: is there a route to lower bounds via MCSP-hardness rather than MCSP-easiness (the meta-complexity direction the later literature pursues), and does that route evade naturality? This paper does not answer it; it only stakes out the easiness side.
- **Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** Corollary 11 (MCSP easy $\Rightarrow$ $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ has $n^k$-hard languages) and Theorem 10 ($\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ has maximum complexity) connect MCSP to explicit lower bounds, foreshadowing the algorithm-to-lower-bound spine. Open crux: bridge the gap between the unconditional $\mathsf{ZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ bound [KW98] and the $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ bound that MCSP-easiness would give, without the assumption. The paper flags the [Wil85] relativization wall as the reason this is hard.
- **Direction 03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP):** Less direct, but Section 3's natural-reduction analysis is a meta-mathematical obstruction of the same flavor as proof-complexity lower bounds; the rigidity argument (size depends on input length only) is a transferable lemma.
- **Direction 04 (GCT):** Not addressed by this paper.
- **LEARNINGS map:** Strong support for findings (2) natural proofs are common (MCSP-easiness instantiates the property), (3) constructivity crux is MCSP (Theorem 2 is the literal statement), (4) relativization is finite/mechanical (Section 2.4 [Wil85] remark), (5) average-case is not worst-case (Corollary 3 turns worst-case MCSP into average-case factoring). It is the upstream prerequisite for (6) and (8) (the Williams spine) without yet containing them.
- **Concrete open cruxes this source exposes:** (i) Is MCSP NP-complete at all, and must any such proof use a non-natural reduction? (open in 2000; the paper proves only that a natural reduction forces strong lower bounds). (ii) Is MCSP self-reducible given an MCSP oracle? (Section 4). (iii) Is Min-Depth-$d$-CSP NP-complete for $d \ge 2$, blocked by the absence of strongly exponential $\mathsf{AC}^0$ lower bounds (Section 4) which is exactly the project's $\mathsf{TC}^0$/approximate-degree hinge in spirit. (iv) Can Corollary 7 reach maximum (not just $2^{\epsilon n}$) complexity (Section 4)? None of these are resolved here; the paper is a map of where the meta-complexity device points, not a construction.
