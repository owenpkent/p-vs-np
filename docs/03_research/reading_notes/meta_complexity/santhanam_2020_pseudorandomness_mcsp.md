## Summary

The paper makes the case that the average-case complexity of the Minimum Circuit Size Problem (MCSP) is a central organizing object for understanding pseudorandomness in the cryptographic regime, and in particular for the long-standing question of whether one-way functions (OWFs) can be based on a natural NP problem. It proves three families of results: (1) unconditional equivalences (Theorem 1) showing that zero-error average-case hardness of $\mathsf{MCSP}[2^{\epsilon n}]$ over the uniform distribution is equivalent to the existence of succinct hitting sets and succinct two-sided pseudorandom distributions, derived via a novel Sampler-Distinguisher zero-sum game and the approximate Min-Max theorem; (2) a Universality Conjecture (existence of universal succinct PRGs) under which a clean chain of equivalences ties together OWFs, auxiliary-input OWFs, non-existence of $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$-natural proofs, zero-error average-case hardness of MCSP, hardness of PAC-learning with membership queries, and stretchable succinct HSGs (Theorems 3 and 9); and (3) two non-black-box results: lower bounds for MCSP against weak circuit classes imply $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$ (Theorem 4 / Theorem 10), and an approximation-to-average-case reduction for AveMCSP (Theorem 5 / Theorem 14), both of which provably cannot be black-box under standard crypto assumptions (Theorem 12).

## Key definitions and results

**Zero-error average-case hardness of MCSP (Section 2.2, page 12).** $\mathsf{MCSP}[s(n)]$ takes a $2^n$-bit truth table and asks whether the function has circuits of size at most $s(n)$. Bounded-error notions are vacuous over the uniform distribution because the problem is heavily biased toward "no" (almost all truth tables are hard). So the relevant notion is the zero-error one: an algorithm always outputs the correct answer or `?`, never errs, and outputs `?` with probability at most $1 - \epsilon(N)$ where $N = 2^n$. $Q$ is zero-error average-case hard for $\mathsf{SIZE}[\mathrm{poly}]$ with success probability $\epsilon$ if no poly-size $D_N$ family achieves success probability above $1 - \epsilon(N)$. Note (page 12, bottom): hardness is not just the complement of easiness; both require almost-all input lengths.

**Natural properties (Definition 2, page 13) and the HS17 bridge (Proposition 1, page 13).** A $\mathfrak{D}$-natural property useful against $\mathfrak{C}[s(n)]$ satisfies constructivity ($L_{\mathfrak{R}} \in \mathfrak{D}$), density ($\Pr_{f}[f \in \mathfrak{R}_n] \geq 1/2$), and usefulness ($\mathfrak{R}_n \cap \mathfrak{C}_n[s(n)] = \emptyset$). Proposition 1 (citing Hirahara-Santhanam [HS17]): $\mathsf{MCSP}[s(n)]$ is zero-error easy on average iff there are $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$-natural properties useful against $\mathsf{SIZE}[s(n)]$. This is the formal equation "natural proofs are essentially zero-error average-case algorithms for MCSP" (stated in Section 1.1.3, page 4 and Section 1.2.1, page 5).

**Succinct objects (Section 1.2.1, pages 5-6; Section 2.2, pages 11-12; Definition 1, page 12).** A succinct set or distribution lives on strings that, read as truth tables, have small circuits. A $\mathfrak{C}$-succinct PRG is a PRG inducing succinct pseudorandom distributions; a $\mathfrak{C}$-succinct HSG is a HSG whose range is succinct hitting sets. A universal succinct HSG / PRG (Definition 1) is a fixed poly-time computable family that is guaranteed to be a succinct HSG / PRG provided any such object exists. Pseudorandom functions are essentially efficiently samplable succinct PRGs (page 5).

**Universal succinct HSGs exist (Proposition 2, page 13).** For any standard circuit class $\mathfrak{C}$ and $n \leq s(n) \leq 2^n$, there is a universal $\mathfrak{C}[s(n)]$-succinct HSG $U$ with seed length $\mathsf{quasi}\text{-}s(n)$. Proof idea: interpret the seed as a circuit of size $s(n)$ and output its truth table. This is the "easy witness" method of Kabanets and Impagliazzo-Kabanets-Wigderson [Kab00, IKW01].

**Three-way equivalence among one-sided notions (Proposition 3, page 14).** For standard $\mathfrak{C}$, family $\mathfrak{D}$, and $s(n) \geq n$, for large $n$ the following are equivalent: (1) $\mathfrak{C}[\mathsf{quasi}\text{-}s(n)]$-succinct hitting sets exist against $\mathfrak{D}$; (2) a $\mathfrak{C}[\mathsf{quasi}\text{-}s(n)]$-succinct HSG with seed length $\mathsf{quasi}\text{-}s(n)$ exists; (3) $\mathfrak{C}\text{-}\mathsf{MCSP}[\mathsf{quasi}\text{-}s(n)]$ is zero-error average-case hard against $\mathfrak{D}$. This is the meta-mathematics-of-lower-bounds observation, related to [Wil16, GKSS17, FSV17].

**Main unconditional result (Theorem 1, informal, page 6; formal as Corollary 1 to Theorem 7, page 18).** The following are equivalent: for all $\epsilon > 0$, (1) succinct hitting sets supported on truth tables of circuit complexity $2^{\epsilon n}$ exist; (2) $\mathsf{MCSP}$ with parameter $2^{\epsilon n}$ is zero-error hard on average; (3) succinct two-sided pseudorandom distributions supported on truth tables of circuit complexity $2^{\epsilon n}$ exist. The surprising direction is (2) implies (3): a one-sided zero-error notion of hardness yields a two-sided (bounded-error) pseudorandom distribution.

**The general hardness-to-pseudorandomness theorem (Theorem 6, page 15).** For languages $Q, Q'$, if there is a $(Q,Q')$-semantic sampler $f$ (Definition 3, page 15: a poly-time sampler whose outputs land in $Q'$ whenever the seed prefix is in $Q$, and which acts as a good averaging/sampling map) with output length $m \leq N$, accuracy $\epsilon$, error $\delta$, and $Q$ is zero-error average-case infeasible for $\mathsf{SIZE}[\mathrm{poly}]$ with success probability $1 - \delta(N)$, then there are pseudorandom distributions with error $30\epsilon(N)$ against $\mathsf{SIZE}[\mathrm{poly}]$ supported on $Q'$.

**Theorem 7 (page 18)** instantiates Theorem 6 for MCSP via the $q(N)$-projection sampler (Definition 4, page 17), which outputs a contiguous substring of the input truth table. The semantic condition holds because a subfunction of a small-circuit function is itself a small-circuit function (using closure under projections), and the sampling condition follows from Hoeffding (Proposition 4, page 15). Conclusion: zero-error average-case infeasibility of $\mathfrak{C}\text{-}\mathsf{MCSP}[s(n)]$ yields $\mathfrak{C}[s']$-succinct pseudorandom distributions.

**Theorem 8 / Theorem 2 (pages 6, 19): SAT analog.** Under Feige's hypothesis (random $k$-CNFs of linear density are zero-error average-case hard to refute, [Fei02]), for each $\epsilon > 0$ there are pseudorandom distributions with error $O(\epsilon)$ supported entirely on satisfiable formulas. The proof uses the same projection sampler with a careful information-theoretically efficient encoding of $k$-CNFs (each clause $(n+1)k$ bits) so that sub-formulas of satisfiable formulas stay satisfiable and valid encodings are not trivially distinguishable.

**Universality Conjecture (Conjecture 1, page 20).** For every $\epsilon < 1$, there is a universal $\mathsf{SIZE}(2^{\epsilon n})$-succinct PRG with non-trivial seed length. It is the analog for two-sided pseudorandom distributions of the (provable) Proposition 2 for one-sided hitting sets.

**Conditional equivalence chain (Theorem 9, page 20; informally Theorem 3, pages 7-8).** Under the Universality Conjecture, the following nine items are equivalent: OWFs secure against $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$; non-uniform OWFs; $\mathsf{SIZE}[2^{\delta n}]$-succinct HSGs for some $\delta$; succinct HSGs for every $\epsilon$; PRGs with non-trivial seed length; $\mathsf{MCSP}[2^{\delta n}]$ hard on average for some $\delta$; $\mathsf{MCSP}[2^{\epsilon n}]$ hard on average for every $\epsilon$; non-existence of $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$-natural proofs against $\mathsf{SIZE}(2^{\epsilon n})$; non-PAC-learnability of poly-size circuits with membership queries over uniform. The proof (page 21) routes each direction through known results (HILL99, GGM86, CIKK16, HS17, OW93) plus the Conjecture, which supplies the otherwise-missing direction "succinct pseudorandom distributions yield a succinct PRG," after which standard stretching (Lemma 2) and the GGM86 succinctification (Lemma 3) close the loop.

**Non-black-box result 1 (Theorem 4, page 9; Theorem 10, page 23).** Let $\mathfrak{C}$ be a weak circuit class closed under projections against which there are natural proofs. If for some constant $k$, $\mathsf{MCSP}[n^k]$ is zero-error average-case hard against $\mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$, then $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$. The engine is Lemma 4 (page 22): succinct hitting sets against a weak (natural-proofs-admitting) $\mathfrak{C}$ give $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$ by a win-win analysis. If $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}$ we are done; if $\mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathfrak{C}$, the universal HSG output is in $\mathfrak{C}$, so the natural property gives a dense set in $\mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$ that the HSG fails to hit, a contradiction. Combined with Proposition 3 this yields Theorem 10.

**Non-black-box result 2 (Theorem 5, page 9; Theorem 14, page 24).** There is an approximation-to-average-case reduction for AveMCSP. Formally (Theorem 14): for any $\delta, k > 0$ there is $\epsilon > 0$ such that if $\mathsf{AveMCSP}[2^{n/2}]$ is zero-error easy on average for circuits of size $N^k$, then $\mathsf{AveMCSP}[2^{\epsilon n}, 2^{\delta n}]$ has poly-size circuits. The proof uses the average-case-robust Nisan-Wigderson generator of [CIKK17] (Theorem 13, page 24). A notable feature: the approximation gap depends on the complexity of the average-case algorithm.

**No-black-box-proof barriers (Theorem 12, page 23).** If OWFs of exponential hardness exist, there is a constant $k$ for which there is no black-box reduction from zero-error average-case hardness of $\mathsf{MCSP}[n^k]$ against $\mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$ to $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}[\mathrm{poly}]$. Proof: relativize to an oracle $A$ complete for $\mathsf{P}$ under projections; then $\mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathfrak{C}^A$ holds, so a black-box reduction would make $\mathsf{MCSP}[n^k]$ zero-error easy on average for $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$, which by the Razborov-Rudich natural-proofs argument [RR97] would invert any OWF in sub-exponential time. Theorem 4 is therefore inherently non-black-box (Section 1.2.3, page 9, also states there is no black-box version of the result under standard crypto assumptions).

## Techniques and proof ideas

**Sampler-Distinguisher zero-sum game (Section 3.2, pages 14-17).** This is the central new device. Row (Min) player strategies are YES instances $x \in Q$ of a fixed length; Column (Max) player strategies are circuits $D \in \mathfrak{D}$ of bounded size. The payoff is $E_y[D(f_N(x,y))] - E_z[D(z)]$, the gap between $D$ on sampler outputs and $D$ on uniform. A win-win on the game value: if for almost all lengths the value is low (Row player can fool every distinguisher), the approximately-optimal Row strategy (a uniform distribution on a small multiset of YES instances, by the Althofer / Lipton-Young approximate Min-Max theorem, Lemma 1, page 15) induces a succinct pseudorandom distribution. If the value is high on infinitely many lengths, the approximately-optimal Column strategy (a uniform distribution on $O(N'/\gamma^2)$ circuits) is assembled, via Adleman's trick and Hoeffding (Proposition 4), into a single poly-size deterministic family that solves $Q$ zero-error on average, contradicting hardness. A builder reuses: the game framing converts "no distinguisher succeeds" into a constructive averaging strategy, and the approximate Min-Max theorem keeps the winning strategy small enough to be poly-size.

**Semantic sampler abstraction (Definition 3, page 15).** The projection sampler is the workhorse instantiation: take a contiguous substring of a truth table (MCSP) or a sub-formula (SAT). The semantic condition (small-circuit truth tables restrict to small-circuit truth tables; satisfiable formulas have satisfiable sub-formulas) is what lets a hardness assumption about $Q$ transfer to a pseudorandom distribution on $Q'$. This is explicitly contrasted (page 7) with Trevisan's extractor-to-PRG connection [Tre01], which involves no semantic property.

**Win-win / easy-witness method.** Proposition 2 (universal HSG) and Lemma 4 both use the circuit-to-truth-table map and the win-win analysis from [Kab00, IKW01]. Lemma 4 is the cleanest illustration: it converts a hitting-set existence into a $\mathsf{P}$ lower bound using only the existence of natural proofs against the weak class.

**Non-black-box reductions via meta-complexity self-reference (Section 1.2.3, page 9; Section 6).** Because MCSP instances encode circuit computations, reductions can inspect the structure of inputs rather than treat the solver as an oracle. Theorem 14 exploits the CIKK17 generator whose output has small average-case circuit complexity exactly when the base function does, allowing an MCSP-style algorithm to read off complexity. The dependence of the approximation gap on the algorithm's complexity is the signature of the non-black-box construction.

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the canonical statement of why meta-complexity is the leading candidate to evade the natural-proofs barrier, which is direction 02 and LEARNINGS finding (3): the constructivity crux is MCSP.

- **The Razborov-Rudich equation made formal.** Proposition 1 (via [HS17]) pins down that a $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$-natural property useful against $\mathsf{SIZE}[s(n)]$ is the same object as a zero-error average-case algorithm for $\mathsf{MCSP}[s(n)]$. This is the exact hinge in the project's barrier discipline: natural proofs are common (LEARNINGS finding 2) precisely because most lower-bound properties are average-case algorithms for MCSP, and the way to evade the barrier is to make $\mathsf{MCSP}$ itself hard on average. Theorem 9 item 8 ("no $\mathsf{SIZE}(\mathrm{poly})$-natural proofs against $\mathsf{SIZE}(2^{\epsilon n})$") sits inside the same equivalence class as "OWFs exist" and "MCSP is hard on average." Evading the natural-proofs barrier and basing crypto on MCSP are, under the Universality Conjecture, the same act.

- **Why meta-complexity can pass the barrier.** The argument is not that MCSP magically dodges Razborov-Rudich; it is that the natural-proofs barrier is a statement about MCSP, so making MCSP average-case hard is logically equivalent to the non-existence of the natural proofs that the barrier warns about. The barrier becomes a target coordinate (CLAUDE.md compass framing), not a wall: the proof must establish the hard instance of the very problem the barrier is about.

- **Non-black-box as the implied fourth barrier.** Theorems 4, 5, 10, 12, 14 are explicitly non-black-box and provably cannot be black-box (Theorem 12) under crypto assumptions. This is direct evidence for LEARNINGS finding (10), the implied fourth barrier of bounded-interface reconstruction: the useful reductions in this area only work when the reduction can open up the instance (which encodes a circuit) rather than query a solver as an oracle. The relativization barrier (LEARNINGS finding 4, relativization is finite/mechanical) is exactly what Theorem 12 invokes via the $\mathsf{P}$-complete oracle.

- **Connection to the Williams spine.** The paper notes (Section 1.1.3, page 4) that Williams [Wil16] implicitly studies MCSP-derandomization connections but in the complexity-theoretic (not cryptographic) regime. This paper takes the cryptographic regime. The futures-converge-on-Williams reading (LEARNINGS finding 8) and the meta-complexity leading path both run through the same algorithm-to-lower-bound machinery (easy-witness, IKW01, used here in Proposition 2 and Lemma 4).

- **Average-case is not worst-case (LEARNINGS finding 5).** The whole paper is about uniform-distribution average-case hardness of MCSP, and Theorems 5 / 14 are about approximation-to-average-case reductions, evidence (per Hirahara) toward excluding Impagliazzo's Pessiland and Heuristica. This is squarely the average-case-vs-worst-case axis.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions and LEARNINGS findings:

- **Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion, the MCSP crux).** This paper is foundational here. It gives the precise dictionary (Proposition 1, Theorem 9) and the constructive game machinery (Theorem 6) to convert MCSP average-case hardness into pseudorandomness. A BUILDER aiming to construct a non-natural lower bound should treat "make $\mathsf{MCSP}[2^{\epsilon n}]$ zero-error hard on average" as the equivalent target, and should study the Sampler-Distinguisher game as the conversion mechanism.

- **Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds toward P/poly).** Theorem 10 is a transfer principle: MCSP hardness against a weak class $\mathfrak{C}$ that admits natural proofs (e.g. $\mathsf{AC}^0$, $\mathsf{AC}^0[p]$) gives $\mathsf{P} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{C}$. This is a concrete, if surprising, lever from a meta-complexity hardness assumption to an explicit circuit lower bound. The catch: it is non-black-box and only fires for weak classes.

- **Direction 03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP).** Theorem 8 ties Feige's hypothesis (a refutation hardness assumption about random $k$-SAT, which is a proof-complexity-flavored statement) to pseudorandom distributions on satisfiable formulas. The semantic-sampler abstraction is the bridge. Item 1 of Theorem 3 (page 7) notes that MCSP is believed NP-complete and unlikely in coNP, and Rudich's conjecture even asserts MCSP is hard on average for coNP. This is the relevant tension for the NP-vs-coNP strand.

- **Direction 04 (GCT).** No direct connection in this paper; the references [FSV17, GKSS17] on succinct hitting sets and algebraic natural proofs are the nearest bridge and are worth following up for the GCT strand.

Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes:

1. **Prove or refute the Universality Conjecture (Conjecture 1).** Everything clean in Theorem 9 is conditional on it. The author flags (page 8) no strong belief either way and suggests understanding uniformity and succinctness of approximate Min-Max strategies [Alt94, LY94] as a route. Failure of the Conjecture would itself imply non-existence of OWFs, so its falsity has strong consequences too (Section 7, page 25).
2. **Establish the equivalences of Theorem 9 unconditionally.** Theorem 1 is the unconditional fragment; closing the gap is the goal. The missing direction is always "succinct pseudorandom distributions yield a succinct PRG."
3. **Can HSGs be stretched in the cryptographic regime, or are succinct HSGs equivalent to standard HSGs?** (Questions 4-6, pages 3, 21.) The Conjecture makes succinct HSGs stretchable; the standard case is open.
4. **Is MCSP-parameter-robust?** Theorem 9 item 7 says under the Conjecture average-case hardness is robust across $2^{\delta n}$ and $2^{\epsilon n}$; an unconditional version is open (Question 8, page 4).
5. **Reduce error to negligible for the SAT / pseudorandom-set constructions** without losing generality (Remark, page 19): the projection sampler caps error; a Nisan-Wigderson sampler does better but only for specific $\mathfrak{C}$.
6. **Find a natural distribution under which MCSP is hard on average in the bounded-error sense** (Section 7, page 25); the uniform distribution is vacuous for bounded error, so a different natural distribution is needed for a possibly easier route to OWFs.
7. **Bridge the non-black-box results to unconditional separations.** Theorems 10 and 14 are non-black-box, which is encouraging for the implied fourth barrier (finding 10), but a BUILDER should ask: what is the smallest weak class for which Theorem 10 could be made to fire unconditionally, and does the AveMCSP approximation reduction (Theorem 14) compose with the Williams ACC0 spine?
