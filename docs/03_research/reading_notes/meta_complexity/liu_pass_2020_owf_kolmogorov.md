## Summary

The paper proves that one-way functions (OWFs) exist if and only if the time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity $K^t$ is mildly hard-on-average to compute (Theorem 1.1 / Theorem 3.1). This gives the first natural and well-studied computational problem (with a natural distribution, the uniform distribution over $n$-bit strings) whose mild average-case hardness exactly characterizes the existence of the central private-key cryptographic primitives (PRGs, PRFs, private-key encryption, digital signatures, commitments). The equivalence is unconditional, in contrast to Santhanam's contemporaneous conditional equivalence for MCSP (San20). The forward direction (avg-case $K^t$-hardness implies OWFs) is a short, self-contained counting argument; the reverse direction (OWFs imply avg-case $K^t$-hardness) routes through a new object, the conditionally-secure entropy-preserving PRG (condEP-PRG).

## Key definitions and results

**Time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity (Section 2.2).** Fix a universal TM $U$ with polynomial emulation overhead. For $\Pi \in \{0,1\}^*$ encoding a pair $(M, w)$, let $U(\Pi, 1^t)$ be the output of $M(w)$ emulated for $t$ steps. Then
$$K^t(x) = \min_{\Pi} \{ |\Pi| : U(\Pi, 1^{t(|x|)}) = x \}.$$
Fact 2.1: there is a constant $c$ such that $K^t(x) \le |x| + c$ for every $t(n) > 0$ and every $x$ (take $\Pi = (M, x)$ with $M$ a constant-length copy machine). This $n + c$ ceiling is load-bearing in both directions.

**Mild average-case hardness (Definition 2.4, Section 2.3).** A function $f$ is $\alpha(\cdot)$-hard-on-average ($\alpha$-HoA) if for every PPT heuristic $\mathcal{H}$ and all sufficiently large $n$, $\Pr[x \leftarrow \{0,1\}^n : \mathcal{H}(x) = f(x)] < 1 - \alpha(|n|)$. $K^t$ is *mildly* HoA if it is $\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}$-HoA for some polynomial $p > 0$ (Section 2.3, end). Definition 2.5 gives the approximate variant ($\beta$-approximate: $\mathcal{H}$ may answer within additive $\beta(|x|)$). Note "mild" here means hardness for at least a $\frac{1}{p(n)}$ fraction of inputs, which is a *weak* hardness requirement; this is what makes the equivalence striking.

**Main Theorem (Theorem 1.1, restated as Theorem 3.1).** The following are equivalent:
(a) OWFs exist.
(b) There exists a polynomial $t(n) > 0$ such that $K^t$ is mildly hard-on-average.
(c) For all constants $d > 0$, $\varepsilon > 0$, and every polynomial $t(n) \ge (1 + \varepsilon)n$, $K^t$ is mildly HoA to $(d \log n)$-approximate.

The proof is (b) $\Rightarrow$ (a) in Section 4, (a) $\Rightarrow$ (c) in Section 5, and (c) $\Rightarrow$ (b) trivially. A consequence (stated below Theorem 3.1): for *every* polynomial $t(n) \ge (1 + \varepsilon)n$ with $\varepsilon > 0$ constant, mild avg-case hardness of $K^t$ is equivalent to OWFs. So the threshold sits just above the input length.

**Theorem 1.2 (hardness of approximation).** If $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is mildly HoA, then for every constant $d$, $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is mildly HoA to $(d \log n)$-approximate. The "trivial" heuristic (output $|x|$, or a $(d \log n)$-approximation with probability $\ge 1 - n^{-d}$, since at most $2^{n - d \log n}$ of $2^n$ strings have $K^t$-complexity below $n - d \log n$) is essentially optimal: any efficient algorithm that beats it even slightly breaks OWFs.

**Decisional and constructive corollaries (Section 1).** Let $\mathrm{MINK}^t[s]$ be the set of $x$ with $K^{t(|x|)}(x) \le s(|x|)$. For every constant $\varepsilon > 0$, every $t(n) \ge (1 + \varepsilon)n$, and $s(n) = n - c \log n$, mild avg-case hardness of the *decisional* language $\mathrm{MINK}^t[s]$ (under uniform instances) is equivalent to OWFs. Also, the *constructive* $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ problem (output a minimal-length generating program $\Pi$, not just the length) is equivalent to the existential one in the average-case regime: the forward proof only needs mild hardness of the constructive variant.

## Techniques and proof ideas

**Direction (b) $\Rightarrow$ (a): OWFs from avg-case $K^t$-hardness (Section 4, Theorem 4.1).** This is the short, elementary direction a builder should internalize first. Let $c$ be the Fact 2.1 constant. Define
$$f(\ell \| \Pi) = \ell \| U(\Pi, 1^{t(n)}),\qquad f : \{0,1\}^{n + c + \lceil \log(n+c)\rceil} \to \{0,1\}^*,$$
where $\ell \in \{0,1\}^{\lceil \log(n+c)\rceil}$ names a length and $\Pi$ is the $\ell$-bit prefix of an $(n+c)$-bit program string $\Pi'$. In words: $f$ interprets its input as "a length $\ell$ plus a program of that length" and runs the program for $t(n)$ steps. A padding trick extends $f$ to all input lengths.

Suppose for contradiction $f$ is invertible: there is a PPT attacker $\mathcal{A}$ inverting $f$ with probability $\ge 1 - \frac{1}{q(m)}$ for $q(n) = 2^{2c+3} n p(n)^2$. Fix a "good" deterministic tape $r$ (averaging) so $\mathcal{A}_r$ inverts with failure $\le \frac{2 p(n)}{q(n)}$. Build the heuristic $\mathcal{H}_r(z)$: run $\mathcal{A}_r(i \| z)$ for all $i \in [n + c]$ and output the length of the shortest program $\Pi$ returned that regenerates $z$ within $t(n)$ steps. Key counting step: for any $z$ with $K^t(z) = w \le n + c$, there is a length-$w$ program $\Pi_z$ generating it, and in the $f$-experiment the value $(w \| z)$ is hit with probability at least $\frac{1}{n+c} \cdot 2^{-w} \ge \frac{1}{n 2^{2c+1}} \cdot 2^{-n}$. If $\mathcal{H}_r$ fails on a set $S$ of measure $\frac{|S|}{2^n}$, then $\mathcal{A}_r$ must fail to invert on those preimages, giving inverter failure $\ge \frac{|S|}{n 2^{2c+1} 2^n} = \frac{\mathrm{fail}_r}{n 2^{2c+1}}$, hence $\mathrm{fail}_r \le \frac{2^{2c+2} n p(n)}{q(n)}$. A union bound over the bad-tape probability $\frac{1}{2p(n)}$ gives total heuristic failure $\le \frac{1}{p(n)}$, so $\mathcal{H}$ computes $K^t$ with probability $1 - \frac{1}{p(n)}$ infinitely often, contradicting mild HoA. Yao's amplification (Theorem 2.3) upgrades the resulting weak OWF to a strong OWF. The intuition: inverting $f$ on $(w \| z)$ *is* finding a short program for $z$, so the inverter is literally a $K^t$-solver, and the $n + c$ ceiling keeps the number of candidate lengths polynomial.

**Direction (a) $\Rightarrow$ (c): avg-case $K^t$-hardness from OWFs (Section 5).** This is the deep direction. The obstacle (Section 1.2, "Avg-case $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$-Hardness from EP-PRGs"): the Kabanets-Cai / Allender-et-al observation (KC00, ABK+06) already shows OWFs imply $K^t$ is *worst-case* hard, because random strings have high $K^t$ with overwhelming probability while PRG outputs always have low $K^t$ (seed plus constant-size code), so a $K^t$-solver distinguishes. But that argument needs the solver to work on *every* input; a heuristic only succeeds on most inputs, and PRG outputs are sparse, so the heuristic may simply fail on them.

The fix is the **entropy-preserving PRG (EP-PRG)** (Section 5.1, Definition 5.1). An EP-PRG $G : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}^{n + \gamma \log n}$ stretches by $O(\log n)$ bits while losing at most $O(\log n)$ bits of *Shannon* entropy: $H(G(\mathcal{U}_n)) \ge n - \alpha \log n$. A **conditionally-secure** EP-PRG (condEP-PRG) only requires the pseudorandomness and entropy-preservation to hold conditioned on some event $E_n$. The two halves of the argument:

1. *condEP-PRG implies $K^t$ is mildly HoA, even to approximate (Theorem 5.2).* Random $m$-bit strings have $K^t(x) \ge m - \frac{\gamma}{4}\log n$ with probability $\ge 1 - n^{-\gamma/4}$ (counting: only $2^{m - \frac{\gamma}{4}\log n}$ short TMs exist, Equation 2). Every PRG output has $K^t(G(s)) \le m - \frac{\gamma}{2}\log n$ deterministically (seed plus constant code, Equation 3). A heuristic $\mathcal{H}$ that $\frac{\gamma}{8}\log n$-approximates $K^t$ would therefore separate the two regimes. The distinguisher $\mathcal{A}(1^n, x)$ outputs 1 iff $\mathcal{H}(x) \ge m - \frac{3}{8}\gamma \log n$. Claim 1: $\mathcal{A}$ outputs 1 on uniform input with probability $\ge 1 - \frac{2}{n^{\gamma/4}}$. Claim 2: $\mathcal{A}$ outputs 1 on $G(\mathcal{U}_n \mid E_n)$ with probability $\le 1 - \frac{1}{n} + \frac{2}{n^{\alpha+\gamma}}$. The entropy-preservation is exactly what bounds the mass on any single "bad" output (via $H_\infty \le H$ and an averaging argument that a $\frac{1}{n}$-fraction of conditioned outputs are "good", i.e. have $-\log \Pr \ge n - \alpha \log n - 1$). For $\gamma \ge 8$ the two probabilities differ by $\ge \frac{1}{n} - \frac{4}{n^2} \ge \frac{1}{n^2}$, contradicting $\mu = 1/n^2$ pseudorandomness. This is the step where *Shannon* entropy (not min-entropy) is the right notion: Lemma 2.2 (a distribution within statistical distance $\frac{1}{n^2}$ of uniform has $H \ge n - 2$) feeds the conditioning argument.

2. *OWFs imply condEP-PRGs (Sections 5.2, 5.3; Theorems 5.5, 5.6).* The construction adapts the standard PRG-from-OWF pipeline. The Blum-Micali-Goldreich-Levin PRG from a one-way *permutation* is automatically entropy-preserving (no entropy loss, output determines input). For *regular* OWFs (Definition 5.3: $2^{r(|x|)-1} \le |f^{-1}(f(x))| \le 2^{r(|x|)}$), the GKL93 / HILL99 / Gol01 / YLW15 construction applies pairwise-independent hashes $h_1, h_2$ as strong extractors to both input and output, parametrized by regularity $r$, plus a Goldreich-Levin hardcore (Equation 1), and is shown (via Leftover Hash Lemma) to be $\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}$-close to uniform, hence Shannon entropy $\ge n - O(\log n)$: an EP-PRG. The crux for *arbitrary* OWFs is **Lemma 5.2**: every OWF is a regular $\mathcal{S}$-OWF over a sufficiently large domain $\mathcal{S}$. Claim 3 (the heart): for every $n$ there is a "regularity level" $r_n \in [n]$ such that the set $S_n = \{x : 2^{r_n - 1} \le |f^{-1}(f(x))| \le 2^{r_n}\}$ has measure $\ge \frac{1}{n}$ (averaging over the $n$ buckets $w(i)$, since $\sum_i w(i) = 1$). Restricting $f$ to $S_n$ makes it regular, and $|S_n| \ge 2^n/n$ keeps it one-way. Conditioning on the event "$i = r(n)$ and $s \in S_{i}$" yields a condEP-PRG from any OWF (the construction $G(i \| s \| \ldots) = G_f^i(\ldots)$ that guesses the regularity level $i$). Finally a "rate-1 efficient" version (running time $n + O(n^\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon < 1$) via a padding trick (Theorem 5.6: output the first $n - \ell$ bits unchanged, apply the condEP-PRG to the last $\ell = n^{1/2c_0}$ bits) lets the argument run for *every* $t(n) \ge (1 + \varepsilon)n$, not just some polynomial $t$. Rate-1 efficiency matters because the random-string $K^t$ lower bound needs $t$ to exceed the PRG's own running time.

**Reusable lemmas for a builder.** Lemma 2.2 (statistical closeness to uniform forces high Shannon entropy). Lemma 5.1 (regular $\mathcal{S}$-OWF yields a dense, pseudorandom hashed construction; re-proved self-contained in Appendix A from the Leftover Hash Lemma and Goldreich-Levin). Lemma 5.2 / Claim 3 (the bucketing argument turning any OWF into a regular OWF over a dense subset). The condEP-PRG abstraction itself (Definition 5.1) is the genuinely new object.

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the *founding* document of the meta-complexity strand the 2050 backward-induction dossier flags as the leading path. The dossier's strand-1 thesis is that "a cheap count captures crypto hardness." Liu-Pass make that precise: the cheap count is $K^t(x)$, the length of the shortest $t$-bounded program for $x$, and its *mild average-case* hardness is provably equivalent to OWFs (Theorem 3.1). This is the cleanest possible instance of meta-complexity (the complexity of computing complexity) being the pivot.

Position within the Williams algorithm-to-lower-bound spine: the forward direction (b $\Rightarrow$ a) is an algorithm-to-hardness reduction in miniature. An efficient algorithm computing $K^t$ on most inputs is mechanically converted into an OWF inverter, hence (contrapositive) cryptographic hardness yields a lower bound against computing $K^t$. This is the same "a fast algorithm for a meta-problem implies a separation" logic that the Williams program runs at the circuit level (#SAT-style algorithm yields $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{ACC}^0$). Liu-Pass run it in the cryptographic / average-case regime instead of the worst-case nonuniform regime.

Barrier-discipline reading (informal; not run through `experiments/_shared/barriers.py`, this is a qualitative note for SURVEYOR, not an ADVERSARY verdict). The forward reduction is a *black-box, relativizing* reduction: $\mathcal{A}$ is used as an oracle by $\mathcal{H}$, and the whole argument is a counting bound over program lengths that would survive oracle access. So in isolation this equivalence does not by itself thread the barriers, and it should not be mistaken for a P vs NP separation technique. Its value to the program is structural: it certifies that the *quantity* $K^t$ is the right meta-complexity invariant to hang hardness on. The dossier's "missing object" (a non-algebrizing, fast-computable invariant, finding #9) is a *worst-case circuit-class* analog of what $K^t$ is here in the average-case crypto setting. Liu-Pass is the existence proof that such an invariant-to-hardness equivalence can be made *unconditional* in at least one regime, which is encouragement for finding the worst-case analog.

Discrepancy / honesty note: the project's barrier discipline is about worst-case separations of complexity classes (P vs NP, $\mathsf{NEXP}$ vs $\mathsf{ACC}^0$). Liu-Pass is about *average-case* cryptographic hardness, which is a strictly different axis (LEARNINGS finding 5: average-case is not worst-case). The equivalence does NOT say anything directly about P vs NP. It says OWFs (Minicrypt) are characterized by $K^t$ avg-case hardness. Conflating "$K^t$ is mildly avg-case hard" with "$\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$" would be an error; OWFs can fail to exist even if $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$. I flag this rather than silently smoothing it.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions:

- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, the MCSP crux).** This is the strongest connection. $K^t$ is the time-bounded Kolmogorov analog of MCSP (the relation is noted via KC00, ABK+06, Tra84). Liu-Pass deliver *unconditionally* what Santhanam (San20) gets only under a new conjecture for MCSP: avg-case hardness of the meta-problem characterizes OWFs. This sharpens the constructivity crux (LEARNINGS finding 3). A natural property is "large + constructive"; here the constructive side is a $K^t$-heuristic, and the theorem says its very existence (beating the trivial baseline) would break crypto. Open question this exposes: is there an *unconditional* MCSP analog of Theorem 3.1, closing the gap to Santhanam's conditional result? And does the $K^t$-to-MCSP translation preserve the average-case equivalence?

- **01 (circuit lower bounds toward P/poly) and the Williams spine.** The forward direction is a template for "meta-problem algorithm implies hardness." Open: can the *worst-case* version (KC00: OWFs imply $K^t$ worst-case hard) be pushed to a circuit lower bound rather than just a cryptographic statement, and does the condEP-PRG machinery have a nonuniform analog? The condEP-PRG is the device that upgraded a worst-case argument (sparse PRG outputs) to an average-case one; the dossier's hinge (the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step) is where an analogous upgrade would have to survive the polynomial method dying at approximate degree $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$.

- **The missing object (LEARNINGS finding 9).** $K^t$ is fast-checkable (given a program, simulate $t$ steps) but the *equivalence* is via a black-box reduction that would algebrize. The dossier wants a fast-computable invariant that does NOT algebrize. Liu-Pass tells us where to look (meta-complexity quantities) and confirms one such quantity ($K^t$) is the crypto pivot, but it does *not* supply a non-algebrizing invariant. That remains the open object.

Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes:
1. Hirahara's open problem (cited as Hir18 in Section 1.1): is there a worst-case (approximation) to average-case reduction for $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ with respect to heuristics that *may err* (not just errorless)? Liu-Pass need the may-err version; Hirahara only gives errorless. Closing this would connect worst-case $K^t$-hardness to OWFs, completing the chain.
2. Does the equivalence extend below the $(1 + \varepsilon)n$ time threshold, or to $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for sublinear stretch? The threshold sits just above input length; what happens at exactly $n$ is left open.
3. Universal extrapolation (IL90, Section 1.1): the paper derives that infeasibility of universal extrapolation is equivalent to mild avg-case hardness of $K^{\mathrm{poly}}$ (since IL90 already tie extrapolation to OWFs). Whether extrapolation w.r.t. *some* poly-time $M$ implies it w.r.t. a *universal* $M$ is left open.
4. Is there an analog characterizing public-key primitives (which OWFs / Minicrypt explicitly exclude, per Imp95)? The paper characterizes only private-key Cryptomania-free primitives. A meta-complexity characterization of public-key crypto would be a separate, harder target.

This anchors strand 1: the "cheap count" is $K^t$, and its mild average-case hardness is unconditionally equivalent to one-way functions. The follow-up program is the worst-case circuit-class translation and the may-err worst-to-average reduction.
