## Summary
This paper shows how to upgrade the entire family of Williams-style algorithmic-method circuit lower bounds from infinitely-often (i.o.) separations to almost-everywhere (a.e.) separations, and additionally to strong average-case hardness, all for the class $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ (deterministic $2^{O(n)}$ time with an $\mathsf{NP}$ oracle). The two engines are (1) a "constructive" version of the Fortnow-Santhanam almost-everywhere sublinear-witness NTIME hierarchy, namely a $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ "refuter" algorithm that explicitly produces a bad input on which any too-fast nondeterministic machine errs (Theorem 1.13 / Theorem 4.6), and (2) a new non-standard XOR lemma based on approximate linear sums of circuits ($\widetilde{\mathsf{Sum}} \circ \mathscr{C}$) that amplifies an $\ell_1$-approximation lower bound directly to strong average-case hardness without a win-win argument (Lemma 3.8 / Theorem 1.14). The headline payoffs are: $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ is a.e. $(1/2 + 2^{-n^\varepsilon})$-inapproximable by $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ (Corollary 1.3), a.e. rigid matrices in $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ (Theorem 1.5), a.e. probabilistic-degree lower bounds (Theorem 1.7), and a near-optimal-seed $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$-computable PRG plus an i.o. NPRG for $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ (Theorems 1.9, 1.10).

## Key definitions and results

**The setting and the two circuit-analysis problems (Section 1.1, p. 2).**
- $\mathsf{CAPP}$ (Circuit Acceptance Probability Problem): given a size-$S$ circuit $C$, estimate $\Pr_x[C(x)=1]$ within additive error $1/S$.
- $\mathsf{Gap\text{-}UNSAT}$: given a circuit $C$, distinguish $C$ unsatisfiable from $C$ having $\ge 2^n/3$ satisfying assignments.
A class $\mathscr{C}$ is *typical* if it is closed under projections and negations (Section 1.1.1, formalized in Section 3.1: given a size-$s$ description, $\lnot C$ and the projection $C(x_1,\dots,x_{i-1}, x_j \oplus b, x_{i+1},\dots,x_n)$ and the substitution of a constant for an input all have size-$s$ circuits with descriptions constructible in $\mathrm{poly}(s)$ time).

**Theorem 1.1 (worst-case a.e. from Gap-UNSAT, p. 2).** There are universal constants $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$, $K \ge 1$ such that the following holds. Let $\mathscr{C}$ be typical and $s(n)$ non-decreasing with $n \le s(n) \le 2^{\varepsilon n}$. If $\mathsf{Gap\text{-}UNSAT}$ on $\mathsf{AND} \circ \mathsf{OR} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuits of size $s(n)^K$ can be solved deterministically in $2^n / n^{\omega(1)}$ time, then there are functions in $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ that do not have $\mathscr{C}$ circuits of size $s(n/2)$, for every sufficiently large $n$. (Note this only needs a non-trivial Gap-UNSAT algorithm, weaker than a full CAPP algorithm.)

**Theorem 1.2 (strong average-case a.e. from CAPP, p. 2).** Let $\mathscr{C}$ be typical. Suppose there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $\mathsf{CAPP}$ of $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size $\mathsf{AND}_4 \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuits can be solved deterministically in $2^{n - n^\varepsilon}$ time. Then there is a language $L \in \mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ and a constant $\delta > 0$ such that for every sufficiently large $n$, $L_n$ cannot be $(1/2 + 2^{-n^\delta})$-approximated by $\mathscr{C}$ circuits of size $2^{n^\delta}$. (Here a function is $(1/2+\varepsilon)$-approximated by a class if some circuit from the class agrees on at least a $(1/2+\varepsilon)$ fraction of inputs; $L_n$ is the restriction to $n$-bit inputs.)

**Corollary 1.3 (a.e. strong average-case for $\mathsf{ACC}^0 \circ \mathsf{THR}$, Section 1.1.2, p. 3).** Combining Theorem 1.2 with the Williams #SAT algorithm for $\mathsf{ACC}^0 \circ \mathsf{THR}$ (Theorem 3.12, citing the journal version [Wil18a]): for every $d, m \in \mathbb{N}$ there is $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{d,m}$ and $L \in \mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ such that $L_n$ cannot be $(1/2 + 2^{-n^\varepsilon})$-approximated by $\mathsf{AC}^0_d[m] \circ \mathsf{THR}$ circuits of $2^{n^\varepsilon}$ size, for every sufficiently large $n$. The paper stresses (p. 3) that prior bounds (Williams worst-case [Wil14], Rajgopal-Santhanam-Srinivasan / Viola [Vio20] $(1/2 + 1/n^{1-\varepsilon})$ against $\mathsf{AC}^0[\oplus]$, Chen-Ren [CR20] sub-half-exponential average-case against $\mathsf{ACC}^0$) are all only i.o. and strictly weaker on the average-case axis.

**Definition 1.4 / Theorem 1.5 (a.e. rigid matrices in $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$, p. 3).** The $r$-rigidity $\mathcal{R}_M(r)$ is the minimum Hamming distance from $M$ to a rank-$\le r$ matrix. Theorem 1.5: there is $\delta > 0$ such that for all finite fields $\mathbb{F}$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ algorithm that on $1^n$ outputs an $n \times n$ matrix $H$ with $\mathcal{R}_H(2^{\log^{1-\varepsilon} n}) \ge \delta n^2$, for all large enough $n$. This is the a.e. strengthening of the i.o. constructions of Alman-Chen [AC19] and Bhangale et al. [BHPT20].

**Definition 1.6 / Theorem 1.7 / Theorem 1.8 (a.e. probabilistic-degree bounds, p. 4).** The $\varepsilon$-error probabilistic degree of $f$ is the minimum $d$ such that some distribution $\mathcal{D}$ on degree-$\le d$ $\mathbb{F}_2$-polynomials has $\Pr_{P \sim \mathcal{D}}[P(x) \ne f(x)] \le \varepsilon$ for all $x$. Theorem 1.7: there is $L \in \mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ with $1/3$-error probabilistic degree $\ge \Omega(n / \log^2 n)$ for every sufficiently large $n$ (a.e. version of Viola [Vio20]). Theorem 1.8: for all $\delta > 0$ there is $L \in \mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ such that $L_n$ cannot be $(1/2 + 2^{-n^{\Omega(1)}})$-approximated by $n^{1/2-\delta}$-degree $\mathbb{F}_2$-polynomials, a.e. (improving the $1/2 + n^{-\delta}$ correlation bound of Razborov-Smolensky; the tradeoff is tight for Majority [Vio19]).

**Theorems 1.9, 1.10 (PRG and NPRG, Section 1.1.3, p. 4).** Theorem 1.9: for all constants $d, m$ there is $\delta > 0$ and an $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$-computable PRG taking an $n$-bit seed and outputting a $2^{n^\delta}$-bit string fooling $\mathsf{AC}^0_d[m]$ circuits of size $2^{n^\delta}$ (near-optimal $\mathrm{polylog}$ seed in the output-length sense). Theorem 1.10: an i.o. NPRG with the same fooling, running in $2^{O(n)}$ time, which resolves an open question of Chen-Ren [CR20]. The PRG follows from Corollary 1.3 via Nisan-Wigderson (Lemma 3.5); the NPRG uses Umans' PRG (Lemma 3.6).

**Theorem 1.12 / 1.13 (the a.e. sublinear-witness NTIME hierarchy with a refuter, Section 1.2.1, p. 5).** Recall $\mathsf{NTIMEGUESS}[t(n), g(n)]$ is the class of languages decided by nondeterministic algorithms running in $O(t(n))$ steps and guessing at most $g(n)$ witness bits (Definition 4.1). Theorem 1.12 (Fortnow-Santhanam [FS16]): for every time-constructible $T(n)$ with $n \le T(n) \le 2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}$, $\mathsf{NTIME}[T(n)] \not\subseteq \text{i.o.-}\mathsf{NTIMEGUESS}[o(T(n)), n/10]$. Theorem 1.13 (the new ingredient, refuter): for every such $T(n)$, there is $L \in \mathsf{NTIME}[T(n)]$ and an algorithm $\mathcal{R}$ that, on input $(M, 1^n)$ where $M$ runs in $o(T(n))$ time and guesses $\le n/10$ bits, outputs (for every fixed $M$ and sufficiently large $n$) a string $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ with $M(x) \ne L(x)$, running in $\mathrm{poly}(T(n))$ time with adaptive $\mathsf{SAT}$ oracle access. This is the formal statement (Theorem 4.6) at the heart of all the a.e. upgrades.

**Theorem 1.14 / Lemma 3.8 (the new non-standard XOR lemma, Section 1.2.2 and 3.4, p. 6 and 16).** Define $f^{\oplus k}(x_1,\dots,x_k) = \bigoplus_{i=1}^k f(x_i)$ (Definition 3.7). $\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ is the class of real linear combinations $C(x) = \sum_i \alpha_i C_i(x)$ with $C_i \in \mathscr{C}$, complexity $\max(\sum_i |\alpha_i|, \sum_i \mathrm{SIZE}(C_i))$; a $[0,1]\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuit additionally satisfies $C(x) \in [0,1]$ for all $x$ (Definition 3.1). Lemma 3.8: for $\delta < 1/2$, $\varepsilon_k = (1-\delta)^{k-1}(1/2 - \delta)$; if $f$ cannot be $(1-\delta)$-approximated in $\ell_1$ distance by $[0,1]\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuits of complexity $O(s \cdot n / (\delta \varepsilon_k)^2)$, then $f^{\oplus k}$ cannot be $(1/2 + \varepsilon_k)$-approximated by size-$s$ $\mathscr{C}$ circuits. The crux of this XOR lemma (versus Yao / Levin / Goldreich-Nisan-Wigderson) is the asymmetry: the *hypothesis* is an $\ell_1$-approximation lower bound against linear sums, and the lemma only ever uses circuits $C$ satisfying the $[0,1]$ promise on the cube. The proof is by induction on $k$, following Levin's proof, with the case analysis "some $T(y)$ far from $1/2$" (reduce to $k-1$) versus "all $T(y)$ close to $1/2$" (Chernoff over $O((\varepsilon\delta)^{-2} n)$ circuits to build the predictor $\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuit $D$, then rescale to $D'$). Full proof in Appendix A (not read line-by-line).

**The two inherent barriers to the prior win-win approach (Section 2.2.2, p. 13).** Lemma 2.1 recaps Chen-Ren's [CR20] win-win hardness amplification ($\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ cannot be $(1/2 + 1/\mathrm{poly}(n))$-approximated by $\mathsf{ACC}^0$). Two barriers are identified that prevent it reaching a.e. strong average-case bounds: (1) an *infinitely-often barrier* (the win-win only yields i.o. lower bounds, because the "$F$ is i.o. average-case easy" branch only gives an $\mathsf{NC}^1 \subseteq \widetilde{\mathsf{Sum}} \circ \mathsf{ACC}^0$ collapse on infinitely many lengths); and (2) a *half-sub-exponential barrier* (a careful analysis forces the hardness function $g$ in a $1/2 + 1/g(n)$ bound against $g(n)$-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ to satisfy $g(g(n)^{O(1)}) \le 2^{n^{o(1)}}$, so $g$ is half-sub-exponential, footnote 9). The new direct-amplification XOR-lemma route is what sidesteps both.

**The two PCP systems used (Section 3.5, p. 17).** Lemma 3.10 ([BV14]): an extremely efficient PCP where the query map $Q$ is a projection and the verifier predicate $R$ is a 3CNF, proof length $2^r \le T \cdot \mathrm{polylog}\, T$. Lemma 3.11 ([CW19, VW20], a PCPP / probabilistically checkable proof of proximity): given a size-$m$ circuit $D$ on $n$ inputs, outputs a 2-SAT instance $F$ on variable sets $\mathcal{Y} \cup \mathcal{Z}$ where $\mathrm{Enc}_i(x)$ is a parity of at most $n/2$ bits of $x$ (an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear error-correcting code), with completeness $c_{\mathsf{pcpp}}$ and soundness $s_{\mathsf{pcpp}}$, the number of clauses a power of 2. The PCPP is what makes the average-case argument go through; the encoding being $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear and low-locality is used heavily in Section 6.

**Theorem 3.12 (the non-trivial #SAT algorithm, Section 3.6, p. 18).** ([Wil18a]) For every $d, m$ there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the satisfying assignments of a $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size $n$-input $\mathsf{AC}^0_d[m] \circ \mathsf{THR}$ circuit can be counted deterministically in $2^{n - n^\varepsilon}$ time. This is the algorithmic input that instantiates Theorem 1.2 into Corollary 1.3.

## Techniques and proof ideas

**1. The refuter as a constructive NTIME hierarchy (Sections 2.1, 4).** The original Williams i.o. lower bound (reviewed in Section 2.1.1) constructs a "cheating" nondeterministic algorithm $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ that uses PCPs plus a non-trivial Gap-UNSAT algorithm to try to speed up a hard language $L^{\mathsf{hard}} \in \mathsf{NTIME}[2^n] \setminus \mathsf{NTIME}[2^n/n]$. Because $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ runs in $o(2^n/n)$ time, the NTIME hierarchy forces them to differ on infinitely many lengths, giving an i.o. separation. The new idea (p. 8 to 9): $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ guesses only $2^{n^{o(1)}}$ bits, so it lives in $\mathsf{NTIMEGUESS}[o(T(n)), n/10]$, and one can use the Fortnow-Santhanam a.e. *sublinear-witness* hierarchy (Theorem 1.12) instead of the general NTIME hierarchy. The remaining problem is "input and advice": the bad input $x_n$ used to be appended as advice, which would only give hardness in $|x_2|$, a trivial bound. The fix is to *generate* $x_n$ algorithmically via a refuter $\mathcal{R}$ (Theorem 4.6), then use a SAT oracle to find a lexicographically-first correct PCP oracle and output it. The refuter is built (Sections 4.2, 4.3) by opening up the Fortnow-Santhanam diagonalizing language $\mathcal{A}^T_{\mathsf{FS}}$ (Definition 4.2), which orders all inputs $x_i^{(n)} = \langle M, n, w_i^{(n)} \rangle$ lexicographically by witness $w_i^{(n)}$; the proof of Theorem 1.12 shows a differing input must exist in this ordered list, and a *binary search* (Lemma 4.4 / Corollary 4.5: $O(\log n)$ queries to a list where the two endpoints differ) over the list, with each list entry computed via a SAT oracle, locates it in $\mathrm{poly}(T(n))$ time. This is the single most reusable gadget: it converts the existence proof of the a.e. NTIME hierarchy into an explicit $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ "bad-input finder." Section 4.4 / Theorem 4.8 gives a "robustly often" variant $\mathcal{R}^T_{\mathsf{RO}}$ (no witness-length restriction, output length in $[n, n+T(n)]$), needed for rigid matrices.

**2. Worst-case a.e. lower bound (Section 5).** Combines the refuter with the PCP of Lemma 3.10. $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ applies the PCP to $\mathcal{A}^T_{\mathsf{FS}}$, guesses a small $\mathscr{C}$ circuit $C$ as the PCP oracle (describable in $\le n/10$ bits), and uses the Gap-UNSAT algorithm to verify $\mathsf{VPCP}^C$ is a tautology. Lemma 5.1 records that $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ makes only one-sided errors ($\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}(z) \le \mathcal{A}^T_{\mathsf{FS}}(z)$). The hard $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ language $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{HARD}}$ on input $y$ of length $m$: set $n = 2^{m/2C}$, use the refuter to find $z$ with $\mathcal{A}^T_{\mathsf{FS}}(z) \ne \mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}(z)$ (forced to $\mathcal{A}^T_{\mathsf{FS}}(z)=1, \mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}(z)=0$ by one-sidedness), then use a SAT oracle to find the lexicographically-first correct oracle $\mathcal{O}$ (which provably has no size-$s(\ell)$ $\mathscr{C}$ circuit, else $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCP}}$ would have guessed it), and output $f_m(y) = \mathcal{O}(y_{\le \ell})$.

**3. Direct average-case amplification with PCPP (Section 6).** This is the technically heaviest part. The algorithm $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCPP}}$ guesses both a small circuit $C$ (PCP oracle) *and* a list of $\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ proof circuits $(Y, Z)$ for the PCPP of Lemma 3.11. Because one cannot verify the guessed circuits really satisfy the $[0,1]$ promise, a *validity test* (Section 6.2.2) is applied: estimate $\mathbb{E}_x P_{ij}(x)$ and $\mathbb{E}_x Q_{ij}(x)$ (degree-4 polynomials in the proof indicators $T_{ij}$) via the Average-of-Product machinery, accepting only if $\mathbb{E}[P] \le 2\delta$ and $\mathbb{E}[Q] \le 1 + \delta$. Lemma 6.2 is the engine: a non-trivial CAPP algorithm for $\mathsf{AND}_4 \circ \mathscr{C}$ lets one compute the Average-of-Product of four $\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuits within additive error $S(n)^4/E(n)$. The key conceptual point (Section 2.2, equation (1)): linear sums *preserve algorithms*, so $\mathbb{E}_x[\sum_i \alpha_i C_i(x)] = \sum_i \alpha_i \mathbb{E}_x[C_i(x)]$ is computable from a #SAT/CAPP algorithm for $\mathscr{C}$, and the inapproximability argument only needs the sum to $\ell_1$-approximate $f$, not to approximate it pointwise. Lemma 6.3 (completeness/soundness of the validity test, proved in Section 6.4 via the arithmetization $\widetilde{\mathsf{Cons}}_i$, Fact 6.6, Claim 3, and Cauchy-Schwarz) certifies that a passing proof is $\ell_1$-close to a genuine Boolean PCPP proof. The conflicting input from the refuter then yields (Section 6.2.4, Case 2 / Lemma 6.5) a function $f$ that is $\delta$-far in $\ell_1$ from every low-complexity $[0,1]\mathsf{Sum} \circ \mathscr{C}$ circuit, and Lemma 3.8 (the XOR lemma) amplifies this directly to a $(1/2 + 2^{-\ell^{\Omega(1)}})$ average-case lower bound. The final hard language $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{avgHARD}}$ (Section 6.2.5) pads back up to length $m$.

**4. PRG and NPRG (Sections 6.5, 7).** The $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$-computable PRG (Theorem 6.8 / 1.9) feeds the average-case-hard $L_n$ from Corollary 1.3 into the Nisan-Wigderson PRG (Lemma 3.5), using that $\mathsf{AC}^0_d[m] \circ \mathsf{Junta}_a$ circuits reduce to $\mathsf{AC}^0_{d+2}[m]$ for which Theorem 3.12 gives a #SAT algorithm. The i.o. NPRG (Theorem 7.1 / 1.10) defines $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{fast}}$ (a unary-language analog of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCPP}}$), splits on whether a small tautology-certifying circuit exists, and in each case uses either Umans' PRG (Lemma 3.6) or NW with the locally-hard function.

**5. Rigid matrices (Section 8.1, read to 8.1.2).** Uses the "robustly often" refuter $\mathcal{R}^T_{\mathsf{RO}}$ (Theorem 4.8) rather than the sublinear-witness one, because guessing a low-rank matrix $A \cdot B$ as a proof needs $\Omega(\sqrt{T(n)})$ bits, which is not sublinear. It combines this with a fast #OV algorithm (Lemma 8.2) and the rectangular PCP of Bhangale et al. (Lemma 8.3), guessing the low-rank factors $A, B$ as the "almost-rectangular" PCP proof. (The completion of this construction and Theorems 1.7/1.8 on probabilistic degree were skimmed.)

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the *robustness layer* on the Williams spine and is directly the focus item for this source: it shows the precise sense in which a strand-1 lower-bound count must be robust. A counting/algorithmic argument that yields hardness on only infinitely many lengths is, in the authors' own framing (Section 1, "The Infinitely-Often Barrier in Complexity Theory," p. 1), "practically not very different from $\mathsf{NQP} \subset \mathsf{ACC}^0$" when the hard lengths are sparse (e.g. $n = 2^{2^{2^k}}$). The dossier's leading path (meta-complexity riding the Williams algorithm-to-lower-bound spine) inherits an implicit requirement: any future $\mathsf{TC}^0$ or $\mathsf{P/poly}$ count had better be a.e. and average-case, and this paper is the template for getting there *given* a sufficiently good circuit-analysis algorithm.

Barrier-discipline connections:
- The whole construction is non-relativizing and non-algebrizing for the same structural reason as Williams [Wil14]: it crucially opens the black box, both through the non-trivial Gap-UNSAT/CAPP/#SAT algorithms (Theorem 3.12 reads circuit internals) and through the PCP/PCPP arithmetization. It inherits the barrier profile of the algorithmic method rather than introducing a new evasion mechanism. This note does *not* run the construction through `experiments/_shared/barriers.py`; a scorecard should treat the underlying technique as "arithmetization + #SAT algorithm" (the Williams ACC0 profile already pinned by the smoke test) and verify that the a.e. and average-case upgrades do not re-introduce a relativizing or natural-proofs step.
- The non-standard XOR lemma (Lemma 3.8) is interesting from a natural-proofs standpoint: it is a hardness-amplification result whose hypothesis is an $\ell_1$-approximation lower bound against *linear sums* of circuits, and the "preserve algorithms" property (equation (1)) is exactly the bridge between a #SAT upper bound for $\mathscr{C}$ and a CAPP upper bound for $\widetilde{\mathsf{Sum}} \circ \mathscr{C}$. This is squarely in the algorithm-to-lower-bound idiom and does not appear to introduce a large+constructive property in the Razborov-Rudich sense.

## What this enables / what remains open

**Maps to research directions (01 to 04):**
- **01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** Primary. This paper closes the i.o.-versus-a.e. gap and the worst-case-versus-average-case gap for $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ against $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ (and $\mathsf{ACC}^0 \circ \mathsf{THR}$). It gives the exact recipe a strand-1 result should follow: a non-trivial CAPP/#SAT algorithm for $\mathsf{AND}_4 \circ \mathscr{C}$ plus typicality of $\mathscr{C}$ yields an *a.e. strong average-case* $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ lower bound, no win-win needed (Theorem 1.2). For the dossier $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge: the paper reaches $\mathsf{ACC}^0 \circ \mathsf{THR}$ (a bottom threshold layer) because Theorem 3.12 gives a #SAT algorithm there, but it does *not* claim full $\mathsf{TC}^0$, consistent with the hinge being open. The reduction is now sharply conditional: produce the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ CAPP/#SAT algorithm and the a.e. average-case lower bound follows mechanically.
- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, MCSP crux):** Indirect. The "linear sums preserve algorithms" idea and the validity-test-as-meta-algorithm are meta-complexity in flavor; the average-case hardness against $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ is the kind of result MCSP-style approaches aim to exploit. The XOR lemma's reliance on the $[0,1]\mathsf{Sum}$ promise is a place an adversary should probe for any hidden constructivity.
- **03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP):** Not directly addressed; PCP/PCPP machinery is used as a tool, not a target.
- **04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions):** Not addressed; this is firmly the algorithmic side.

**Maps to LEARNINGS findings:**
- Finding (6) (Williams is the existence proof that barriers are passable): directly extended. The a.e. and average-case upgrades show the passable region is larger than first realized.
- Finding (8) (futures converge on the Williams spine): this paper is a load-bearing piece of the spine, supplying the robustness the dossier's 2050 count needs.
- Finding (5) (average-case is not worst-case): the entire Section 2.2.2 barrier discussion and the new XOR lemma are about exactly this gap; the paper documents *why* the previous win-win route could not deliver a.e. strong average-case hardness (the i.o. barrier and the half-sub-exponential barrier) and how the direct-amplification route avoids both.
- Finding (3) (constructivity crux is MCSP): the refuter is a constructive object (it explicitly produces the bad input), and the validity test is a constructive meta-algorithm; both are adjacent to the MCSP family.
- Finding (1) (barriers compose) and (4) (relativization is finite/mechanical): the construction stacks PCP, PCPP, refuter, and XOR lemma, and each layer must individually respect the barrier discipline; the refuter's binary-search core (Lemma 4.4) is a finite, mechanical object.
- Finding (9) (the missing object is a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant): worth flagging for the BUILDER/ADVERSARY. The quantity $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{PCPP}}$ actually computes is an Average-of-Product of arithmetized PCPP proofs (a rational expectation of degree-2/degree-4 polynomials), which is exactly the kind of rational trace/rank/volume invariant the dossier says *algebrizes*. This paper does not produce the conjectured non-algebrizing mod-2 torsion "Bockstein bridge"; it stays within the algebrizing-invariant world, which is why it remains conditioned on a $\mathsf{TC}^0$ circuit-analysis algorithm that does not yet exist.

**Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes:**
1. The biggest open problem flagged in the paper itself (Abstract; Section 1, p. 1): is $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}} \subseteq \text{i.o.-}\mathsf{ACC}^0$ false? Equivalently, is $\mathsf{NEXP} \subset \text{i.o.-}\mathsf{NP}$? The a.e. results here are for $\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ via the *sublinear-witness* hierarchy, sidestepping the still-open full a.e. NTIME hierarchy ($\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n] \subset \text{i.o.-}\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n/n]$ is open, BFS75-style oracle obstruction noted on p. 2).
2. Can the $\mathsf{ACC}^0 \circ \mathsf{THR}$ result be pushed to full $\mathsf{TC}^0$? This requires a non-trivial #SAT or CAPP algorithm for $\mathsf{AND}_4 \circ \mathsf{TC}^0$, the dossier hinge. The paper makes this dependency explicit and mechanical (Theorem 1.2 plus Theorem 3.12), so the only missing piece is the algorithm.
3. Can the half-sub-exponential ceiling (footnote 9, Section 2.2.2) on the win-win approach be tightened or is the new XOR-lemma route strictly stronger in all regimes? The paper shows the new route avoids both win-win barriers but the comparison is qualitative.
4. The rigid-matrix and probabilistic-degree applications (Section 8, skimmed) are stated as a.e. theorems; a careful reader should confirm the parameter regimes (e.g. the $\sqrt{T(n)}$ witness-length obstruction that forced the "robustly often" refuter, Section 8.1.2) match the dossier's needs.
