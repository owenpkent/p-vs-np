## Summary
The paper proves that the constructivity axiom of natural proofs is *unavoidable* for circuit lower bounds, even weak ones, by exhibiting exact equivalences between lower bounds and the existence of efficiently-computable useful (but not necessarily large) properties. The headline result (Theorems 1.1 and 1.2) is that for every typical circuit class $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathcal{C}$ if and only if there is a polynomial-time algorithm (equivalently a $\mathsf{P}/O(\log n)$ property of Boolean functions) that is *useful* against $\mathcal{C}$. A second axis (Theorem 1.7) shows the nonexistence of $\mathsf{P}$-natural useful properties against $\mathcal{C}$ is equivalent to a strong derandomization: $\mathsf{ZPE}$ having "$\mathcal{C}$ seeds." The machinery yields new $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ lower bounds (Theorems 1.3, 1.4), the win-win Theorem 1.8 ($\mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}] \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^c]$ or $\mathsf{BPP}$ has zero-error subexp simulation), and the equivalence $\mathsf{NEXP} \ne \mathsf{BPP}$ iff $\mathsf{BPP}$ has nontrivial nondeterministic (heuristic ZPTIME) simulations (Theorem 1.9).

## Key definitions and results

**Typical classes and usefulness (Section 1, p. 2).** A non-uniform circuit class $\mathcal{C}$ is *typical* if $\mathcal{C} \in \{\mathsf{AC}^0, \mathsf{ACC}^0, \mathsf{TC}^0, \mathsf{NC}^1, \mathsf{NC}, \mathsf{P/poly}\}$. A property of Boolean functions $\mathcal{P}$ is *useful against* $\mathcal{C}$ if for all $k$, there are infinitely many $n$ such that (i) $\mathcal{P}(f)$ is true of at least one $f : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$, and (ii) $\mathcal{P}(g)$ is false for every $g$ having $n^k$-size $\mathcal{C}$-circuits. So on infinitely many lengths, $\mathcal{P}$ distinguishes *some* function from all easy ones. Usefulness is the third natural-proofs axiom in isolation, with largeness and constructivity removed (the abstract: a $\mathsf{P}$-useful constructive property is exactly two of the three natural-proofs conditions).

**Theorem 1.1 (p. 2).** For all typical $\mathcal{C}$: $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathcal{C}$ if and only if there is a polynomial-time computable property of Boolean functions that is useful against $\mathcal{C}$ with $O(\log n)$ bits of advice. Equivalently, $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathcal{C}$ iff there is a language in $\mathsf{P}/O(\log n)$ defining a useful property.

**Theorem 1.2 (p. 3).** For all typical $\mathcal{C}$: $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathcal{C}$ if and only if there is a polynomial-time *algorithm* (over arbitrary-length strings, no advice) that is useful against $\mathcal{C}$. The advice of Theorem 1.1 is removed by relaxing "property of Boolean functions" (defined only on $2^n$-length truth tables) to a *natural algorithm* operating on all string lengths (Definition 2.1, p. 9): pad string $x$ to length a power of two and read it as a truth table $f_x$.

Williams stresses (p. 3) that Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 explain *why* even $\mathsf{NEXP}$ lower bounds are hard: any $\mathsf{NEXP}$ lower bound must exhibit *constructivity* and *usefulness* (two of the three natural-proofs conditions). Adding largeness would likely make the algorithm impossible to construct. Footnote 4 (p. 3) notes the converse question (do non-constructive large properties imply new lower bounds) does not seem interesting, since $\mathsf{coNP}$-natural useful properties against $\mathsf{P/poly}$ already exist trivially.

**Theorem 1.3 (p. 3) and its quantified form (p. 17).** For all $d, m$ there is an $\varepsilon = 1/m^{\Theta(d)} > 0$ such that $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ does not have $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size depth-$d$ $\mathsf{AC}^0[m]$ *witnesses*. That is, there are $\mathsf{NEXP}$ languages whose verifiers only accept witness strings of exponentially high $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuit complexity. Williams flags the asymmetry (p. 3): we can prove exponential witness-size lower bounds for $\mathsf{NEXP}$ even though the best language lower bound for $\mathsf{NEXP}$ against $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ is only "third-exponential."

**Theorem 1.4 (p. 4).** $\mathsf{NE} \cap \textit{io-}\mathsf{coNE}$ and $\mathsf{NE}/1 \cap \mathsf{coNE}/1$ do not have $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits of $n^{\log n}$ size. These are intriguing because they must be proved *differently*: the Williams [Wil11] $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{ACC}^0$ proof relies on a tight nondeterministic time hierarchy (Zak [Z83]), but the semantic classes $\mathsf{NTIME} \cap \mathsf{coNTIME}$ are not known to have such a hierarchy or complete languages. The proof of Theorem 1.4 instead builds on Theorem 1.1 and a modification of the $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ lower bound. Footnote 5 (p. 4) records a correction: the conference version claimed $\mathsf{NE} \cap \mathsf{coNE}$; the journal/arXiv version proves $\mathsf{NE} \cap \textit{io-}\mathsf{coNE}$ (credit to Impagliazzo and Oliveira [Oli13]).

**Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 (p. 4, proofs p. 20-21).** Generic SAT-to-lower-bound: if SAT for $n^{O(\log^c n)}$-size $\mathcal{C}$-circuits is solvable in $O(2^n/n^{10})$ time for all $c$, then $\mathsf{NE} \cap \textit{io-}\mathsf{coNE}$ and $\mathsf{NE}/1 \cap \mathsf{coNE}/1$ lack $n^{\log n}$-size $\mathcal{C}$ circuits (1.5). Theorem 1.6 weakens the hypothesis to a $1/6$-approximation of acceptance probability (a CAPP-style algorithm) in $O(2^n/n^{10})$ nondeterministic time.

**$\mathcal{C}$ seeds and Theorem 1.7 (p. 4, full proof p. 21-23).** $\mathsf{ZPE} = \mathsf{ZPTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$, $\mathsf{RE} = \mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$. $\mathsf{ZPE}$ *has $\mathcal{C}$ seeds* if every $\mathsf{ZPE}$ predicate $M$ has, for each $x$, a polynomial-size $\mathcal{C}$-circuit $C_x$ such that $M(x, tt(C_x)) \ne {?}$ (Definition 5.1, p. 21): the truth table of a small circuit always suffices as a successful seed. Theorem 1.7: for typical $\mathcal{C}$, the following are equivalent: (1) there are no $\mathsf{P}$-natural properties useful (resp. ae-useful) against $\mathcal{C}$; (2) $\mathsf{ZPE}$ has $\mathcal{C}$ seeds for almost all (resp. infinitely many) input lengths. Direction $\neg(1) \Rightarrow \neg(2)$ amplifies a $\mathsf{P}$-natural useful property into a $\mathsf{ZPE}$ predicate that accepts random strings with decent probability but lacks small seeds. Direction $\neg(2) \Rightarrow \neg(1)$ turns the absence of small seeds into a high-circuit-complexity hitting set, and "check for a hitting set" becomes the $\mathsf{P}$-natural useful property (via Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 1).

The discussion on p. 5 draws the key barrier reading: the *lack* of $\mathcal{D}$-natural properties implies strong derandomizations of randomized exponential size $\mathcal{D}$. And (p. 5, second paragraph) proving that a canonical $\mathsf{ZPE}$ derandomization exists would imply no $\mathsf{P}$-natural properties useful against $\mathsf{P/poly}$, hence $\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$. So some derandomization problems are as hard as $\mathsf{P}$ vs $\mathsf{NP}$.

**Theorem 1.8 (win-win, p. 5, proof p. 25-26).** Either $\mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}] \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^c]$ for some $c$, or $\mathsf{BPP} \subset \textit{io-}\mathsf{ZPTIME}[2^{n^\varepsilon}]/n^\varepsilon$ for all $\varepsilon > 0$. Either randomized exptime is easy with non-uniform circuits, or two-sided-error randomized computation has a zero-error subexponential simulation with $n^\varepsilon$ advice. **Corollary 1.1 (p. 6):** for some $c > 0$, $\mathsf{RP} \subset \textit{io-}\mathsf{ZPSUBEXP}/n^c$ (error removed in subexp time with fixed-poly advice infinitely often, advice independent of the RP running time). **Corollary 1.2 (p. 6, proof p. 26-27):** $\mathsf{AM} \subseteq \textit{io-}\Sigma_2\mathsf{SUBEXP}/n^c$ for some $c$.

**Theorem 1.9 (p. 6, proof p. 27-28).** $\mathsf{NEXP} \ne \mathsf{BPP}$ if and only if for all $\varepsilon > 0$, $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \textit{io-}\mathsf{HeuristicZPTIME}[2^{n^\varepsilon}]/n^\varepsilon$. Remark 1 (p. 28, from an anonymous reviewer): equivalently, $\mathsf{NEXP} \ne \mathsf{BPP}$ iff $\mathsf{BPP} \subseteq \textit{io-}\mathsf{HeuristicNTIME}[2^{n^\varepsilon}]/n^\varepsilon$, so separating $\mathsf{NEXP}$ from $\mathsf{BPP}$ is equivalent to a nontrivial *nondeterministic* simulation of $\mathsf{BPP}$.

**Theorem 1.10 (p. 6, proof p. 28-29).** If there is an $\mathsf{RP}$-natural property useful against $\mathcal{C}$, then there is a $\mathsf{P}$-natural property useful against $\mathcal{C}$. The deterministic $P'$ uses $P$ as a subroutine; the key idea is to *swap the input with the randomness* in $P$.

**Preliminary tools (Section 2).** Truth-table encoding for non-power-of-two strings via $f_T$ (p. 7-8). Proposition 1 (p. 8): circuit complexity of a substring is bounded by that of the whole string. Lemma 2.1 (p. 8): a universal $c \ge 1$ such that $CC(f_S) \le CC(f_T) + (c \log |T|)$ for any substring $S$ of $T$ (and depth/modulus-bounded versions with a $(c\log|T|)^{1+o(1)}$ overhead). Umans Theorem 2.1 [Uma03] (p. 9): a hardness-based PRG $G$ that, for any $Y$ with $CC(Y) \ge s^g$, fools size-$s$ circuits with seed length $g\log|Y|$ in $\mathrm{poly}(|Y|)$ time. The Natural Proofs definitions (p. 9) restate Constructivity, Largeness ($1/2^{O(n)}$ fraction), Usefulness, and Definition 2.1 generalizes to *natural algorithms* on arbitrary-length inputs (Constructivity: $L(A) \in \Gamma$; Largeness: $A$ accepts $\ge 1/n^{O(1)}$ of $n$-bit strings; Usefulness: i.o. $A$ accepts some $x$ and rejects all $n^k$-size truth tables).

**Related work and the IKW problem (Section 2.2, p. 10, Claim 2.1, Theorem 2.2).** IKW [IKW02] proved one direction (NP-natural without largeness implies $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$) and posed: does a $\mathsf{P}$-natural property useful against $\mathsf{P/poly}$ imply $\mathsf{EXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$? Claim 2.1 (p. 11): if there is no $\mathsf{P}$-natural property useful against $\mathsf{P/poly}$, then $\mathsf{NP} \ne \mathsf{ZPP}$. Theorem 2.2 (p. 11): an affirmative answer to the IKW problem would prove $\mathsf{EXP} \ne \mathsf{ZPP}$ unconditionally. Chow [Cho11] (almost-natural proofs) and Allender [All01] are the closest prior relatives.

## Techniques and proof ideas
The reusable methods a builder would extract:

1. **Witnesses-versus-languages equivalence (Theorem 3.1, p. 12-13).** For typical poly-size $\mathcal{C}$, the three statements are equivalent: $\mathsf{NEXP} \subset \mathcal{C}$; $\mathsf{NEXP}$ has $\mathcal{C}$ witnesses; $\mathsf{NEXP}$ has *oblivious* $\mathcal{C}$ witnesses (a single circuit $C_n$ encodes witnesses for all length-$n$ inputs). The $(2) \Rightarrow (3)$ step merges witnesses for all inputs of a length into one witness via a counting predicate $V'$ that depends only on $|x|$, not $x$. The $(3) \Rightarrow (1)$ step bootstraps: build a predicate $V_k$ whose witnesses encode accepting histories, then apply the oblivious-witness assumption to $V_k$ itself. This strengthens IKW [IKW02], which had only $(1) \Rightarrow (2)$ for $\mathcal{C} = \mathsf{P/poly}$.

2. **Witness lower bounds to constructive useful properties (Theorem 3.2, p. 14-15).** For size functions $s(n) \in [n^2, 2^n/(2n)]$, the equivalence: $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ lacks $s(cn)$-size witness $\mathcal{C}$-circuits (some $c$) iff there is a $\mathsf{P}/(\log n)$-computable property useful against $s(cn)$-size $\mathcal{C}$ iff there is a polynomial-time algorithm useful against $s(cn)$-size $\mathcal{C}$. The forward direction reads the property off a "bad" input $x'_i$ of a good predicate whose witnesses need large circuits; the advice $x_k$ encodes which bad input. Removing the advice is done by an explicit padding trick: the algorithm $A(y)$ rejects unless $y = z01^k$, recovers $k$ from trailing ones, and there is *exactly one* input length per $(k, \ell)$ on which the bad input recurs.

3. **The universal "$P^\star$" property via Succinct Halting (Theorem 3.3, p. 15-16).** To collapse a family of properties $\{P_k\}$ (one per size function $s_k$) into a single $\mathsf{P}$-computable $P^\star$ useful against all $s_k(cn)$, define $\mathrm{HISTORY}(y)$ over the $\mathsf{NEXP}$-complete Succinct Halting problem: accept iff a prefix of $y$ of power-of-two length encodes an accepting computation history. This is the universality result: if *any* constructive useful property exists, then this one explicit $\mathsf{P}$ property is useful against all polynomial size functions. Combining 3.1 + 3.2 + 3.3 yields Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 (proof p. 16).

4. **ACC witness lower bounds by adapting the Williams [Wil11] framework (Theorem 1.3, p. 17).** Assume $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ has $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ witnesses; this implies $\mathsf{TIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ has $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits, so the ACC-SAT algorithm (Beigel-Tarui SYM-AND [BT94]) gives a nondeterministic $2^{n-n^\delta}$ algorithm that, via reduction to Succinct 3SAT and circuit composition, simulates every $L \in \mathsf{NTIME}[2^n]$ faster than the time hierarchy [Z83] allows. Contradiction.

5. **Hardness-test-plus-PRG to convert witness lower bounds into language lower bounds (Theorem 4.1, p. 18, Theorem 4.2 / Lemma 8 of [MNW99], and Theorem 1.4, p. 19-20).** The chain: from a unary language without small witnesses (Corollary 4.2, p. 18) build a hardness test for $\mathsf{ACC}^0$, guess a hard function and verify it with the test (Umans PRG, Theorem 2.1), get a PRG that fools size-$s$ circuits, and use it to derandomize CAPP / Merlin-Arthur games. Theorem 4.2 (Miltersen-Vinodchandran-Watanabe [MNW99]) lifts $\mathsf{TIME}[2^{O(n)}] \subseteq \mathsf{SIZE}[s(n)]$ to $\mathsf{TIME}[g(n)] \subseteq \mathsf{MATIME}[s(3\log g(n))^c]$. Then the contradiction is engineered against $\mathsf{TIME}[2^{n^{2\log n}}]$ via a counting/diagonalization step (enumerate $n^{\log n}$-size circuits, find the lex-first truth table without small circuits in $O(2^{n^{2\log n}})$ time). The $\mathsf{NE}/1 \cap \mathsf{coNE}/1$ version uses a single advice bit to encode whether the PRG simulation succeeds.

6. **Seed/natural-property amplification (Theorem 1.7, p. 22-23).** The $\neg(1) \Rightarrow \neg(2)$ construction: given a $\mathsf{P}$-natural ae-useful algorithm $A$, define a $\mathsf{ZPE}$ predicate $V(x,z)$ that partitions $z$ into $2^{dk+1}$ blocks and accepts if $A$ accepts some block; by largeness a random $z$ is accepted with probability $> 2/3$ but every accepted $z$ has high $\mathcal{C}$-circuit complexity (so no small seed). The reverse uses Proposition 1 (substrings inherit small circuits) to argue a random hitting set has high complexity, and "check for a hitting set" is the natural property.

7. **Hitting-sets-to-io-natural-algorithms (Theorem 5.1, p. 24).** If $\mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ lacks $n^2$-size hitting sets encoded by $n^c$-size circuits (for all $c$), then there is an io-$\mathsf{P}$-natural algorithm useful against $n^c$-size circuits. The $A(Y, a)$ uses $O(\log N)$ advice $a$ (the count of accepted inputs) and a counting check; $A'$ runs on arbitrary lengths via the interval partition $I_n = [n^2, (n+1)^2 - 1]$.

8. **Win-win via easy-witness / Kabanets (Theorem 1.8, p. 25-26).** Suppose all $\mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}]$ predicates have small ($n^{c-1}$-size) circuits encoding *easy hitting sets* for all inputs of a length: then $\mathsf{RTIME}[2^{O(n)}] \subset \mathsf{TIME}[2^{O(n)}]/n^c$, improvable to $\mathsf{SIZE}[n^c]$ via $\mathsf{EXP} \subset \mathsf{P/poly} \Rightarrow \mathsf{EXP} = \mathsf{MA}$ [BFNW93] and a complete language. Otherwise Theorem 5.1 gives a natural algorithm to randomly guess hard functions and derandomize $\mathsf{BPP}$ in subexp with $n^\varepsilon$ advice (Umans PRG). Theorem 1.9 then combines 1.8 with Impagliazzo-Wigderson [IW01] and Ko's theorem [Ko82].

9. **Swap input with randomness (Theorem 1.10, p. 28-29).** Given a randomized large-useful algorithm $A$ taking $n$ input bits and $n^{k-2}$ random bits, amplify to error $< 1/4^n$, then the deterministic $A'(T)$ partitions $T$ into substrings $T_i$ of length $n^{1/k}$ and uses the *whole* $T$ as randomness for $A(T_i, T)$. Largeness is shown by a union bound over the $2^{n^{1/k}}$ possible randomness strings; usefulness by Lemma 2.1 (a small-circuit $T$ forces small-circuit substrings $T_i$, contradiction).

## Relevance to this project
This is the *technical primary* for the natural-proofs strand of the algorithmic-method spine. It is cited as [25] in the 2015 essay note already in this directory, and it is the source for the claim, made repeatedly in the dossier, that the Williams method is non-natural by being non-constructive in the *large* sense.

Connections to the barrier discipline:
- **Constructivity is unavoidable, largeness is the escape (Theorems 1.1, 1.2).** This is the precise sense in which the algorithmic method *evades* the natural-proofs barrier: it produces a property that is constructive and useful but *not large*. The dossier's barrier scorecard for the Williams technique should cite Theorems 1.1/1.2 as the formal statement that any $\mathsf{NEXP}$ lower bound meets exactly two of the three natural-proofs axioms. The escape hatch is the deliberate failure of largeness, which is exactly what Razborov-Rudich [RR97] requires to break PRFs. The intuitive argument (p. 3) that the $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ proof of [Wil11] is non-natural because its SAT algorithm is "too slow" to yield a polytime *large* useful property is here upgraded to a theorem: constructivity is necessary, and the proofs make the useful property explicit.
- **Natural-proofs-vs-derandomization duality (Theorem 1.7).** The absence of $\mathsf{P}$-natural useful properties is *equivalent* to a strong derandomization ($\mathsf{ZPE}$ has $\mathcal{C}$ seeds). This is the rigorous form of the dossier intuition that the natural-proofs barrier is not a wall but a derandomization problem (weaker than, but implied by, strong PRGs). It directly supports the program stance that barriers are a compass, not a stop sign.
- **Williams is the existence proof barriers are passable (LEARNINGS finding 6).** The new $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ results (Theorems 1.3, 1.4) are constructed *through* this natural-proofs-aware machinery, demonstrating concrete non-natural lower bounds.

This is research direction 01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0) fused with direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion). The seeds/derandomization material (Theorems 1.7, 1.8, 1.9) is the cleanest in-corpus statement of the natural-proofs-as-derandomization reading.

## What this enables / what remains open

**Maps to research directions:**
- **01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly):** Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 are new unconditional ACC results (exponential witness-size lower bounds for $\mathsf{NEXP}$; $n^{\log n}$ language lower bounds for the semantic classes $\mathsf{NE} \cap \textit{io-}\mathsf{coNE}$ and $\mathsf{NE}/1 \cap \mathsf{coNE}/1$). The generic SAT-to-lower-bound Theorems 1.5/1.6 give the recipe for the next class: a faster SAT (or CAPP) algorithm for $\mathcal{C}$ yields lower bounds for these semantic classes. This is the front to push toward $\mathsf{TC}^0$.
- **02 (natural-proofs evasion, MCSP crux):** This is the central paper. Theorems 1.1/1.2 make constructivity unavoidable; Theorem 1.7 makes the *absence* of natural properties equivalent to derandomization; Theorem 1.10 (RP-natural implies P-natural) shows randomization does not help build natural properties. The conclusion (Section 7, p. 29) explicitly raises the equivalence with Chapman-Williams [CW15]: $\mathsf{NP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$ iff natural properties true of SAT but useful against all poly-size SAT-solving circuits, which is squarely in the MCSP family. The constructive-useful object here *is* a meta-complexity property.
- **03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP):** Touched via Klivans-van Melkebeek [KvM02] (graph nonisomorphism subexp proofs) in the AM analysis (Corollary 1.2), but not a focus.
- **04 (GCT, multiplicity obstructions):** Not addressed. The conclusion (p. 29) quotes Mulmuley's "$\mathsf{P} \ne \mathsf{NP}$ because $\mathsf{P}$ is big" only as philosophical framing for Theorems 1.1/1.2, not as a GCT connection.

**Maps to LEARNINGS findings:**
- Finding (6) (Williams is the existence proof barriers are passable): directly, via Theorems 1.3-1.4 built through natural-proofs-aware machinery.
- Finding (3) (constructivity crux is MCSP): Theorems 1.1/1.2 say constructivity is unavoidable, and the conclusion's [CW15] equivalence frames $\mathsf{NP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$ as a SAT-solving meta-complexity property. The useful-property object is meta-complexity.
- Finding (2) (natural proofs are common): footnote 4 (p. 3) and Section 2.2 note $\mathsf{coNP}$-natural useful properties against $\mathsf{P/poly}$ are trivial; largeness, not constructivity, is the rare ingredient.
- Finding (5) (average-case is not worst-case): the largeness condition is precisely the average-case (fraction of all functions) ingredient that the method drops; the first conclusion open problem (average-case hard $\mathsf{NEXP}$ witnesses for $\mathsf{ACC}^0$) is exactly this gap.
- Finding (8) (futures converge on the Williams spine): the SAT-to-lower-bound Theorems 1.5/1.6 are the spine in its general form.

**Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes (Section 7, p. 29-30, plus structural gaps):**
1. **Average-case hard $\mathsf{NEXP}$ witnesses for $\mathsf{ACC}^0$.** Are there $\mathsf{NEXP}$ predicates whose valid witnesses correspond to Boolean functions no poly-size $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuit agrees with on $1/2 + 1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ of inputs? This would give unconditional derandomized $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ simulations. Williams names the impediment: we do not believe $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ can compute MAJORITY, and computing Majority appears necessary for hardness amplification (cites Shaltiel-Viola [SV10]). This is exactly the dossier's $\mathsf{TC}^0$ hinge surfacing from the other side: the place where the polynomial/hardness-amplification machinery dies is the Majority bottleneck.
2. **Equivalences for non-uniform ($\mathsf{P/poly}$-natural) properties.** This paper studies $\mathsf{P}/O(\log n)$-decidable properties; are there equivalences between lower bounds and the more general $\mathsf{P/poly}$-natural notion?
3. **What algorithms follow from stronger lower bounds?** Tension between "$\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$" (asserts efficient recognition of some hard functions, Theorems 1.1/1.2) and "factoring is hard" (denies efficient recognition of a non-negligible fraction). The conclusion (p. 30) cites Chapman-Williams [CW15]: $\mathsf{NP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$ iff natural properties true of SAT but useful against all poly-size SAT-solving circuits.
4. **IKW problem (Section 2.2).** Does a $\mathsf{P}$-natural property useful against $\mathsf{P/poly}$ imply $\mathsf{EXP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$? By Theorem 2.2 an affirmative answer proves $\mathsf{EXP} \ne \mathsf{ZPP}$ unconditionally; this remains open.
5. **The Theorem 1.4 hierarchy gap.** The semantic classes $\mathsf{NTIME} \cap \mathsf{coNTIME}$ have no known tight time hierarchy or complete languages, which is why Theorem 1.4 needs a different proof. Closing this hierarchy gap is a structural obstacle for pushing the method.
