## Summary
The paper proves an Easy Witness Lemma for $\mathsf{NP}$ and for nondeterministic quasi-polynomial time $\mathsf{NQP} = \mathsf{NTIME}[n^{\log^{O(1)} n}]$, where the original Impagliazzo-Kabanets-Wigderson (IKW) easy witness lemma applied only to nondeterministic exponential-time classes such as $\mathsf{NEXP}$ (Lemma 1.1). Concretely (Lemma 1.2): there is a constant $c \ge 1$ such that for all $k$, if $\mathsf{NP} \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^k]$ then every $L \in \mathsf{NP}$ has witness circuits of size $n^{ck^3}$; the analogue (Lemma 1.3) holds for $\mathsf{NQP}$ with witness circuits of size $2^{c \log^{k^3} n}$. The payoff (Theorems 1.1, 1.2) is a tightened algorithm-to-lower-bound connection in which merely faster Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat algorithms (rather than $2^n$-time Circuit-SAT) imply circuit lower bounds for $\mathsf{NP}$ or $\mathsf{NQP}$, and the headline instantiation (Theorem 1.3) is that for every fixed $k$, $\mathsf{NQP}$ does not have $n^{\log^k n}$-size $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$ circuits, i.e., $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits augmented with a bottom layer of linear threshold gates. The technical engine is a new "almost" almost-everywhere circuit lower bound for Merlin-Arthur protocols with a little advice (Theorem 3.1), which substitutes for the $\mathsf{EXP} = \mathsf{MA}$ collapse that powered IKW but is unavailable for the smaller classes.

## Key definitions and results

**The motivating gap (Section 1, p. 1-2).** The IKW Easy Witness Lemma (Lemma 1.1, stated informally on p. 1) says: if $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n]$ has circuits of size $s(n)$, then there is a $k$ such that every yes-instance for every verifier for a problem in $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n]$ has a witness $w$ (of $O(2^n)$ length) encodable by a circuit of size $\le s(s(n^k)^k)^k$; viewing $w$ as a truth table $f_w$, that $f_w$ has small circuits. For polynomial or quasi-polynomial $s(n)$ this says small circuits for $\mathsf{NEXP}$ imply small witness circuits, hence (by enumerating candidate witness circuits) $\mathsf{NEXP} \subset \mathsf{P/poly}$ implies $\mathsf{NEXP} = \mathsf{EXP}$. The deficiency the paper attacks (p. 1, "The Quest for Lower-Complexity Functions"): the IKW connection only yields circuit lower bounds for functions in $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^{n^\varepsilon}]$ and higher, because its proof needs nondeterministic exponential time. An easy witness lemma for $\mathsf{NP}$ or $\mathsf{NQP}$ is the missing piece for pushing the hard class downward.

**Typical circuit classes and Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat (Section 1.1, p. 2; Preliminaries p. 6-7).** A circuit class $\mathcal{C}$ is *evaluable* (given a description of $C \in \mathcal{C}$ and a partial 0/1 assignment, the sub-circuit is determined in polynomial time), *closed under negation* (poly-time algorithm producing $\neg C$), and *closed under conjunctions* (poly-time algorithm producing a single $\mathcal{C}$-circuit equivalent to $C_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge C_n$). A class with all three properties is called *typical*. Standard classes $\mathsf{AC}^0, \mathsf{ACC}, \mathsf{TC}^0, \mathsf{NC}^1, \mathsf{NC}, \mathsf{P/poly}$ are typical. The lower bound applications are stated for the *Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat* problem (p. 2): a promise problem where the input $\mathcal{C}$-circuit on $n$ inputs is either unsatisfiable or has at least $2^n/4$ satisfying assignments. A nondeterministic algorithm solves Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat if (i) it outputs yes/no/don't-know on every path, (ii) on inputs with $\ge 2^n/4$ satisfying assignments it outputs no on some path, (iii) on unsatisfiable inputs it outputs yes on some path, and (iv) it never outputs yes on one path and no on another for the same input. This is a weaker (easier) problem than $\mathcal{C}$-SAT, believed to be in deterministic polynomial time (it follows from PRGs sufficient for $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{BPP}$).

**Easy Witnesses for NP (Lemma 1.2, p. 2).** There is a $c \ge 1$ such that for all $k \ge 1$, if $\mathsf{NP} \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^k]$ then every $L \in \mathsf{NP}$ has witness circuits of size at most $n^{ck^3}$.

**Easy Witnesses for NQP (Lemma 1.3, p. 2).** There is a $c \ge 1$ such that for all $k \ge 1$, if $\mathsf{NQP} \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[2^{\log^k n}]$ then every $L \in \mathsf{NQP}$ has witness circuits of size $2^{c \log^{k^3} n}$.

The unified statement is Lemma 4.1 (p. 12). Witness circuits are defined precisely in Definition 2.2 (p. 7): a verifier $V$ for $L \in \mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)]$ has witness circuits of size $w(n)$ if for all $x \in L$ there is a witness $y_x$ of length $O(t(n))$ such that $V(x, y_x)$ accepts and $y_x$, viewed as a truth table, has circuit complexity at most $w(n)$.

**Sharpened algorithm-to-lower-bound connections (Theorems 1.1, 1.2, p. 3).**
- Theorem 1.1: Let $\mathcal{C}$ be typical and $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$. If Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat on $n$-input, $2^{\varepsilon n}$-size circuits is solvable in nondeterministic $O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)n})$ time, then there is a $c \ge 1$ such that for all $k$, $\mathsf{NTIME}[n^{ck^4/\varepsilon}]$ does not have $n^k$-size $\mathcal{C}$-circuits.
- Theorem 1.2: Let $\mathcal{C}$ be typical and $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$. If Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat on $n$-input, $2^{n^\varepsilon}$-size circuits is in nondeterministic $O(2^{n - n^\varepsilon})$ time, then for all $k$ there is a $c \ge 1$ with $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^{\log^{ck^4/\varepsilon} n}]$ not having $2^{\log^k n}$-size $\mathcal{C}$-circuits.

The conceptual content (p. 3, paragraph after Theorem 1.2): for $2^{\varepsilon n}$ size and $n$ inputs, Circuit-SAT is already in polynomial time, and for $2^{n^\varepsilon}$ size it is in quasi-polynomial time. So these theorems ask for improvements to problems that are *already in P or QP*, possibly via nondeterminism, and the relevant Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat problem is believed to be in $\mathsf{P}$ even for $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ circuit sizes.

**The headline lower bound (Theorem 1.3, p. 3).** For every constant $k$, $d$, and $m \ge 2$, there is an $e \ge 1$ and a problem in $\mathsf{NTIME}[n^{\log^e n}]$ which does not have depth-$d$ $n^{\log^k n}$-size $\mathsf{AC}[m]$ circuits, even with $n^{\log^k n}$ linear threshold gates at the bottom layer. In the introduction (p. 2) this is summarized as: $\mathsf{NQP}$ does not have $n^{\log^k n}$-size $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$ circuits, for all fixed $k$. This is the new lower bound the focus of these notes targets: $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits with a single bottom layer of linear threshold (THR) gates, the first concrete step from $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ toward $\mathsf{TC}^0$, now obtained for the much smaller class $\mathsf{NQP}$ rather than $\mathsf{NEXP}$.

**The "almost" almost-everywhere MA lower bound (Theorem 3.1, p. 9).** There are $d_1, d_2, d_3 \ge 1$ such that for all circuit-size functions $s(n)$ and time-constructible $s_1, s_2$ satisfying (i) $s_2(n) \ge s(n)^{2 d_2}$, (ii) $s_1(n) \ge s(s_2(n))$, (iii) $s_1(n) \ge s(n)^{2 d_1 + 1}$, there is a language $L_1$ computable by a Merlin-Arthur protocol in $O(s_1(n)^2 \cdot s_2(n)^{d_3})$ time with $2 \log s_2(n)$ advice such that for all sufficiently large $n$, either the circuit complexity of $L_1^n$ is greater than $s(n)$, or the circuit complexity of $L_1^{s_2(n)}$ is greater than $s(s_2(n))$. In words: for almost every $n$, the hard MA-with-advice language has high circuit complexity on length $n$ or on the polynomially-larger length $s_2(n)$. This is "almost" almost-everywhere because the lower bound is guaranteed on at least one of every pair of polynomially-far-apart input lengths, not literally every length.

**Significant separation (Section 2, p. 8).** Following Fortnow-Santhanam [FS17], a class $\mathcal{C}$ has a *significant separation* from $\mathcal{D}$ if there is an $f \in \mathcal{C}$ such that for all $g \in \mathcal{D}$ there is a $k$ so that for every $m$, $f_n$ differs from $g_n$ on some length $n \in [m, m^k]$. Theorem 3.1 for poly-size circuits yields a significant separation of MA (with advice) from small circuits, and the authors note theirs is a bit stronger (a lower bound on every pair of polynomially-far-apart lengths, not just on some length in every polynomially-long interval).

**Supporting structural facts (Section 2).**
- Theorem 2.1 (Umans [Uma03], p. 7): a universal-constant PRG $G$ such that for $Y$ with circuit complexity $CC(Y) \ge s^g$, $G(Y, \cdot)$ fools all size-$s$ circuits to within $1/s$, computable in $\mathrm{poly}(|Y|)$ time. This is the hardness-to-randomness engine used to derandomize the Arthur part of the protocols.
- Theorem 2.2 (Santhanam [San07], p. 7-8): a PSPACE-complete language $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$ that is paddable, downward self-reducible, and "checkable" (a probabilistic poly-time checker $M$ querying only length-$|x|$ oracle queries, accepting with probability 1 when given the correct oracle and $x \in L$, rejecting with probability $\ge 2/3$ regardless of oracle when $x \notin L$). All four properties (paddable, downward self-reducible, self-correctable, checkable) are used. The proof on p. 8 explicitly verifies these properties for Santhanam's $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}} = \{1^i\} \cup \{1^i 0 x : x \in S\}$ built over the Trevisan-Vadhan [TV02] downward-self-reducible self-correctable PSPACE-complete language $S$.
- Theorem 2.3 (folklore diagonalization, p. 8): for space-constructible $s(n) < 2^n/(2n)$ there is $L_{diag} \in \mathsf{SPACE}[s(n)^2]$ with no $s(n)$-size circuits for all but finitely many $n$. The almost-everywhere hard language is built by exhaustively finding, in $O(s(n)^2)$ space, the lexicographically first truth table on $2 \log s(n)$ inputs not computed by any size-$s(n)$ circuit, then padding.

## Techniques and proof ideas

**Why IKW does not transfer directly (Section 1.2, p. 3-4).** The IKW proof of Lemma 1.1 assumes (A) $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n]$ has $n^k$-size circuits and (B) it does not have $n^{O(k^2)}$-size witness circuits, and derives a contradiction with a hard language $L_{hard} \in \mathsf{TIME}[2^{n^{k+1}}] \subseteq \mathsf{SPACE}[n^{k+1}]$ produced by direct diagonalization. Assumption (A) plus padding puts $L_{hard}$ in $\mathsf{TIME}[2^{n^{k+1}}]$ with $n^{O(k^2)}$-size circuits, hence (using $\mathsf{EXP} \subset \mathsf{P/poly} \Rightarrow \mathsf{EXP} = \mathsf{MA}$, [BFNW93], plus [MNW99]) $L_{hard}$ has a Merlin-Arthur protocol in $n^{O(k^2)}$ time. Assumption (B) then lets one derandomize Arthur by guessing-and-verifying a hard witness $y_i$ of circuit complexity $\ge n^{ck^2}$, feeding it to the Umans PRG, and simulating the protocol nondeterministically with $n$ bits of advice in $2^{O(n)}$ time, finally contradicting $L_{hard}$'s definition via (A). When $\mathsf{NTIME}[2^n]$ is replaced by $\mathsf{NP}$ or $\mathsf{NQP}$, the collapse $\mathsf{EXP} = \mathsf{MA}$ (or $\mathsf{PSPACE} = \mathsf{MA}$) is no longer available, so it is unclear what hard MA lower bound to contradict.

**The Santhanam workaround and its weakness (Section 1.2, p. 4).** Santhanam's $\mathsf{MA}/1 \not\subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^k]$ [San07] gives an explicit MA-protocol-with-1-bit-advice language $L_k$ in $n^{O(k^2)}$ time without $n^k$-size circuits, which can be derandomized under the witness-failure assumption. But Santhanam's lower bound is *not known infinitely-often*: it only rules out $n^k$-size circuits for $L_k$ on all but finitely many lengths, not on infinitely many. Running the argument yields only an "infinitely-often witness" conclusion: if $\mathsf{NP}$ has $n^k$-size circuits then $\mathsf{NP}$ has $n^{O(k^2)}$-size witness circuits for infinitely many lengths. That is too weak for the lower bound applications, which need almost-everywhere witnesses (and would otherwise need an almost-everywhere nondeterministic time hierarchy, open since it is open whether $\mathsf{NEXP} \subseteq \mathrm{io}\text{-}\mathsf{NP}$).

**The new MA lower bound construction (Section 3, p. 9-12).** The hard MA language $L_1$ combines two protocols selected by $2 \log s_2(n)$ bits of advice $\alpha_n := \min\{s_2(n), \ell(n)\}$, where $\ell(n)$ is the largest $\ell$ such that $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}^\ell$ has a size-$s_1(n)$ circuit (Eq. 1-2, p. 9-10).
- Part 1 (when $\alpha_n = s_2(n)$): Merlin guesses an $s_1(n)$-size circuit for $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$ on $s_2(n)$-bit inputs, Arthur randomly checks it (via the Santhanam checker $M$, Theorem 2.2), then uses the verified circuit as an oracle to solve $L_{diag}$ (Theorem 2.3) via the reduction $R$ of Claim 1 (an $O(s(n)^{2 d_2})$-time reduction from $L_{diag}$ to $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$ producing instances of length $s(|y|)^{2 d_2} \le s_2(n)$). Because $L_{diag}$ has no $s(n)$-size circuits a.e., this branch forces high complexity on length $n$.
- Part 2 (when $\alpha_n < s_2(n)$): Merlin guesses an $\alpha_n$-input circuit of size $s_1(n)$ and Arthur uses it to compute $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$ on a padded input. The advice ensures the guessed circuits always exist in both branches, so the protocol satisfies the MA promise on all input lengths (verified on p. 10-11), running in $O(s_1(n)^2 \cdot s_2(n)^{d_3})$ time.

The lower bound proof (p. 11-12) supposes $L_1$ has small circuits on both length $n$ and length $s_2(n)$ for infinitely many $n$ and derives a contradiction with Theorem 2.3 via three steps: Proposition 1 (for a.e. $m$, if $L_1^m$ has an $s(m)$-circuit then $\ell(m) < s_2(m)$, hence $\alpha_m < s_2(m)$, so Part 2 runs and computes padded $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$), Lemma 3.1 (for a.e. $m$, if $L_1^m$ has a size-$s(m)$ circuit then $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}^m$ has a size-$s(m)$ circuit), and an Amplification Claim inside Lemma 3.1 that bootstraps size-$s(m)$ circuits for $L_{\mathsf{PSPACE}}$ from short input lengths up to length $m$ using the downward self-reduction (each self-reduction step blows the size up by an $O(s(m)^{2 d_1 + 1})$ factor, absorbed by constraint (iii) $s_1(n) \ge s(n)^{2 d_1 + 1}$). The contradiction is that $L_{diag}$ would then have $s(n)$-size circuits infinitely often.

**From the MA lower bound to easy witnesses (Section 4, p. 12-14).** Lemma 4.1: there are $e, g \ge 1$ such that for increasing time-constructible $s(n)$, with $s_2(n) := s(e \cdot n)^e$ and any $t(n)$, if $\mathsf{NTIME}[O(t(n)^e)] \subset \mathsf{SIZE}[s(n)]$ then every $L \in \mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)]$ has witness circuits of size $s_2(s_2(s_2(n)))^{2g}$, provided (a) $s(n) < 2^{n/e}/n$ and (b) $t(n) \ge s_2(s_2(s_2(n)))^d$ for a large constant $d$. The proof instantiates Theorem 3.1 with $s'(n) := s(e n)$, $s_2'(n) := s'(n)^{d'}$, $s_1'(n) := s'(s_2'(n))$ for a suitable $d'$ with $\max(2 d_2, 2 d_1 + 1, d_3 + 2) \le d' \le e$, so the hard MA language $L_1$ exists with $2 d' \cdot n$ advice. The "almost" almost-everywhere structure is then derandomized: if witness circuits of size $w(n) = s_2(s_2(s_2(n)))^{2g}$ failed (Eq. 5, p. 13), the bad inputs $x_i$ would give, by guess-and-verify, truth tables $y_i$ of circuit complexity $\ge w(|x_i|)$ that power the Umans PRG (Theorem 2.1) and fool size-$s_2(s_2(s_2(n)))^2$ circuits, letting a nondeterministic $O(t(n)^e)$-time machine $N$ with $(2 d' + 1) n$ advice simulate $L_1$ on the relevant input lengths $n_i$ and $s_2'(n_i)$ simultaneously (p. 13-14). The single hard advice string $x_{hard}$ works for both lengths because the simulating circuit on the longer length also has size bounded by $s_2(s_2(s_2(n_i)))^2$. This contradicts Theorem 3.1's lower bound when $e \ge 2 d' + 2$.

**The PCP / EVAL-GATE application argument (Section 5, p. 14-17).** Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 are proved by the now-standard scheme [Wil10, Wil11, SW13] but using the new NP/NQP easy witness lemma in place of the NEXP one, so the hard language can sit in $\mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)]$ rather than $\mathsf{NEXP}$. Assume (A) $\mathsf{NTIME}[n^{ck^4/\varepsilon}]$ has $n^k$ $\mathcal{C}$-circuits and (B) a fast Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat algorithm. The Easy Witness Lemma for NP (Lemma 1.2) gives $\mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)]$ witness circuits of $n^{O(k^3)}$ size for every NP verifier. Take $L \in \mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)] \setminus \mathsf{NTIME}[t(n)^{1 - \varepsilon/2}]$ (nondeterministic time hierarchy [SFM78, Zak83]) on unary inputs. The Ben-Sasson-Viola short-PCP verifier [BSV14] turns the $O(t(n))$-time verifier $V$ into a quasi-linear-size oracle circuit $C_x^O$ ($\ell = \log_2(T(n)) + O(\log \log T(n))$ inputs, $\mathrm{poly}(n, \log T(n))$ size) that is unsatisfiable when $x \in L$ and has $\ge 2^\ell (1 - 1/n)$ satisfying assignments otherwise (p. 15). A nondeterministic algorithm $N$ guesses the witness circuit $W_n$ encoding the PCP oracle $O$, plugs it into $C_x^O$, and would naively need exhaustive search on the resulting circuit. The crux (the EVAL-GATE trick, p. 15-16) is that $\mathcal{C}$ may be weaker than unrestricted circuits, so one cannot just plug $W_n$ in and feed it to the Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat algorithm. Instead: EVAL-GATE (given $C^O$, an oracle circuit $O$, an input $x$, and a gate index $i$, output the $i$-th gate value) is in $\mathsf{P}$, so by (A) it has $\mathcal{C}$-circuits $E$ of size $n^{O(k)}$. Using $E$ and the gate-consistency 3-CNF $F_i$, build a $\mathcal{C}$-circuit $D(x) := \neg(\bigwedge_i F_i(E(\cdots), E(\cdots), E(\cdots)))$ that is 0 exactly when $E$ is consistent on all gates (Eq. 6, p. 16). Because $\mathcal{C}$ is closed under conjunctions and projections, $D$ is a $\mathcal{C}$-circuit of size $n^{O(k^4)}$, and the assumed Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat algorithm runs on $D$ in nondeterministic $O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)\ell})$ time. This lets $N'$ decide $L$ in $t(n)^{1-\varepsilon} \cdot \mathrm{poly}(\log t(n))$ time, contradicting the time hierarchy. Theorem 1.2 (p. 17) uses the identical strategy with quasi-polynomial parameters.

**Reaching $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$ (Theorem 1.3 via Theorem 5.1, p. 17).** To instantiate Theorem 1.2 for the class $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$, the only remaining ingredient is a fast satisfiability algorithm. Theorem 5.1 (Williams [Wil14]): for all $d$ and $m \ge 2$, there is an $\varepsilon > 0$ and an $O(2^{n - n^\varepsilon})$-time deterministic algorithm for SAT on depth-$d$ size-$2^{n^\varepsilon}$ circuits over unbounded fan-in AND, OR, $\mathrm{MOD}_m$ with linear threshold gates at the bottom layer. Plugging this Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat-strength algorithm into Theorem 1.2 yields the $\mathsf{NQP} \not\subset \mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$ lower bound.

## Relevance to this project

This source is a central node on the Williams algorithm-to-lower-bound spine, which the 2050 backward-induction dossier identified as the *leading path* of the program. It does two project-critical things.

First, it pushes the hard class downward from $\mathsf{NEXP}$ to $\mathsf{NQP}$ (and supplies the NP-level easy witness lemma, Lemma 1.2, that makes Theorem 1.1's NP-level conclusions conditionally possible). The dossier's LEARNINGS finding (6), that Williams is the existence proof that the three barriers are passable, is reinforced here: this paper makes the passage *more efficient* by lowering the time bound on the class for which the algorithm-to-lower-bound conversion produces a lower bound. The conclusion (Section 6, p. 18) explicitly frames this as bringing "the age-old problem of proving that NP does not have linear-size circuits ... in striking distance," which is the directional reading the project's stance prescribes.

Second, and most directly relevant to the dossier's *hinge*, this paper takes the first concrete step from $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ toward $\mathsf{TC}^0$ by proving a lower bound against $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$: $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ circuits with a single bottom layer of linear threshold gates (Theorem 1.3, p. 3). The dossier's hinge is the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step, where the polynomial method dies because MAJORITY has approximate degree $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$. A full bottom-to-top layer of THR gates is exactly $\mathsf{TC}^0$; one bottom layer of THR under $\mathsf{ACC}$ is the first inch into that regime, and this paper shows the algorithmic method can take that inch (riding the [Wil14] $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$-SAT algorithm). The conclusion (Section 6, p. 18, "NP versus ACC?") names the obstruction to going further: the lack of a Gap-$\mathsf{ACC}$-Unsat algorithm running in $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$ time on $2^{\varepsilon n}$-size circuits, because the ACC-SAT algorithm depends on the [Yao90, BT94] $\mathsf{ACC} \to \mathsf{SYM} \circ \mathsf{AND}$ transformation, which blows the circuit up by a quasi-polynomial factor and so cannot yield a fast enough Gap-$\mathsf{ACC}$-Unsat algorithm. This is a precise, named coordinate for where the next push must happen.

On the barrier discipline: the entire construction is non-relativizing and non-algebrizing for the same structural reason as the rest of the Williams program. The PCP / EVAL-GATE step (Section 5, p. 15-16) cracks open the gate-level representation of circuits ($\mathcal{C}$-circuits computing EVAL-GATE, gate-consistency 3-CNFs), and the SAT algorithms exploit the explicit circuit representation rather than oracle access, which is exactly the non-black-box behavior the dossier flags as the source of barrier evasion. The natural-proofs barrier is also evaded in the usual Williams way: the argument is a single explicit diagonalizing language ($L_{diag}$, Theorem 2.3) propagated through one MA protocol, not a large constructive property, so it does not satisfy the largeness condition of Razborov-Rudich. I did not run the barrier checker on this technique; doing so (encoding the algorithm-to-lower-bound conversion plus the EVAL-GATE/PCP step as a `ProofTechnique`) would be a useful follow-up to confirm the expected profile (non-relativizing, non-natural, non-algebrizing).

## What this enables / what remains open

**Maps to research directions.**
- Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds, Williams ACC0 toward P/poly): this is the direct successor to Williams 2011. It (a) lowers the hard class to $\mathsf{NQP}$ and (b) climbs from $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ to $\mathsf{ACC} \circ \mathsf{THR}$. It is the literal "first step from $\mathsf{ACC}^0$ toward $\mathsf{TC}^0$" the focus asked for.
- Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion / the MCSP crux): the easy witness lemma is a meta-complexity statement (small circuits for a class imply compressible witnesses, i.e., witnesses with small minimum circuit size). The witness-circuit notion is an MCSP-adjacent object, so this paper is a data point for the dossier's LEARNINGS finding (3) that the constructivity crux is MCSP and finding (9) on fast-computable invariants.
- Direction 03 (proof complexity, NP vs coNP): tangential here; the Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat formulation is about (un)satisfiability of circuits, not propositional proof systems, but the appearance of unsatisfiability-distinguishing algorithms is a bridge worth noting.
- Direction 04 (GCT): not addressed.

**Maps to LEARNINGS findings.** This source most strongly touches findings (6) Williams is the existence proof barriers are passable (made more efficient here), (8) futures converge on the Williams spine (this paper is squarely on that spine), and (1) barriers compose (the construction must dodge all three at once, and the EVAL-GATE step is where non-relativization/non-algebrization is forced). Finding (9), the missing object as a non-algebrizing fast-computable invariant, is illuminated: the invariant that the fast algorithm computes here is Gap-$\mathcal{C}$-Unsat (distinguishing unsatisfiable circuits from those with many satisfying assignments), and the open question of finding such an invariant for $\mathsf{ACC}$ that runs in $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$ time is precisely the named obstruction (Section 6).

**Concrete open questions / cruxes this source exposes.**
1. The $\mathsf{ACC} \to \mathsf{TC}^0$ frontier (the dossier hinge): can one get a Gap-$\mathsf{ACC}$-Unsat algorithm running in $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$ time on $2^{\varepsilon n}$-size circuits, evading the quasi-polynomial blowup of the [Yao90, BT94] $\mathsf{ACC} \to \mathsf{SYM} \circ \mathsf{AND}$ transformation (Section 6, p. 18)? The authors suggest a better SAT algorithm might even follow from the assumption $\mathsf{NP} \subset \mathsf{ACC}$.
2. Can the framework reach $\mathsf{NP} \not\subset \mathsf{ACC}$ (rather than $\mathsf{NQP}$), and ultimately $\mathsf{NP} \not\subset \mathsf{SIZE}[O(n)]$, which the authors note is still open and must be proved first before the linear-size $\mathsf{P/poly}$ question (Section 6, p. 18, plus footnote 2 on p. 2: $\mathsf{NP} \not\subset \mathsf{SIZE}[n^k]$ would also give $\mathsf{NEXP} \not\subset \mathsf{P/poly}$ and nontrivial derandomizations of MA).
3. Can the connection be extended to deterministic classes? The authors flag (footnote 1, p. 2) that easy witness lemmas for deterministic classes exist, but the algorithm-to-lower-bound *connection* is not known to extend to non-uniform lower bounds for P or EXP, because the non-uniform circuit must be nondeterministically guessed.
4. Non-trivial learning algorithms for circuit classes would give BPEXP lower bounds [FK09, OS17]; can SAT algorithms expedite such learning?
