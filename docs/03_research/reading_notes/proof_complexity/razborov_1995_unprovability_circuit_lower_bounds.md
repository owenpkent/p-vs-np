## Summary

The paper turns the Razborov-Rudich natural-proofs intuition into a formal unprovability theorem about fragments of Bounded Arithmetic. Its headline (Corollary 6.5) is that if a strong pseudorandom generator exists (one secure against circuits of size $2^{n^\epsilon}$), then the theory $S_2^2(\alpha)$ cannot refute the statement that "$\alpha$ encodes a circuit of size $t(n)$ for SATISFIABILITY" for any superpolynomial $t(n)$. Equivalently, $S_2^2(\alpha) \not\vdash LB(t, S, \alpha)$: the natural arithmetic formalization of a circuit lower bound is not a theorem of $S_2^2$. A companion result (Theorem 6.1) shows the weaker base theory $\mathcal{S}(S_2)$ cannot prove superpolynomial lower bounds for depth-3 circuits with PARITY gates, with no unproven assumption at all, while it provably can prove exponential lower bounds for plain constant-depth circuits (Theorem 5.1). All of these are derived as corollaries of interpolation-like (feasible-interpolation-style) theorems for "split versions" of classical bounded-arithmetic theories introduced in the paper.

## Key definitions and results

**The framework: arithmetizing a circuit lower bound (Section 5, page 13).** Following Razborov's earlier "Bounded Arithmetic and lower bounds in Boolean complexity" [19], one introduces a predicate variable $\gamma$ encoding the wiring of a circuit, with $Circuit(t, N, \gamma)$ a $\Sigma^b_1(\gamma)$-formula asserting $\gamma$ is a valid size-$t$ circuit on $|N|$ variables, and $Output(t, N, x, \gamma)$ representing its output on input $x$. Here $N \approx 2^n$ is the truth-table length, so the bit size of the relevant objects is $N$, not $n$. The target formula (equation (5), page 13) is

$$LB(t, S, \gamma) \;\rightleftharpoons\; Circuit(t(N), N, \gamma) \supset \exists x \in \{0,1\}^{|N|}\,(Output(t(N), N, x, \gamma) \not\equiv S(N, x)),$$

which says: no size-$t(N)$ circuit computes the explicit function $S$. Crucially (first remark, page 2, and page 13), the encoding $\gamma$ is allowed to carry the *truth-tables of all intermediate functions in the circuit*, the strongest possible framework, exactly as in [19]. And (second remark, page 2) the theory need only believe $t(n) \geq n^{\omega(1)}$ on the integers, not prove it, so $t(n) = n^{\log^* n}$ is admissible.

**Split theories (Section 1, Figure 1 on page 3; Section 5, page 13).** The split version $\mathcal{S}(S_2)$ works in a language $L_2(\alpha, \beta)$ with two predicate variables, viewing $(\alpha, \beta)$ as a circuit with a PARITY gate at the top: $\alpha$ encodes the left half, $\beta$ the right half, and the function computed is $\alpha \oplus \beta$ (Figure 1). $\mathcal{S}(S_2)$ allows induction on arbitrary bounded formulae in $L_2(\alpha)$ and arbitrary bounded formulae in $L_2(\beta)$, but the base of the induction hierarchy $\mathcal{S}\Sigma^b_0 = \mathcal{S}\Pi^b_0$ mixes both (page 13). The split lower-bound statement $SLB(t, S, \alpha, \beta)$ asserts that the PARITY-combination of two size-$t(N)$ circuits $\alpha, \beta$ disagrees somewhere with $S$. The first-order results about $S_2^1(\alpha)$ and $S_2^2(\alpha)$ are then *derived* from the split results (Corollaries 6.3, 6.5) by substituting a $\Delta^b_1(\alpha,\beta)$-abstract $PARITY(t(N), N, \alpha, \beta)$ for $\alpha$.

**Theorem 3.1 (new characterization of circuit size, page 8).** For every partial Boolean function $f$, $size(R_f) = \Theta(S(f))$, and for monotone $f$, $size(R^{mon}_f) = \Theta(S_{mon}(f))$. Here $R_f$ is the Karchmer-Wigderson relation and $size(R)$ is the minimal "size" of a polynomial-local-search (PLS) problem $L$ to which $R$ reduces. The size of a local search problem $L$ is defined (page 8) as $\left|\bigcup_{u,v} F_L(u,v)\right| \cdot 2^{2C(F_L, c_L) + C(N_L)}$, where the $C$'s are communication complexities of the predicate / cost / neighborhood functions. This is the bridge that lets circuit *size* (not just depth, as in the standard Karchmer-Wigderson game) be captured by a communication-flavored, PLS-flavored object that arithmetic can reason about.

**Background on bounded arithmetic (Section 4).** Strongly regular theories (Definition 4.1, page 11): $R \supseteq BASIC_2$, axiomatizable by $\Sigma^b_0$-formulae, with every term boundable by an $L_2$-term. The key reusable facts are: the witnessing theorem relativizes to strongly regular $R$ (Proposition 4.2, page 11, gives a poly-time oracle machine $M$ witnessing $\Sigma^b_1(L)$-consequences of $S^1_R$); $S^2_R$ is $\Sigma^b_2(L)$-conservative over $T^1_R$ (Proposition 4.3, page 12, from Buss [7]); and $T^1_R$-provable $\Sigma^b_1$ statements are witnessed by oracle PLS problems (Proposition 4.4, page 12, the relativized Buss-Krajicek characterization [8]).

**The base-theory results (Section 5 and Section 6).**
- Theorem 5.1 (page 14): for every fixed $d \geq 2$, $\mathcal{S}(S_2) \vdash SLB_d(t, S, \alpha, \beta)$ with $t(N) = \lfloor 2^{\frac{1}{30}|N|^{1/(2d-3)}}\rfloor$ and $S$ = PARITY. So the split base theory *does* prove exponential lower bounds for plain depth-$d$ circuits, by formalizing the Hastad Switching Lemma (available in $S_2(\alpha)$ per [19, Appendix E.4]).
- Theorem 6.1 (page 14): $\mathcal{S}(S_2) \not\vdash SLB^{\oplus}_3(t, S, \alpha, \beta)$ for any superpolynomial $t$. The base theory *cannot* prove superpolynomial lower bounds for depth-3 circuits *with PARITY gates*. This is unconditional (no crypto assumption) and is presented as formal evidence (page 14) for the Razborov-Rudich remark that the Razborov / Smolensky $AC^0[p]$ lower bounds [34, 22, 4] required arguments stronger than the switching-lemma-based $AC^0$ bounds [11, 27, 12].

**The conditional main results (Section 6).**
- Theorem 6.2 (page 15): if there is a poly-time generator $G_k$ with *depth-hardness* $DH(G_k) \geq 2^{k^{\Omega(1)}}$, then $\mathcal{S}(S_2) + \mathcal{S}\Sigma^b_1\text{-}PIND \not\vdash SLB(t, S, \alpha, \beta)$. Corollary 6.3: under the same assumption, $S_2^1(\alpha) \not\vdash LB(t, S, \alpha)$.
- Theorem 6.4 (page 15, the main result): if there is a poly-time generator $G_k$ with (ordinary, size) hardness $H(G_k) \geq 2^{k^{\Omega(1)}}$, then $\mathcal{S}(S_2) + \mathcal{S}\Sigma^b_2\text{-}PIND \not\vdash SLB(t, S, \alpha, \beta)$. Corollary 6.5: under the same assumption, $S_2^2(\alpha) \not\vdash LB(t, S, \alpha)$. This is the abstract's headline: "SAT has a small circuit" is not refutable in $S_2^2(\alpha)$.

**Second-order interpolation theorems (Section 7).** Theorems 7.5, 7.6, 7.7 generalize the section-6 results to split versions $\mathcal{S}(V_2)$ of the second-order theory $V_2$, augmented with $\mathcal{S}\Sigma^{w1,b}_1\text{-}PIND$, $\mathcal{S}\Sigma^{w1,b}_1\text{-}IND$, and $\mathcal{S}\Sigma^{w1,b}_2\text{-}PIND$ respectively. The cleanest statement is the *separation theorem* extracted in the conclusion (equation (11), page 25): whenever $S_2^2 \vdash (A(N,\alpha) \wedge B(N,\beta)) \supset \exists x\,(C(N,x,\alpha) \not\equiv D(N,x,\beta))$, the two NP-style sets $\{\{x\}C(N,x,\alpha) \mid A(N,\alpha)\}$ and $\{\{x\}D(N,x,\beta) \mid B(N,\beta)\}$ can be *separated by a circuit of size $2^{(\log N)^{O(1)}}$* (quasipolynomial). Informally: two NP-sets that are provably disjoint in $S_2^2$ can be separated by a quasipolynomial-time set.

## Techniques and proof ideas

**The skeleton is feasible interpolation made arithmetic.** The mechanism (Section 6, pages 15 to 18, and Section 7) is: a proof of the lower bound $SLB$ in a split theory is, by Herbrand's theorem / the witnessing theorem for the skolemized theory $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}(S_2)$, witnessed by terms $s_1, \ldots, s_r$ that depend on $\alpha$ and $\beta$ only through the values each "player" can compute alone. Lemma 6.8 (page 16) is the load-bearing observation: any term of $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}(L_2)$ can be evaluated by a *bounded-round, polynomial-communication protocol* between a player holding $\alpha$'s data and a player holding $\beta$'s data. This converts an arithmetic proof into a communication protocol, which by the Karchmer-Wigderson machinery (Proposition 2.1) and the size characterization (Theorem 3.1) yields a small circuit / a natural property.

**The contradiction is engineered with Proposition 2.4 and 2.6 (the natural-proofs propositions).** Section 2.4 restates natural proofs in the constructivity / largeness / usefulness form (page 6), and Propositions 2.4 and 2.5 (page 7) say a $SIZE(2^{(\log N)^{O(1)}})$-natural (resp. $DEPTH$-natural) property useful against $P/poly$ forces every poly-time generator to have hardness (resp. depth-hardness) at most $2^{k^{o(1)}}$. Proposition 2.6 (page 7) is unconditional: there is *no* $DEPTH,SIZE(O(1), 2^{(\log N)^{O(1)}})$-natural property useful against $AC^{0,3}[2]$ (depth-3 circuits with PARITY). The proofs of Theorems 6.1, 6.2, 6.4 all end (pages 17 to 18) by extracting, from the assumed arithmetic proof, exactly such a quasipolynomial-size or quasipoly-depth natural property, which then contradicts either Proposition 2.6 (for the unconditional Theorem 6.1) or the crypto assumption via Proposition 2.4 / 2.5 (for the conditional Theorems 6.2, 6.4).

**The depth-3-with-PARITY case (Theorem 6.1, pages 16 to 17) is the prototype.** Herbrand witnesses give a protocol producing a disagreement string $x$ from two circuits; the players reconstruct circuits for arbitrary functions $f_1, f_2$ with $S^{\oplus}_3 \leq t(N)$, run the protocol, and find a coordinate where $f_1 \neq f_2 \oplus s_n$. By Proposition 2.2 this yields a set $E_n$ of quasipolynomial $DEPTH,SIZE$ complexity separating $\mathcal{F}_n^{-1}(1)$ from $\mathcal{F}_n^{-1}(0)$. A win-win on whether $|E_n| \geq \frac{1}{2}|F_n|$ produces a $DEPTH,SIZE(O(1), 2^{(\log N)^{O(1)}})$-natural property useful against $AC^{0,3}[2]$, contradicting Proposition 2.6.

**The second-order construction (Section 7, pages 19 to 24) overcomes a non-constructive step.** The proof of Theorem 3.1 (the "is this rectangle empty?" decision on page 9) is highly non-constructive, which blocks a purely first-order syntactic analogue. The remedy is the split second-order theory $\mathcal{S}(V_2)$ and a $Cl_2$-SIM "simulation" principle (Definition 7.1, page 19) stating that isomorphic internal computations by the two parties yield the same result. The interpolation Theorems 7.5 to 7.7 are then proved by induction on term structure (the base case's four-way case analysis on page 22, $E(a, \gamma)$, is "exactly the place where we use the power of our base theory not available in the first order setting").

## Relevance to this project

This paper is the canonical formalization of LEARNINGS finding (2) "natural proofs are common" and finding (3) "the constructivity crux is MCSP," recast as a *logical* statement: the reason weak theories cannot prove circuit lower bounds is that any such proof would be a natural proof, and natural proofs break PRGs. It instantiates the dossier A6 independence path directly: it is the theorem that says "circuit lower bounds are independent of $S_2^2$ under cryptographic assumptions." It also exposes the strand's central caveat in the sharpest possible form: the result is conditional on a *strong* PRG (hardness $2^{n^\epsilon}$ against circuits), exactly the cryptographic-strength assumption that the project's barrier discipline flags. The unconditional Theorem 6.1 (no PRG needed, but only depth-3-with-PARITY) marks the precise frontier where the assumption becomes necessary.

Barrier scorecard for the technique "prove a superpolynomial circuit lower bound for SAT inside $S_2^2$ (or a comparable feasible fragment)." This is the technique the paper *rules out*, so the scorecard is read as: any such proof is disqualified, and the paper tells us which barrier fires.
- **Natural proofs: HIT (this is the whole point).** The construction of Section 6 shows a $S_2^2$-proof of $LB$ yields a $SIZE(2^{(\log N)^{O(1)}})$-natural combinatorial property useful against $P/poly$ (constructive: quasipolynomial-size decision; large: the win-win guarantees density). Under a strong PRG this is impossible (Proposition 2.4). So feasible-fragment lower-bound proofs are natural in the Razborov-Rudich sense. This matches the project barriers checker treating largeness + constructivity as the natural-proofs hit.
- **Relativization: HIT (by inheritance).** The witnessing and conservation machinery (Propositions 4.2, 4.3, 4.4) is explicitly relativizing: it holds for arbitrary strongly regular theories with oracle predicates. The lower-bound argument the proof extracts is a communication / black-box-circuit argument, the same kind that relativizes. The paper does not claim to escape relativization; it produces an object (a natural property) that lives at the relativizing/algebrizing level. This is consistent with LEARNINGS finding (10), the "bounded-interface reconstruction" fourth barrier: the players reconstruct circuits from a bounded communication interface (Lemma 6.8), and that bounded interface is exactly what makes the extracted argument too generic to evade the known barriers.
- **Algebrization: HIT (by inheritance).** Nothing in the extracted argument is non-algebrizing; it is communication-complexity and natural-property machinery, both of which algebrize. The paper predates Aaronson-Wigderson 2008 and does not address algebrization directly, so this entry is the surveyor's inference, not the paper's claim.

Net: the paper is itself an application of the barrier discipline, formalized. It does not propose a P-vs-NP technique that evades the three barriers; it proves that a large class of *would-be* techniques (feasible-fragment provability) is exactly the class the natural-proofs barrier kills.

## What this enables / what remains open

Mapping to the research directions and the LEARNINGS findings.

- **Direction 03 (proof complexity / NP vs coNP).** This is the strand's foundational independence result. It says NP-vs-coNP-flavored lower bounds (circuit lower bounds for SAT) are not theorems of $S_2^2$ under crypto assumptions, which is the proof-complexity reading of "the proof must use techniques outside feasible arithmetic." Builders should treat $S_2^2$ as a *floor*: any candidate lower-bound argument that can be carried out in $S_2^2$ is, by this theorem, either false or a natural proof. The conclusion's separation theorem (equation (11)) and the open question of whether the same holds for $T^2_R$, $S_2(\alpha,\beta)$, $U^1_2$, or $V^1_2$ (page 25, "seems to be open even under any reasonable complexity assumption") are concrete proof-complexity targets.
- **Direction 02 (natural-proofs evasion / the MCSP crux).** The paper is the logical mirror of the MCSP story: "feasibly provable lower bound" plays the role of "constructive property," and the extraction of Section 6 is the bridge. It sharpens LEARNINGS finding (3): if a future MCSP-based or meta-complexity argument is to separate P from NP, it must produce a lower bound that is *not* provable in $S_2^2$, i.e. it must be non-constructive in the precise arithmetic sense this paper isolates.
- **Direction 01 (circuit lower bounds toward P/poly).** Theorem 5.1 versus Theorem 6.1 is a precise map of which circuit lower bounds are feasibly provable: switching-lemma $AC^0$ bounds yes, $AC^0[2]$ / depth-3-with-PARITY bounds no (in the base theory). This is a logical refinement of LEARNINGS finding (1) "barriers compose": the same depth-3 step that is hard for the polynomial method is also unprovable feasibly. It is a logical cousin of the dossier hinge (the $\mathsf{TC}^0$ step), suggesting the question "is $NC^1 \not\subseteq TC^0$, or the relevant $TC^0$ lower bound, provable in $S_2^2$?" as a follow-up.
- **The crypto-strength caveat (the key open tension).** The entire conditional family rests on a generator with hardness $2^{n^\epsilon}$. This is the project's recurring worry: results that need cryptographic hardness assume something close to what we are trying to prove. The honest reading is that this paper *converts* the P-vs-NP difficulty into the difficulty of proving lower bounds without crypto, rather than dissolving it.

Concrete open questions this source exposes (several stated by Razborov in Sections 8 and 9):
1. Can $S_2^2(\alpha)$ in the separation theorem (equation (11)) be strengthened to $T^2_R$, $S_2(\alpha,\beta)$, $U^1_2$, or $V^1_2$? Open even under reasonable complexity assumptions (page 25). This is the direct path toward the goal of understanding the strength of $V^1_1$ (Section 1, page 2).
2. Is the *monotone* separation theorem true for $T^2_R$? (Page 26.) Example 1 shows it fails for $T^3_R$ via the weak pigeonhole principle $WPHP(f_\beta \circ f_\alpha)$ and the Alon-Boppana clique/co-clique monotone separation, reversing to give the known result $S_2^2(f) \not\vdash WPHP(f)$ [9].
3. Does $T^2_2(f) \vdash WPHP(f)$? Does $T^3_R(f) \vdash WPHP(f_\beta \circ f_\alpha)$? (Page 26, "contrary to the common belief, it is open.")
4. Is $S_2(\alpha)$ $\Sigma^b_1(\alpha)$-conservative over $T^2_2(\alpha)$? Razborov calls obtaining a decent characterization of $\Sigma^b_1$-theorems of $T^2_2$ "the most immediate accessible question" (page 26).
5. Can the cryptographic assumption be weakened or removed for the size case (Theorem 6.4 / Corollary 6.5), as it is for the depth-3-PARITY case (Theorem 6.1)? The paper leaves $S_2^2$ requiring a strong PRG; whether a weaker hypothesis suffices is the crux of whether the A6 strand can stand on its own.
